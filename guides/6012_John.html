The science
How red light therapy helps

General Vitality & Longevity

Red and near-infrared light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, the cellular powerplants responsible for ATP production. Enhanced mitochondrial efficiency means more sustained energy for everything your body does — from your morning workouts to daily mental clarity. At 64, supporting mitochondrial function is foundational because declining cellular energy is a primary driver of age-related weakness, fatigue, and cognitive decline. Optimised ATP production means your muscles recover faster from resistance training, your brain stays sharp, and your organs function at their best. This translates to genuine longevity: cells with better energy production repair themselves more effectively and resist age-related degeneration.

Fat Loss — Stubborn Belly Fat

Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, releasing stored triglycerides into your bloodstream where your body can access and burn them as fuel. This is particularly valuable for stubborn abdominal fat that resists diet and exercise alone. Applied before your training sessions, it mobilises fat from those specific areas, making it available for oxidation during your workouts. Combined with your existing fasting protocol — which already optimises fat burning when insulin is low — red light amplifies fat mobilisation. Research shows 444% more fat loss from treated areas when combined with exercise compared to exercise alone, meaning your SOLO 2.0 directly enhances what your gym work and nutrition are already accomplishing.

Immunity & Disease Prevention

Red and NIR light enhances mitochondrial function in immune cells like macrophages and lymphocytes, giving them more ATP to detect and eliminate pathogens and cancer-precursor cells effectively. By reducing systemic inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha) that drive chronic disease and cancer risk, red light therapy creates a biochemical environment hostile to malignant cell development. This is particularly important given your concern about sugar consumption — chronic inflammation from dietary excess is a primary cancer risk factor. Regular sessions directly counteract this by systematically reducing inflammatory load whilst supporting your body's natural cancer surveillance mechanisms. Combined with your overall health discipline, this creates powerful preventive medicine at the cellular level.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your vitality and energy goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm — ideal before your training. For fat loss, use the panel 10–15 minutes before your workout sessions when your body is primed to oxidise mobilised fat. For immunity support, evening or morning sessions work equally well as this benefit is cumulative. Given your fasting protocol, morning sessions when your insulin is low will maximise the fat-mobilisation effect. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to, but morning generally offers the most synergy with your training and fasting schedule.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
