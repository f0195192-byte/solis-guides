The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Red and NIR light penetrate to reach neurons in brain regions responsible for mood regulation. These wavelengths enhance mitochondrial function in these neurons, boosting ATP production and supporting optimal neurotransmitter synthesis, including serotonin and dopamine. Enhanced cerebral blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients to these critical regions, whilst reduced neuroinflammation helps restore balanced mood pathways. This cellular energy boost directly improves stress resilience and emotional regulation.

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

SAD occurs when reduced winter sunlight disrupts circadian rhythms and suppresses serotonin and melatonin production. Red light therapy works by stimulating your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst supporting the brain regions that synthesise serotonin. Unlike blue light which suppresses melatonin, red wavelengths signal "sunset" to your circadian system, helping restore normal rhythms even during limited daylight. Enhanced mitochondrial function in mood-regulating neurons further supports the serotonin boost you need to manage winter mood changes.

Brain Fog & Mental Clarity

Brain fog reflects mitochondrial inefficiency in neurons—your brain is energy-hungry and fog occurs when ATP production can't keep pace with cognitive demands. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to reach neurons directly, activating cytochrome c oxidase and dramatically boosting ATP production in brain cells. Enhanced energy means your brain's executive function improves, memory recall sharpens, and mental processing accelerates. Improved cerebral blood flow simultaneously delivers more oxygen and removes metabolic waste that accumulates during cognitive fatigue.

Energy & Motivation

Chronic fatigue is often mitochondrial dysfunction—your cellular powerplants aren't producing sufficient ATP. Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in the mitochondrial energy production pathway, dramatically increasing ATP output. This cellular energy boost translates directly to noticeable improvements in daily stamina, reduced afternoon crashes, and restored motivation. Reduced oxidative stress simultaneously protects mitochondrial function from further damage, supporting sustained energy production.

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle and restoring radiance.

Acne & Breakouts

Acne involves bacteria, overactive oil glands, and deep inflammation. Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where it downregulates excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses, effectively calming angry, painful spots from within. Unlike harsh topicals that strip skin or antibiotics that disrupt your microbiome, red light addresses inflammation without damage, resulting in faster healing and fewer breakouts.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Uneven pigmentation stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to your skin's surface. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin, dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With better circulation, your cells have the fuel they need to regenerate faster. Old, pigmented cells from sun damage get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, clear cells, resulting in a more even, radiant complexion.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen provides your skin's structural support—without it, skin loses firmness and develops wrinkles. Red light directly stimulates fibroblasts to increase collagen production whilst enhancing elastin synthesis. The wavelengths penetrate deep into skin layers where this essential structural protein is manufactured, whilst improved blood flow delivers the amino acids and nutrients fibroblasts need to build new collagen efficiently. This restores the skin's natural elasticity and firmness.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For mood, energy, and SAD support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm—this is particularly important for you given your focus on mood and energy. For skin rejuvenation goals, any consistent time works, though many prefer evening sessions so they can apply skincare immediately after. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to, but morning sessions will give you the most benefit for your mood and energy goals.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
