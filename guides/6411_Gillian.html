The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red wavelengths (630nm and 660nm) penetrate your skin's deeper layer—the dermis—where they activate fibroblasts, the cells that produce collagen and elastin. By supercharging these cells with ATP (cellular energy), red light therapy essentially gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged collagen fibres and synthesise fresh ones more efficiently. The result is measurably reduced wrinkle depth and improved skin firmness. Clinical studies show 90% of subjects experienced reduced fine lines after just 8 sessions, with wrinkle reduction continuing to improve over weeks of consistent treatment.

Skin Texture, Glow & Radiance

Red light therapy improves microcirculation—the tiny blood vessels feeding your skin's surface—dramatically increasing oxygen and nutrient delivery. This accelerates your skin's natural cell turnover cycle, allowing old, dull cells to shed faster and revealing fresh, luminous cells underneath. The 630nm wavelength specifically reduces inflammatory cytokines that contribute to redness and blotchiness, resulting in a visibly more even, radiant complexion. Enhanced blood flow also improves nutrient absorption in your skin, supporting the synthesis of proteins and antioxidants that maintain healthy, glowing skin.

Collagen & Skin Elasticity

Skin laxity occurs when collagen breakdown exceeds collagen production. Red and near-infrared light work synergistically: red light stimulates fibroblasts directly whilst NIR light penetrates even deeper to support collagen synthesis throughout multiple skin layers. Enhanced mitochondrial function means more ATP available for the energy-intensive process of collagen production and cross-linking—the process that gives skin its structural integrity and elasticity. This is particularly important for the neck, arms, and upper legs where collagen loss is often most visible. Results build over time as new collagen accumulates, creating measurable improvement in skin firmness and elasticity.

Fat Loss & Metabolism Support

Red and near-infrared light absorption by mitochondria in fat cells increases ATP production, creating temporary pores in fat cell membranes and releasing stored triglycerides into your bloodstream where they become available fuel. Combined with movement and your metabolic support, your body can access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. When applied to specific areas like your abdomen, red light helps preferentially release fat from those exact locations. Studies show combining red light therapy with exercise led to 444% more fat loss in treated areas compared to exercise alone. The 810-850nm near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep abdominal tissue, making them particularly effective for stubborn visceral fat.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your skin and anti-ageing goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, whilst potentially boosting daytime energy. For fat loss support, using the panel 15–30 minutes before movement maximises fat mobilisation and burning. For overall wellness and metabolism support, mornings are ideal. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 near-infrared LEDs (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) that emit light invisible to the human eye—this is completely normal and means they're working powerfully beneath the surface. The red LEDs you can see are the visible portion. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow from the NIR LEDs, confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (abdominal fat, collagen in deeper skin layers), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (facial fine lines, radiance), a greater distance can be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a facial anti-ageing protocol looks different to a fat loss protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Given your macular retinoschesis, eye protection is important. Red and near-infrared light are not inherently harmful to healthy eyes, but your situation requires extra care. Always wear the protective goggles included with your SOLO 2.0 during facial sessions, or keep your eyes closed. Never direct the light into your eyes. If you experience any discomfort, move the device further away or take a break. Your vision is precious—protecting it is more important than maximising intensity.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For facial treatments, cleanse your face completely before starting.
