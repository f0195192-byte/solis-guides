How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Chronic pain like yours is driven by pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding damaged tissues, restricting blood flow and sensitising nerve endings. Near-infrared light penetrates deep into your spine, lower back, neck, and cervical tissues to downregulate these inflammatory compounds whilst increasing blood flow to injured areas. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. Your body's natural pain-relieving mechanisms activate more efficiently, reducing the intensity of signals reaching your brain.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Post-injury arthritis involves breakdown of protective cartilage and accumulation of inflammatory compounds in joint spaces. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes—the cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen supporting this repair process, breaking the inflammatory cycle that prevents healing. This is particularly valuable for your thoracic outlet syndrome and cervical stenosis where conventional treatments only mask symptoms rather than supporting actual tissue repair.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Your whole-body stiffness reflects central nervous system hypersensitivity where your brain amplifies normal sensations into widespread chronic pain. Additionally, mitochondrial dysfunction is causing the fatigue that compounds your condition. Red light therapy addresses both: it desensitises overactive nerve endings that misinterpret signals as pain, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold, whilst simultaneously enhancing mitochondrial function throughout your entire body to boost cellular ATP production that combats the exhaustion characteristic of chronic pain syndromes.

Stubborn fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides that your body struggles to access and burn. Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. Combined with movement, your body can actually access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. Applied directly to your abdomen, red light helps release fat from that specific area when paired with light activity—not magic spot reduction, but genuine membrane permeability allowing preferential fat burning from treated zones.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your pain and inflammation goals, morning sessions are ideal—they help combat overnight stiffness and set a positive tone for movement throughout the day. For fibromyalgia and systemic energy, morning also mirrors natural sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm. For fat loss, use the panel 30 minutes before light activity. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your spine, joints, and tissues. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
