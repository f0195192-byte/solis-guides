The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Skin Rejuvenation

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a youthful texture. For TSW-damaged skin, enhanced ATP production in these cells gives them the energy needed to rebuild your skin's support structure. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to accelerate the natural repair and renewal cycle, literally strengthening skin that has become thin and leathery.

Eczema & Topical Steroid Withdrawal (TSW)

TSW creates a cascade of inflammation and barrier dysfunction—your skin has lost its ability to retain moisture and regulate immune responses properly. Red light therapy accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to rebuild your protective "shield" at the cellular level. Critically, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing the relentless itching that perpetuates the scratch-damage cycle. By reducing inflammatory cytokines and improving skin cell energy production, red light helps restore your skin's natural barrier function and immune balance without harsh medications that can worsen TSW.

Hyperpigmentation & Skin Tone

Uneven pigmentation stems from sluggish cellular turnover and poor blood flow. Old, pigmented cells linger whilst fresh ones struggle to break through—creating those distinct coloured patches. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin (angiogenesis), dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With enhanced circulation, your cells have the fuel they need to regenerate faster. Old, pigmented cells get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, clear ones. The result is a more even, balanced skin tone across your entire body.

Scarring & Wound Healing

When your skin barrier is compromised like in TSW, wounds occur easily and scar formation becomes a real concern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition—the way healthy skin actually heals—whilst preventing excessive scar tissue formation. For fresh wounds, red light accelerates the migration of repair cells to damaged sites and enhances healthy tissue formation. Cuts and scrapes close faster with less visible scarring and reduced infection risk, because your body's natural repair mechanisms become significantly more efficient.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your TSW and skin goals, consistency matters most—pick a time you'll stick to. That said, timing can enhance specific benefits. For anti-ageing and skin healing, morning sessions support your natural circadian rhythm. For itching reduction and calming TSW symptoms, evening sessions 1–2 hours before bed can help soothe your nervous system and reduce nighttime itching when it's often worst. Many TSW sufferers find evening red light therapy helps them sleep better too. The best time is whichever you'll use consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. These invisible wavelengths penetrate deeper than visible red light, reaching into your skin layers to support barrier repair and healing. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles), you generally want to be closer. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

For your TSW skin specifically: We're recommending slightly greater distances than some protocols because your skin is extremely sensitive right now. This gentle approach still delivers therapeutic results whilst minimising any irritation risk as your barrier heals.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different from a pain relief protocol.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions. Your goggles are included—use them if needed.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For your TSW skin, this also means avoiding any potential irritants during your treatment window—just clean skin and the therapeutic light.
