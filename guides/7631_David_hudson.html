The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Chronic pain stems from pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding your tissues, sensitising nerve endings and perpetuating pain cycles. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep to the source of inflammation, directly downregulating these harmful compounds whilst enhancing blood flow to damaged areas. Improved circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste. The result is measurable pain reduction and restored mobility without pharmaceutical dependence.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Arthritis involves protective cartilage breakdown combined with accumulated inflammatory compounds in synovial fluid. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, stimulating chondrocytes — the cells responsible for cartilage repair — whilst reducing systemic inflammation. Enhanced blood flow to the joint delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen, breaking the inflammatory cycle that prevents healing. This leads to reduced morning stiffness, improved mobility, and actual support for joint tissue repair rather than just symptom suppression.

Testosterone & Energy Support

Testosterone production and energy both depend on mitochondrial efficiency. Leydig cells in your testes require substantial ATP to synthesise testosterone efficiently, whilst every cell in your body powers itself through mitochondrial energy production. Red and NIR light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, dramatically increasing ATP output. This boosts your body's capacity to synthesise testosterone naturally whilst giving every cell more fuel for recovery, strength, and vitality. Enhanced energy and hormone production work synergistically to restore physical resilience.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your pain management and joint health goals, morning sessions help reduce stiffness and prepare your joints for daily activity. For energy and testosterone support, morning or early afternoon sessions mirror your body's natural hormone rhythms and support circadian alignment. For recovery and reduced evening pain, afternoon or early evening sessions give your tissues time to respond before rest. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to daily.

Why can't I see all the lights on my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your joint tissue and muscles where chronic pain originates. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
