The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate your dermis to directly activate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. By supercharging these cells with ATP (cellular energy), red light therapy essentially gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged collagen fibres and build fresh ones more efficiently. The result is measurably smoother skin with visibly reduced wrinkle depth. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem—your skin can't hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create the relentless itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively, whilst calming overactive nerve endings that perpetuate itching. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means your facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, dilating excessively in response to triggers. Red light therapy is uniquely suited for this because it's a "cold" therapy—no heat, no thermal damage to already-sensitive skin. Instead, it works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. The result is reduced persistent redness, calmer blood vessel reactivity, and fewer trigger responses without harsh medications.

Skin Texture & Glow

Dull, uneven skin stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to the skin's surface. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels (angiogenesis), dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With better circulation, your cells have the fuel they need to regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, dull cells get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh ones. Your skin tone becomes more even, brightness improves, and that flat complexion transforms into something genuinely radiant.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen and elastin form your skin's structural scaffolding—the support system that keeps skin firm and youthful. As we age, production slows and these fibres deteriorate, causing sagging and loss of definition. Red light directly stimulates fibroblasts to build fresh collagen and elastin more efficiently, essentially restoring your skin's internal structure. This creates genuine firmness and elasticity, not temporary plumping, but actual structural improvement from within that compounds over time.

Perimenopause & Menopause Symptoms

Declining oestrogen disrupts your hypothalamus (brain's temperature regulation centre), creating vasomotor instability responsible for hot flushes and night sweats. Hormonal shifts affect neurotransmitter balance, contributing to mood swings, anxiety, and sleep disruption. Red light therapy stabilises these disrupted systems by stimulating beta-endorphin release (natural mood-lifters) whilst modulating autonomic nervous system function. Enhanced mitochondrial function supports energy, whilst improved cerebral blood flow helps cognitive symptoms like brain fog. The result is reduced hot flush severity, improved mood stability, and better sleep quality.

Hormonal Balance

Hormonal disruption during transitions stems from both fluctuating hormone levels and systemic inflammation that compounds hormonal dysregulation. Red light therapy enhances mitochondrial function in hormone-producing glands and reduces the inflammatory cytokines that interfere with delicate endocrine balance. By supporting cellular energy production at the glandular level, your body's natural regulatory mechanisms can function more effectively, helping restore equilibrium during hormonal transitions.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin goals, morning or afternoon sessions work beautifully—red light mirrors natural sunrise wavelengths and supports skin repair throughout the day. For your menopausal symptoms and hormonal balance, morning sessions are ideal as they help regulate circadian rhythms and stabilise the autonomic nervous system that drives vasomotor symptoms. This natural alignment with your body's rhythm creates compounding benefits. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeply into your skin's dermal layer and supporting collagen synthesis. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For surface-level skin concerns like wrinkles and redness, a moderate distance (30 cm) allows even coverage without oversaturation. For deeper penetration supporting hormonal and systemic benefits, slightly closer distances (20 cm) ensure adequate energy reaches the tissues involved.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you might need slightly longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different skin conditions and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why an eczema protocol looks different to a collagen protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions. Your SOLO 2.0 includes protective goggles for this purpose.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing, makeup, sunscreen, and moisturisers can block light penetration, significantly reducing effectiveness. Ensure the targeted areas are free from barriers to allow maximum absorption and optimal results for your skin and hormonal goals.
