The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Atopic dermatitis is a broken skin barrier problem—your protective lipid layer fails to retain moisture, triggering inflammatory cascades and intense itching. Red light therapy accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating keratinocyte proliferation and fibroblast collagen synthesis, literally rebuilding the structural scaffold that holds moisture. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings responsible for the relentless itch-scratch cycle. Clinical trials show 79% of patients experience reduced itching within weeks, with visible skin thickening and reduced flare frequency. Unlike topical steroids (which thin skin long-term) or immunosuppressants, red light supports your skin's actual ability to function properly again.

Acne & Breakouts

Acne stems from excess sebum production, bacterial colonisation (Cutibacterium acnes), and deep follicular inflammation. Red light penetrates to sebaceous glands where it downregulates excess oil production whilst simultaneously modulating inflammatory response, calming painful, scarring breakouts from within. Unlike antibiotics (which disrupt your skin microbiome and breed resistance) or harsh retinoids (which cause severe irritation), red light addresses inflammation without damage. Studies show 77.9% reduction in inflammatory acne spots after just 8 weeks, with clearer skin and reduced scarring—your skin learns to regulate itself properly.

Scarring & Skin Texture

Scars form when collagen deposits in a chaotic, disorganised pattern during rushed wound closure. Red light encourages orderly collagen deposition—the way healthy skin is actually structured—whilst boosting fibroblast activity to literally rebuild damaged skin texture. For depressed scars, enhanced collagen production helps "lift" and level the skin's surface. Enhanced microcirculation delivers nutrients for optimal repair. The result is measurably smoother, more uniform skin texture over 6-8 weeks.

Joint & Neck Pain

Joint pain stems from inflammatory cytokine accumulation and poor microcirculation depriving tissue of oxygen and nutrients. Near-infrared light penetrates deep into joint capsules and muscle tissue, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst stimulating nitric oxide release that dilates blood vessels. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and removes inflammatory waste, breaking the pain cycle. Studies show 50% reduction in chronic pain and 70% improvement in arthritic joint discomfort within 4 weeks—genuine pain relief supporting your body's natural repair capacity.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin goals, morning sessions are ideal—they mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, which helps regulate inflammatory responses throughout the day. For joint and neck pain, use the device after activity when tissue is warm, which enhances circulation and recovery. Given your goal to restore skin comfort and manage dermatitis flare-ups that worsen in cold London weather, morning light therapy can help "prime" your skin barrier for the day ahead. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
