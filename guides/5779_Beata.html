The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to activate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. By supercharging these cells with ATP (cellular energy), red light therapy gives them the fuel to repair damaged collagen fibres and build new ones efficiently. This directly reduces fine line depth and wrinkle appearance, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers nutrients to skin cells, accelerating natural renewal cycles.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea involves hyperreactive facial blood vessels and chronic inflammation. Red light therapy is uniquely suited because it's a "cold" therapy — no heat, no thermal damage to already-sensitive skin. It works at the cellular level by modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring whilst balancing reactive oxygen species. The result is reduced persistent redness, calmer blood vessel reactivity, and improved tolerance to triggers. Your skin's inflammatory threshold increases, meaning everyday stressors have less impact on flare-ups.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Uneven pigmentation and dark spots stem from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to skin's surface. Red light therapy stimulates angiogenesis — the formation of new blood vessels — dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every skin layer. With better circulation, cells regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, pigmented cells from sun damage or scarring get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh cells. Your skin tone becomes more even, brightness improves, and that dull complexion transforms into something naturally radiant.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition — the way healthy skin is actually structured — whilst preventing excessive cells responsible for raised scars. For indented scars, boosted collagen production literally helps "lift" the skin's surface. For fresh wounds, red light accelerates repair cell migration and enhances healthy tissue formation. Cuts and surgical incisions close faster with less scarring and reduced infection risk.

Skin Texture & Glow

Radiant skin depends on healthy cell turnover, strong barrier function, and robust microcirculation. Red and NIR light enhance mitochondrial function in skin cells, giving them energy for efficient renewal. Improved blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory waste. Enhanced cellular energy combined with reduced inflammation creates a more even skin texture, clearer complexion, and that coveted natural glow.

Collagen & Elasticity

Your skin's firmness depends entirely on collagen and elastin — the structural fibres that keep skin tight and resilient. As we age, collagen production slows and existing fibres break down, causing sagging and loss of elasticity. Red light directly stimulates fibroblasts, the cells responsible for both collagen synthesis and elastin production. By enhancing ATP production in these cells, red light therapy accelerates collagen turnover, helping your skin rebuild its support structure. The result is visibly firmer, more elastic skin with restored resilience.

Hormonal Balance & Endometriosis Relief

Endometriosis creates chronic pelvic pain through tissue inflammation and immune dysregulation. Near-infrared light penetrates pelvic tissue to reduce inflammatory markers like IL-6 whilst modulating immune responses. Red light therapy decreases the infiltration of inflammatory T-cells that attack reproductive tissue, addressing the root dysfunction rather than just masking pain. Enhanced cellular energy and improved blood flow to pelvic organs support natural healing and hormonal regulation.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Period pain and irregular cycles stem from dysregulated hormonal timing and pelvic inflammation. Red light therapy works on multiple fronts: it reduces inflammatory cytokines in reproductive tissue, improves blood flow to reproductive organs (helping "reset" hormonal timing mechanisms), and enhances mitochondrial function in hormone-producing cells. Improved circadian rhythm regulation from red light exposure also supports more regular cycles and reduced PMS severity.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For skin rejuvenation and radiance, morning sessions align with your natural circadian rhythm and allow skin to absorb serums post-treatment throughout the day. For hormonal and period pain support, afternoon or early evening sessions give your body time to respond before sleep. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
