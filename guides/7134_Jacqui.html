The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Joint Health

Chronic joint pain stems from inflammatory compounds accumulating in synovial fluid whilst protective cartilage breaks down. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep into the joint capsule, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate pain cycles. Simultaneously, NIR wavelengths stimulate chondrocytes — the cells responsible for cartilage repair — whilst enhancing blood flow to deliver oxygen and nutrients critical for tissue regeneration. The result is measurable reduction in pain and improved mobility without pharmaceutical intervention.

Post-Surgical Recovery

After surgery, your body faces a critical repair window. Red light therapy supercharges this process by enhancing ATP production in healing tissue, giving cells the energy they need to synthesise new collagen and restore structural integrity efficiently. Red wavelengths directly stimulate fibroblasts whilst NIR penetrates deeper to reduce inflammatory response that would otherwise slow healing. Enhanced circulation delivers crucial nutrients and removes inflammatory waste accumulating at the surgical site. Clinical studies show significantly faster wound closure, reduced scarring, and accelerated functional recovery when red light is applied during the post-operative period.

Hormonal Balance & Menopause Support

Menopause means declining oestrogen disrupts your hypothalamus (the brain's temperature regulation centre), creating the vasomotor instability responsible for hot flushes and night sweats. Red light therapy stabilises these disrupted systems by stimulating beta-endorphin release — natural mood-lifters — whilst modulating your autonomic nervous system function. This helps your brain's regulatory centres adapt to hormonal changes more smoothly. Enhanced mitochondrial function supports sustained energy and cognitive clarity, whilst improved cerebral blood flow helps reduce brain fog characteristic of this transition. The result is reduced hot flush severity, improved mood stability, and better sleep quality.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis STUDIO 2.0?

For your pain and joint concerns, using your STUDIO 2.0 in the evening or after activity when inflammation peaks will give you the most immediate relief. For hormonal balance and menopause symptoms, morning sessions align with your natural circadian rhythm and provide steadier support throughout the day. For post-surgical recovery, consistency matters more than timing — daily sessions whenever convenient during that critical repair window are most important. You can also use it twice daily if needed: morning for hormonal support and evening for pain management. The best time is the one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your STUDIO 2.0 has 480 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your joints and tissues where therapeutic effects occur. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the STUDIO 2.0's large panel. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
