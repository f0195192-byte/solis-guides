The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety, Depression & SAD Support

Anxiety and depression involve disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood — conditions both chronic Lyme and Hashimoto's exacerbate through systemic inflammation. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine). Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst near-infrared wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. For seasonal affective patterns, red light helps regulate circadian rhythms and supports serotonin synthesis when daylight is limited. By addressing the mitochondrial and inflammatory root causes, you're tackling the biological dysfunction underlying anxiety rather than just managing symptoms.

Energy Levels & Mitochondrial Support

Chronic Lyme and Hashimoto's both cause mitochondrial dysfunction — your cellular powerplants don't produce sufficient ATP, the energy currency fuelling every biological process. Red light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway, dramatically increasing ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. This is particularly important for post-Lyme fatigue and thyroid-related exhaustion. Enhanced mitochondrial efficiency means your cells have more fuel to power everything from basic metabolism to brain function, addressing fatigue at its cellular foundation rather than working around it.

Nervous System Regulation

Chronic Lyme and Hashimoto's dysregulate your autonomic nervous system, leaving you stuck in sympathetic (fight-or-flight) activation. Red light therapy modulates nervous system function through enhanced mitochondrial support in neurons and by reducing systemic inflammation that keeps your nervous system hypervigilant. This helps shift you toward parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) dominance, reducing anxiety, improving stress resilience, and supporting the nervous system's natural ability to regulate itself. Better nervous system function cascades into improved mood, better sleep, and reduced inflammation throughout your body.

Anti-Ageing & Skin Rejuvenation

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. The same systemic anti-inflammatory effects supporting your nervous system and energy also benefit skin by reducing inflammatory markers that accelerate skin ageing.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your mood and energy goals, morning sessions are ideal — they mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, helping establish a positive emotional baseline for the day. Morning light exposure also aligns with your body's natural cortisol curve, supporting healthy energy mobilisation without overstimulation. For anxiety management, consistency matters more than timing, so the best time is whenever you'll use it reliably. If you experience any evening mood lift, you can do additional sessions, but avoid using within 2 hours of bedtime if you're sensitive, as some users find it slightly energising.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Circulation

Wound healing
Red light accelerates tissue repair by stimulating collagen synthesis and reducing inflammation, helping any lingering Lyme-related joint or tissue issues heal more effectively.
