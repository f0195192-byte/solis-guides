The science
How red light therapy helps

Endometriosis, Adenomyosis & Pelvic Pain

Adenomyosis and endometriosis are fundamentally inflammatory conditions where tissue overgrowth and dysregulated immune responses create chronic pelvic pain. Near-infrared light penetrates deep into pelvic tissue, reducing inflammatory markers like IL-6 and TNF-alpha whilst modulating immune cell activity. This calms the overactive inflammatory response driving pain without suppressing normal immune function. Enhanced blood flow to affected tissue supports healing whilst reduced neuroinflammation helps desensitise overactive nerve endings that amplify pain signals. The result is measurable reduction in pelvic pain intensity and improved quality of life.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Menstrual pain stems from dysregulated hormonal signalling and excessive uterine inflammation during your cycle. Red light therapy addresses both mechanisms. It modulates circadian rhythm regulation in reproductive tissues, helping restore balanced hormone production throughout your cycle. Simultaneously, it reduces inflammatory mediators responsible for cramping pain, particularly when used during the luteal phase. Enhanced blood flow to reproductive organs supports optimal progesterone and oestrogen production, creating more regular, less painful cycles over time.

Sleep Quality & Circadian Regulation

Poor sleep stems from disrupted melatonin production and dysregulated circadian rhythm. Unlike blue light which suppresses melatonin, red and near-infrared wavelengths stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally. Evening red light exposure signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down whilst enhanced mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating sleep means your circadian system operates efficiently. This supports faster sleep onset, fewer night-time awakenings, and deeper restorative sleep phases.

Energy & Fatigue Management

Chronic fatigue in adenomyosis stems from mitochondrial dysfunction—your cells lack energy for basic repair and renewal. Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, dramatically boosting ATP production. This gives your cells fuel for repair, recovery, and optimal function. Reduced oxidative stress from improved mitochondrial efficiency means less cellular damage and better energy availability throughout the day. Combined with improved sleep and reduced inflammation, energy improvements are often among the first benefits noticed.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing is important. Use your energy protocol in the morning—this mirrors sunrise and boosts your circadian rhythm whilst giving your cells immediate ATP fuel. For pelvic pain and period pain, daytime use is ideal so the anti-inflammatory benefits peak during waking hours. For sleep, use your circadian protocol 1–2 hours before bed—this is when the melatonin-supporting effect is strongest. Consistency matters more than perfection, so choose times you can stick to daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 near-infrared LEDs (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which are completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your pelvic tissue, abdomen, and mitochondria. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
