The science
How red light therapy helps

Chronic Pain Management

Both MS and hEDS involve chronic inflammation and mitochondrial dysfunction that perpetuate pain. Red and near-infrared light penetrate deep into affected tissues, downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing ATP production in damaged cells. This gives your tissues energy to repair themselves, reduces inflammatory signalling that amplifies pain perception, and improves blood flow to remove inflammatory waste. The result is measurable pain reduction without medication dependency, addressing root cellular dysfunction rather than just masking symptoms.

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Chronic pain and systemic illness often dysregulate neurotransmitter balance whilst inflammation in brain tissue impairs mood regulation. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in neurons and mood-regulating brain regions, boosting ATP for neurotransmitter production. Near-infrared penetration reaches deep brain tissue to reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. Simultaneously, it stimulates beta-endorphin release—your brain's natural mood-lifters—and modulates stress hormone (cortisol) responses. The result is measurably improved mood, reduced anxiety, and better emotional resilience.

Nervous System Regulation

MS and hEDS both involve nervous system dysregulation—overactive pain signalling, impaired parasympathetic (calming) function, and abnormal nerve-pain communication. Red light therapy desensitises overactive nerve endings by reducing inflammatory pain mediators and restoring proper pain threshold calibration. Enhanced mitochondrial function in nerve tissue supports regeneration and proper signalling. Improved autonomic nervous system balance shifts your system from fight-or-flight dominance toward parasympathetic activation, reducing baseline stress responses and allowing your nervous system to regulate more normally.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

For your goals—chronic pain, mood, and nervous system regulation—timing is particularly important. Morning sessions are ideal for mood and anxiety: red light in the morning aligns with your natural circadian rhythm and sets a positive emotional tone for the day. For pain management, use whenever pain is most intense—many people find mid-morning or afternoon sessions helpful, as pain often peaks at specific times. For nervous system regulation and evening wind-down, use 1–2 hours before bed: red light supports parasympathetic activation without the circadian disruption of blue light, helping your nervous system shift into rest mode for better sleep. The best approach is consistency: establish a routine you'll maintain, whether that's daily morning sessions for mood or targeted pain sessions throughout the day.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into tissue to reach the cellular level. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Beyond your primary goals, regular use of your SURGE 2.0 may deliver additional benefits particularly relevant to managing chronic conditions like MS and hEDS:

Chronic pain and nervous system dysregulation often destroy sleep. Red light supports deeper, more restorative sleep by regulating melatonin and helping your nervous system wind down—especially important for pain management and recovery.
Inflammation reduction
Systemic inflammation is central to both MS and hEDS progression. Red light reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines throughout your body, supporting immune modulation and potentially slowing disease progression.
Energy & mitochondrial function
Mitochondrial dysfunction underlies both fatigue in MS and cellular dysfunction in hEDS. Enhanced ATP production means more sustained energy for daily activities and better tissue repair capacity.
Immune support
Red light enhances mitochondrial function in immune cells, supporting balanced immune responses. For MS, this may help modulate autoimmune activity; for hEDS, stronger immunity supports tissue integrity.
Wound healing & tissue repair
Both conditions involve impaired tissue healing. Red light accelerates collagen synthesis and tissue repair, supporting your body's natural healing mechanisms for cuts, bruises, and general tissue maintenance.
