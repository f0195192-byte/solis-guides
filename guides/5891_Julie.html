The science
How red light therapy helps

Psoriasis

Psoriasis is your immune system mistakenly attacking healthy skin, causing cells to proliferate at 10 times the normal rate. Red light therapy normalises this aberrant cell cycle by modulating the immune response. It reduces the infiltration of inflammatory T-cells driving the attack whilst normalising skin cell proliferation rates back to healthy baseline. Near-infrared wavelengths reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-alpha and IL-17 that perpetuate plaques. The result is visible clearance of scaly plaques—not temporary suppression but actual normalisation of your skin's cellular behaviour.

Knee & Joint Pain

Your joint pain stems from inflammatory compounds accumulating in synovial fluid and breakdown of protective cartilage. Red light penetrates deep to the joint capsule, reducing inflammatory markers whilst stimulating chondrocytes—the cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients supporting this repair process, breaking the inflammation-damage cycle that conventional treatments only mask. This is particularly important for active individuals like yourself who need functional joints.

Circulation & Infection Prevention

Near-infrared light enhances mitochondrial function in immune cells like macrophages and lymphocytes, giving them the ATP energy needed to patrol effectively and mount strong immune responses. Improved microcirculation from enhanced nitric oxide production means better oxygen delivery to tissues—a critical factor in preventing recurrent cellulitis. Enhanced immune cell function combined with better tissue oxygenation creates a hostile environment for bacterial infection whilst supporting your body's natural defences.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

For your goals, morning sessions are ideal. Red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths, supporting your circadian rhythm and immune function—both critical for infection prevention and psoriasis management. Morning sessions also allow your joints time to respond before your physically demanding work. You can add evening sessions for additional joint support if needed, but consistency at the same time daily will maximise immune benefits.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 near-infrared LEDs (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) that are completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and indicates they're working. The NIR light penetrates deeply to reach your joints and immune cells. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
