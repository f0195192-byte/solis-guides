The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to directly stimulate fibroblasts — the specialised cells responsible for producing and maintaining collagen and elastin. By enhancing ATP production in these cells, red light therapy gives them the energy to repair damaged collagen fibres and synthesise fresh ones more efficiently. This process measurably reduces wrinkle depth, improves skin firmness, and restores a smoother, more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure simultaneously delivers more oxygen and nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle that slows with age.

Skin Texture & Glow

Red light therapy accelerates cellular turnover and dramatically improves microcirculation to skin layers. Better blood flow means more oxygen and nutrient delivery, while accelerated cell renewal means old, dull cells are cleared away faster and replaced with fresh, vibrant ones. The 630nm and 660nm wavelengths work synergistically to enhance this process, creating visibly smoother skin texture and that characteristic luminous glow. Reduced inflammatory responses also mean less redness and blotchiness, resulting in a more balanced, radiant complexion over consistent use.

Collagen & Elasticity

Fibroblasts are the foundation of skin firmness, constantly producing the collagen matrix that keeps skin plump and elastic. Red light activates these cells at the mitochondrial level, providing the ATP energy they need for efficient collagen synthesis. NIR penetration reaches deeper dermal layers where this structural collagen resides, creating comprehensive rejuvenation throughout your skin's architecture. The result is measurable increases in skin elasticity, improved firmness, and restored natural resilience — your skin literally becomes more structurally sound from within.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For skin rejuvenation goals, morning sessions work beautifully — they mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and set a positive tone for your day. Alternatively, evening sessions (1–2 hours before bed) also work well and may support sleep quality. For best results with anti-ageing and collagen stimulation, consistency matters more than timing — choose a time you'll stick to daily and make it part of your routine. Many users find morning sessions most convenient, pairing their red light therapy with skincare immediately after for optimal ingredient absorption.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. These NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface as the visible red LEDs. To confirm the NIR lights are active, point a smartphone camera at the panel — you'll see a faint purple glow confirming all wavelengths are operating.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
