The science
How red light therapy helps

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain

Your central nervous system has become hypersensitive, amplifying pain signals. Red and near-infrared light desensitises these overactive nerve endings, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold. Simultaneously, NIR penetrates deep to enhance mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting ATP production to combat the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia. This dual action addresses both the pain amplification and the energy deficit driving your symptoms.

Neuropathy & Nerve Pain

Near-infrared wavelengths stimulate regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates the tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating pain signals. The result is reduced burning and tingling sensations, improved nerve function, and better sensation in affected areas.

Headaches & Migraines

Red and NIR light penetrate your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons and reduce neuroinflammation that drives migraines. This provides more ATP for healthy neural communication whilst reducing the inflammatory cascade that triggers pain. Improved cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst your brain's natural pain-relief mechanisms activate more efficiently.

Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Near-infrared light reduces inflammatory markers like IL-6 in pelvic tissue and modulates immune responses, providing pain relief without pharmaceuticals. Enhanced blood flow to reproductive organs supports tissue repair whilst reduced inflammation helps "reset" hormonal signalling mechanisms that contribute to pain cycles. The result is reduced pelvic pain, improved circulation to affected areas, and better management of pain intensity.

Fatigue & Energy

Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This stimulation dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. Your cells have more fuel to power repair, immune function, and daily activities — the cellular foundation that supports everything else.

Hair Loss & Hair Thickness

Red light wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, extending the active growth phase of your hair cycle. It also triggers nitric oxide release in your scalp, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is increased hair density, thicker individual strands, and a healthier scalp environment supporting sustained hair growth.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific needs, timing is important. Morning sessions (for energy and fatigue) mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm—ideal for fibromyalgia and energy support. For migraine prevention, morning is optimal. For endometriosis and pelvic pain, daily sessions during your cycle are most effective, ideally in the morning or early afternoon. For general pain management, consistency matters more than timing, though afternoon sessions can support evening comfort. For sleep support (which many fibromyalgia patients benefit from), use the panel 1–2 hours before bed. Aim for consistency—the best schedule is the one you'll stick to given your symptom variability.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR (near-infrared) LEDs that emit light at 810nm, 830nm, and 850nm wavelengths—this is completely invisible to the human eye and entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial or head treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
