The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Red light therapy addresses eczema on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to rebuild your protective "shield," helping your skin retain moisture effectively again. It also calms overactive nerve endings directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that perpetuates eczema. By reducing inflammatory T-cell infiltration and supporting skin barrier integrity, your skin's natural regulatory mechanisms restore, leading to fewer flare-ups and deeper, more restorative sleep as itching subsides.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea stems from hyper-reactive facial blood vessels and inflammatory dysregulation. Red light therapy is a "cold" therapy — no heat, no thermal aggravation. Instead, it works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. It reduces persistent erythema and calms excessive vessel reactivity, increasing your skin's inflammatory threshold so everyday triggers have less impact. The result is noticeably reduced facial redness and fewer reactive episodes.

Pigmentation & Dark Spots

Sunspots persist because old, pigmented cells from UV damage accumulate on your skin's surface due to sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow. Red light therapy stimulates angiogenesis—the formation of new blood vessels—dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every skin layer. With better circulation, your cells regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, pigmented cells get cleared away quickly and replaced by fresh, clear ones. The result is visibly more even skin tone and brightness as age spots fade.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when collagen deposits in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition — the way healthy skin is naturally structured. It also prevents excessive cell proliferation responsible for raised scars, whilst for indented or depressed scars, boosted collagen production helps "lift" the tissue level and smooth the surface. Enhanced cell migration and healthy tissue formation mean your body's repair mechanisms work more efficiently, with faster healing and less visible scarring.

Sleep Quality

Red and near-infrared wavelengths naturally stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin — your body's natural sleep hormone — unlike blue light which suppresses it. By using red light in the evening, you signal "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down. Enhanced mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating your circadian rhythm means sleep regulatory systems operate more efficiently. As hand itching subsides from eczema improvement, you'll experience fewer nighttime wake-ups, allowing deeper restorative sleep phases critical for recovery.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing is strategic. For eczema and scar healing, morning or midday sessions work well — they give your tissue time to respond throughout the day. For sleep quality and circadian support, use your SOLO 2.0 in the evening, 1–2 hours before bed, when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. For rosacea and pigmentation, you can use anytime — consistency matters more than timing. Aim for a routine you'll stick with: perhaps morning sessions for eczema and scars, then your evening 15-minute protocol for sleep. This gives May maximum benefit across all your goals.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeper into skin and tissue than visible red light alone. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
