The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Chronic pain is fundamentally a problem of inflammatory cytokines and poor tissue blood flow. Red and near-infrared light downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate pain cycles, whilst stimulating nitric oxide release that dilates blood vessels and dramatically improves circulation. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients for tissue repair whilst removing inflammatory waste. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep into joints, spinal tissue, and muscles where your pain originates, addressing the root cause rather than simply masking symptoms. The result is measurable pain reduction without the side effects of pharmaceutical alternatives.

Neuropathic pain from damaged nerve fibres involves faulty signals and poor microcirculation starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients. Enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves need for repair whilst removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating pain signals. This is particularly significant because conventional treatments for neuropathy offer only symptom management; red light therapy supports actual nerve tissue repair and functional recovery.

Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing enhances results. Morning sessions (anxiety, mood, energy support) work best when red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm — ideal for reducing anxiety and stress throughout the day. Afternoon sessions work well for pain relief and neuropathy — your body has the whole day to respond. Evening sessions 1–2 hours before bed (sleep support) signal natural wind-down to your brain. For gut health, mornings are optimal. Vicky, your best routine is likely: morning anxiety and mood support (10 mins face), afternoon pain relief (15 mins lower back or neck/shoulders), and evening sleep support (15 mins before bed). Consistency matters more than perfect timing — stick to what you'll maintain.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and means they're working perfectly. The NIR lights are delivering powerful therapeutic benefits beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, nerves, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, mood support), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a pain protocol looks different to a skin or anxiety protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially from a device as powerful as the SUPREME 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
