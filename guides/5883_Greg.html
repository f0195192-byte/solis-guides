The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Atopic Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem: your skin cannot retain moisture, inflammatory signals run excessive, and hypersensitive nerve endings create that relentless itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to rebuild your protective "shield" and helping retain moisture effectively. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching, and visible improvement in skin texture and tone. Rather than masking symptoms with steroids, red light supports your skin's ability to function properly again.

Anti-Ageing, Collagen & Skin Rejuvenation

As we age, skin's support structure breaks down as collagen and elastin production slows. Red light activates your fibroblasts — the cells responsible for building and maintaining collagen and elastin. By delivering ATP (cellular energy) to these cells, red light therapy gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged collagen fibres and build fresh ones more efficiently. The result is measurably smoother skin, visibly reduced wrinkle depth, improved firmness, and restored youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from near-infrared exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle — essential for long-term skin health and preventing future damage.

Skin Texture & Radiance

Uneven tone, dull skin, and redness all stem from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to your skin's surface. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin, dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With better circulation, your cells have the fuel needed to regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, damaged cells get cleared away quickly and replaced by fresh, clear cells. The result is a more even skin tone, improved brightness, and transformation of that dull, blotchy complexion into something genuinely radiant and healthy-looking.

Fat Loss & Body Composition

Stubborn fat resists diet and exercise because fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides. Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. Combined with exercise, your body can actually access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. Applied to specific zones like abdomen, thighs, and legs, red light helps release fat from those exact locations. The result is enhanced fat loss from stubborn areas when combined with exercise and nutrition — your body accessing stored energy more efficiently and supporting sustainable body composition improvements.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your eczema and skin health goals, morning or early afternoon sessions give your skin time to recover and respond. For fat loss, using the panel 10–15 minutes before or immediately after exercise maximises metabolic effect. For sleep quality and stress reduction (which often helps eczema flare-ups), use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Going further
How else can it help you?

In addition to your primary goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver a range of broader wellness benefits that many users notice over time:

Sleep quality
Red and NIR light can help regulate melatonin secretion and circadian rhythm, supporting deeper, more restorative sleep — particularly important for eczema sufferers, as poor sleep often triggers or worsens flare-ups.
Mood & stress resilience
Mitochondrial stimulation supports steady energy production, and many users report improved mood and reduced stress within the first few weeks. Since stress is a major eczema trigger, better stress resilience helps prevent flare-ups.
Wound healing
Red light accelerates tissue repair by stimulating collagen synthesis and reducing inflammation, helping minor cuts, scrapes, or post-eczema skin damage heal faster and with less visible scarring.
Circulation & skin health
Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to skin throughout your body, supporting recovery from inflammatory conditions and enhancing the health of all skin tissue.
Immune support
By improving mitochondrial function and reducing systemic inflammation, regular sessions may help support your body's natural immune responses and reduce inflammatory signalling that drives eczema.
