The science
How red light therapy helps

PCOS, Fertility & Egg Quality

PCOS means your ovaries have impaired mitochondrial function—the energy factories in your egg cells are struggling. Near-infrared light penetrates ovarian tissue directly, enhancing mitochondrial ATP production in developing eggs. This gives oocytes the energy they need for proper maturation, chromosome organisation, and healthy embryo development. Simultaneously, red light modulates the inflammatory and insulin resistance mechanisms underlying PCOS, improving hormone balance and restoring more regular ovulation cycles. Enhanced blood flow to reproductive organs delivers oxygen and nutrients while supporting optimal hormone production. The result: improved egg quality, better ovulation, and higher conception and implantation rates.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Dark spots persist because old, pigmented skin cells aren't clearing away fast enough. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin, dramatically improving oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. Enhanced circulation accelerates skin cell turnover—old, pigmented cells get cleared quickly and replaced by fresh, clear cells from beneath. The 630nm wavelength in particular reduces inflammatory markers that contribute to uneven tone. The result: visibly more even skin tone as sun damage and hyperpigmentation gradually clear away, replaced by healthy, clear skin.

Skin Texture, Glow & Collagen

Red light activates fibroblasts—the cells responsible for building collagen and elastin. By delivering ATP directly to these cells, red light therapy gives them the energy to repair damaged collagen fibres and synthesise new, healthy ones. This improves skin firmness, reduces fine lines, and restores that youthful plumpness. Enhanced blood flow from near-infrared exposure delivers nutrients and oxygen that support skin renewal, whilst reduced oxidative stress means less collagen breakdown. The cumulative effect is visibly smoother, firmer, more radiant skin—genuine structural improvement from within.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your fertility and hormonal goals, morning sessions are ideal—they help regulate circadian rhythms and support natural hormone production cycles. For skin, any time works, though using the panel before your evening skincare routine allows your skin to absorb serums more effectively afterward. If you're doing both fertility and skin sessions, consider morning sessions for abdomen (fertility support) and evening for face (skincare integration). Consistency matters most—pick times you can stick with daily or several times weekly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 near-infrared LEDs (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) that emit light completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and means they're working. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming the NIR lights are active. These invisible wavelengths penetrate deepest into tissue, which is exactly what you need for egg quality and deep skin support.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a fertility or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
