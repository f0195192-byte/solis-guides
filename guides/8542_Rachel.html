The science
How red light therapy helps

Acne & Breakouts

Your hormonal breakouts stem from excess sebum production in follicles combined with bacterial growth and deep inflammation. Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands, downregulating oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses that make breakouts angry and painful. It kills acne-causing bacteria without disrupting your skin microbiome like antibiotics do, addressing the root cause rather than suppressing symptoms. The 660nm wavelength is particularly effective at reducing inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate breakout cycles.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Your eczema stems from a broken skin barrier and hypersensitive nerve endings driving the itch-scratch cycle. Red light accelerates barrier repair by enhancing ATP in skin cells, helping rebuild your protective shield to retain moisture. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings that trigger itching, breaking the cycle that stress flares perpetuate. The 630nm wavelength particularly helps reduce inflammatory markers driving eczema whilst 810-850nm wavelengths penetrate deeper to reach affected tissue layers. Unlike topical steroids that suppress symptoms, red light supports your skin's ability to function properly again.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Dark spots and uneven tone result from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to your skin's surface. Red light therapy stimulates angiogenesis—the formation of new blood vessels—dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every skin layer. With enhanced circulation, your cells regenerate faster and old, pigmented cells get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, clear ones. The 660nm wavelength also reduces inflammatory markers causing post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, allowing your natural radiance to return.

Skin Texture & Glow

Dull, rough texture reflects poor cellular regeneration and stagnant microcirculation. Red and NIR wavelengths boost ATP production in skin cells, giving them energy for efficient turnover and repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing metabolic waste that dulls skin. This combination accelerates the natural renewal cycle, revealing fresher, smoother skin layers beneath the dull surface. The result is genuine radiance, not from cosmetic shimmer but from healthy, optimised skin function.

Collagen & Elasticity

Fine lines and loss of firmness occur as collagen and elastin production declines with age. Red light penetrates the dermis to activate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for building and maintaining collagen and elastin. By supercharging these cells with ATP, red light essentially gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged collagen fibres and synthesise new ones more efficiently. The 630nm and 660nm wavelengths are particularly effective at stimulating this collagen production, visibly smoothing wrinkles and restoring firmness from within.

Female Hair Thinning

Hair loss happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of growth phase, and when poor scalp circulation means your follicles aren't receiving the nutrients they need. Red light wakes up stem cells in hair follicles, kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase. The 810-850nm wavelengths penetrate to trigger nitric oxide release in scalp cells, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density and thicker individual strands without chemicals or side effects.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your skin goals and stress-related eczema flares, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm—this helps stabilise cortisol patterns that trigger flares. For acne driven by hormonal cycles, consistency matters most, so choose a time you'll stick to daily. For your knee, morning or early afternoon gives tissue time to respond before activity. For sleep support (which helps eczema and hormonal acne), use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights on my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For your facial protocols, this means treating your face before applying any products (even moisturiser blocks penetration). For your knee, wear shorts or use direct skin exposure for maximum effect.
