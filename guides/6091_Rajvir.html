The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis where they stimulate fibroblasts — cells responsible for collagen and elastin production. By providing these cells with enhanced ATP energy, red light therapy accelerates collagen synthesis and repairs damaged fibres, directly reducing fine line depth and improving skin firmness. Enhanced blood flow delivers nutrients that support this repair process, gradually restoring a smoother, more youthful texture.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema involves a broken skin barrier and overactive inflammatory response. Red light reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst accelerating your skin's barrier repair by boosting ATP in skin cells. It simultaneously calms hypersensitive nerve endings that trigger the itch reflex, breaking the scratch cycle. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved skin function without the side effects of topical steroids.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Dark spots and uneven tone result from poor cell turnover and sluggish circulation. Red light stimulates blood vessel formation in your skin, dramatically improving oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. This accelerates the natural shedding of old, pigmented cells and replaces them with fresh ones. The 630nm wavelength also reduces inflammatory compounds that contribute to hyperpigmentation, resulting in visibly more even, brighter skin tone.

Skin Texture & Glow

Skin radiance depends on efficient cellular turnover and healthy blood flow. Red and NIR light enhance both by boosting ATP in skin cells (improving repair mechanisms) and stimulating angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation). This increases microcirculation, delivering more oxygen and nutrients whilst removing metabolic waste. The result is smoother texture, reduced puffiness, and that naturally luminous glow that comes from genuinely healthy, well-nourished skin.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen is your skin's structural foundation. Red light directly activates fibroblasts via mitochondrial stimulation, providing the cellular energy needed for efficient collagen synthesis. This doesn't just maintain existing collagen — it actively builds new fibres, restoring the 3D scaffold that keeps skin firm and elastic. Over time, this creates measurable improvements in skin elasticity, bounce, and firmness without fillers or invasive procedures.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase and when poor scalp circulation starves them of nutrients. Red light therapy wakes up dormant hair follicle stem cells, reactivating them into the growth phase, whilst NIR penetrates deep to trigger nitric oxide release in scalp cells. This dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is thicker hair strands, increased hair density, and a healthier scalp environment — all without pharmaceutical side effects.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your skin goals, morning sessions work beautifully — they mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and align with your circadian rhythm, which supports skin repair and collagen synthesis. For hair growth, any time is fine, though many users find consistency more important than timing. If you're using the panel for multiple goals, you could do face/skin sessions in the morning (around 10-15 minutes) and scalp sessions in the evening, or combine them into one longer morning routine. The best time is whichever one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a hair growth or inflammation protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
