The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Post-Workout Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres and metabolic waste accumulation — causing delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24-48 hours post-workout. Red light therapy intervenes by reducing muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds. The result is a shorter recovery window, significantly reduced soreness, and better performance gains from your training.

Athletic Performance & Endurance

Athletic performance fundamentally depends on mitochondrial function. Red and near-infrared light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in mitochondrial ATP production, dramatically increasing cellular energy output. Enhanced ATP availability means your muscles have more fuel for sustained effort, allowing longer training sessions with better intensity maintenance. Improved circulation delivers more oxygen to working muscles whilst removing performance-limiting lactate accumulation. The result is measurably enhanced endurance, delayed fatigue onset, and better overall athletic output across training cycles.

Injury Recovery & Rehabilitation

Injury recovery depends on rapid tissue repair and inflammation management. Red and NIR wavelengths penetrate deep into injured muscle and joint tissues, stimulating fibroblasts to produce new collagen whilst enhancing blood flow for oxygen and nutrient delivery. Near-infrared penetration reaches deep structures — perfect for ligament sprains, muscle strains, and joint capsule damage. Enhanced mitochondrial function accelerates repair cell migration to injury sites whilst modulating inflammatory response to prevent excessive swelling. The result is faster healing, restored mobility, and reduced risk of chronic dysfunction or re-injury.

Pain Relief & Joint Health

Chronic pain — particularly in back, spine, knees, shoulders, and neck — stems from pro-inflammatory cytokine accumulation and poor tissue repair. Red light therapy downregulates these inflammatory compounds whilst enhancing blood flow to damaged areas. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues, joint capsules, and ligaments where chronic pain originates. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory waste perpetuating pain cycles. The result is measurable pain reduction, improved mobility, and faster tissue repair supporting long-term joint health.

Pre-Workout Activation

Pre-workout red light therapy "primes" your muscles and nervous system for optimal performance. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy reserve at the start of training, whilst improved circulation and oxygen delivery prepare tissues for high-intensity effort. Red light also stimulates nitric oxide release, enhancing blood vessel dilation and warm-up efficiency. The result is improved performance during the session, reduced injury risk from better tissue preparation, and a physiological head-start that compounds performance gains over training cycles.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your goals, Thierry, timing is strategic. Post-workout recovery should happen within an hour of finishing your session — this is when muscle repair mechanisms are most active and responsive to red light. Pre-workout activation works best 15–30 minutes before training to prime your muscles. For endurance and systemic performance, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, priming your mitochondria for the day's activity. For pain management, consistency matters more than timing — use when pain is most bothersome or at a regular time each day. The best time is ultimately the one you'll stick to, but these windows optimise results.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
