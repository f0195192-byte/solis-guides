The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Anxiety and depression involve disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood. Red and near-infrared light penetrate your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production. Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. The result: measurably improved mood scores, reduced anxiety symptoms, and better stress resilience.

Low Mood & Depression

Depression reflects persistent low neuronal energy production and excessive inflammatory signalling in mood-regulating brain centres. Red light therapy directly activates cytochrome c oxidase in mitochondria, boosting ATP production in neurons that synthesise serotonin and other mood-supporting neurotransmitters. Enhanced cellular energy combined with reduced neuroinflammation supports your brain's natural capacity to regulate mood and emotional resilience. Clinical studies show significant improvements in depression scores even after single sessions.

Nervous System Regulation

Chronic stress keeps your nervous system in "fight or flight" mode, flooding your body with cortisol and adrenaline. Red and NIR light modulates this overactive stress response by enhancing mitochondrial function in key nervous system structures. This supports your parasympathetic nervous system — your body's natural "rest and recover" mode — allowing you to downregulate stress responses more effectively. Better nervous system regulation means improved stress resilience, faster recovery from anxiety, and lower baseline cortisol.

Acne & Breakouts

Acne is a complex cascade involving excess sebum production, bacterial overgrowth, and deep inflammation. Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where acne originates, downregulating excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses. Unlike harsh topicals that strip skin or antibiotics that disrupt your microbiome, red light addresses inflammation without causing damage. Enhanced collagen synthesis from fibroblast stimulation supports skin healing and reduces scarring from previous breakouts.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem: your skin cannot hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create relentless itching. Red light therapy accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblast collagen production, helping rebuild your protective shield to retain moisture effectively. It also calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. Enhanced cellular energy means skin cells can function properly again.

Psoriasis

Psoriasis is an autoimmune condition where skin cells multiply at 10 times the normal rate, creating thick scaly plaques. Red light normalises skin cell proliferation cycles, essentially telling overactive cells to return to healthy growth rates. Simultaneously, it reduces infiltration of inflammatory T-cells responsible for the autoimmune attack. Clinical studies show significant clearance of plaques that previously resisted conventional therapies — not just temporary suppression, but actual normalisation of your skin's cellular behaviour.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis STUDIO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For mood and anxiety support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, helping set a positive tone for the day. For nervous system regulation and sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the calming effect is most powerful. For skin concerns, morning or evening works equally well — the best time is the one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your STUDIO 2.0 has 480 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
