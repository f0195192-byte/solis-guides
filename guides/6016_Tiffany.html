The science
How red light therapy helps

Perimenopause Symptoms & Hormonal Balance

Declining oestrogen disrupts your hypothalamus (brain's temperature regulation centre), creating the vasomotor instability responsible for hot flushes and night sweats. Red light therapy stabilises these disrupted systems by stimulating beta-endorphin release—your body's natural mood-lifters—whilst modulating autonomic nervous system function. Enhanced mitochondrial function supports stable energy throughout hormonal shifts, whilst improved cerebral blood flow helps with cognitive symptoms like brain fog. This allows your regulatory centres to adapt to hormonal changes more smoothly without HRT.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Period pain stems from hormonal fluctuations and inflammatory responses in pelvic tissue. Near-infrared light penetrates the abdomen to reach reproductive organs, reducing inflammatory markers like IL-6 whilst modulating immune responses. Red light therapy also helps regulate circadian rhythms and improves reproductive organ blood flow, essentially "resetting" hormonal timing mechanisms. This supports more regular cycles, reduces pain severity, and addresses underlying inflammation without relying on pharmaceutical interventions.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain

Fibromyalgia means your central nervous system has become hypersensitive—your brain amplifies pain signals, turning normal sensations into widespread chronic pain. Combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing severe fatigue, you're left exhausted and aching. Red light therapy addresses both core issues: it desensitises overactive nerve endings by recalibrating your pain threshold whilst enhancing mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting ATP production that combats the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Neuropathy means damaged nerve fibres send faulty signals, creating burning sensations, shooting pain, numbness, or "pins and needles" feeling. Poor microcirculation compounds the problem by starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients needed for repair. Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates the tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves need whilst removing inflammatory compounds that perpetuate pain signals.

Headaches & Migraines

Migraines involve neuroinflammation, disrupted mitochondrial function in neurons, and abnormal cerebral blood flow. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in brain tissue, boosting ATP production that supports stable neural activity. Reduced neuroinflammation helps normalise pain pathways whilst improved cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients that support healthy brain function. This addresses root mechanisms rather than just masking pain symptoms.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your perimenopause and hormonal goals, morning sessions are ideal—red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm, the foundation of hormonal regulation. For period pain, use whenever discomfort occurs, ideally daily during your cycle. For fibromyalgia and nerve pain, morning or early afternoon works well—you want time for your tissue to respond before sleep. For migraines, use preventatively every morning, or immediately when you feel one starting. Consistency matters more than timing—the best time is when you'll stick to it.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Demonstrates that RLT significantly improves pain scores, "fibro-fog," and quality of life, with approximately 95% of patients showing measurable improvements in fibromyalgia. Rheumatology International.

Shows that NIR light therapy significantly increased nerve conduction velocities and reduced pain index scores by 63.2% in neuropathy patients receiving 3 treatments per week over 6 weeks. Journal of Neurological Sciences.

A single session of light therapy to the forehead resulted in a 63.1% reduction in anxiety scores and 54.3% reduction in depression—relevant for mood support during hormonal transitions. Behavioral and Brain Functions.
