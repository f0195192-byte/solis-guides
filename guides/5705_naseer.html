Your health & wellness goals

ME/CFS & Chronic Fatigue

Your primary goal. ME/CFS (Myalgic Encephalomyelitis / Chronic Fatigue Syndrome) is a complex, multi-system condition characterised by profound, persistent fatigue that is not relieved by rest, alongside post-exertional malaise — a worsening of symptoms following physical or mental exertion. You've been managing this for 8 years, and you've described the fatigue as the most debilitating aspect of your day-to-day life.

Brain Fog & Cognitive Impairment

A hallmark symptom of ME/CFS. Brain fog refers to difficulties with memory, concentration, information processing, and mental clarity — often described as thinking through treacle. It can significantly impair daily functioning and is driven by neuroinflammation and poor cellular energy supply to the brain.

Widespread Pain — Joints, Muscles & Nerves

You've described pain across multiple areas including back and spine, knees and joints, shoulders and neck, and nerve-related pain. In ME/CFS, widespread musculoskeletal pain is common and is linked to central sensitisation, systemic inflammation, and impaired tissue repair — all driven by underlying mitochondrial dysfunction.

Fibromyalgia-type & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Fibromyalgia frequently co-exists with or overlaps ME/CFS. It involves the central nervous system becoming hypersensitive to pain signals — amplifying sensations across the whole body. Combined with the fatigue of ME/CFS, it creates a cycle of exhaustion and aching with few effective conventional treatment options.

Headaches & Migraines

Recurrent headaches and migraines are common in ME/CFS and can be driven by neuroinflammation, poor cerebral circulation, autonomic nervous system dysregulation, and orthostatic intolerance. They are often debilitating on top of an already demanding symptom load.

Autoimmune & Thyroid Support

You have autoimmune hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's), in which the immune system attacks thyroid tissue, progressively reducing its ability to produce hormones. Suboptimal thyroid function compounds the fatigue, brain fog, and pain of ME/CFS significantly, creating an overlapping symptom burden that is difficult to separate clinically.

The science
How red light therapy helps

ME/CFS & Energy Levels — Mitochondrial Support

The leading research hypothesis for ME/CFS centres on mitochondrial dysfunction — the cellular powerplants that produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), your body's energy currency, are not working efficiently. This means your cells are energy-starved regardless of how much you rest, producing the distinctive, unrelenting fatigue that defines ME/CFS.

Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in the mitochondrial respiratory chain. This stimulation dramatically increases ATP output and reduces oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function — essentially rebooting your cells' energy production capacity. A 2023 Scientific Reports study found that 660nm and 830nm light boosted mitochondrial ATP production by up to 70% in human immune cell samples. A separate study published in the Journal of Athletic Training found a 30% increase in energy efficiency and morning alertness after 14 nightly treatments.

For you specifically, Naseer, the goal is not to push through exertion but to support recovery at the cellular level — improving the baseline energy available to your body without triggering post-exertional malaise. Start with very short sessions (5–10 minutes) and build gradually, paying close attention to how your body responds in the 24–48 hours following each session.

Post-Viral Fatigue & Systemic Recovery

ME/CFS shares significant biological overlap with long COVID and post-viral fatigue syndromes — both involve persistent mitochondrial dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and immune dysregulation that continues long after the initial trigger. The mechanisms are closely related, and the research on post-viral fatigue is directly relevant to you.

A 2023 UK case study (Health Rising) documented complete symptom resolution from chronic post-viral crashes after two weeks of daily 2-minute red light treatments at 660nm. A 2024 study by Sigman et al. found that 12 treatments over 4 weeks led to significant improvements in cognitive test scores for long COVID patients using 660nm and 850nm. Red light therapy restores mitochondrial function throughout the body, reduces brain tissue inflammation causing cognitive impairment, and gives your muscles and organs the cellular fuel they need to function.

Brain Fog & Cognitive Function

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's total energy despite being just 2% of your body weight — neurons are extraordinarily energy-hungry. In ME/CFS, the brain is among the worst-affected organs by mitochondrial dysfunction. Neuroinflammation further impairs cognitive processing, creating the characteristic brain fog, memory difficulties, and word-finding problems.

Near-infrared light penetrates the skull and enhances mitochondrial function in neurons and glial cells directly. Boosted ATP production supports memory formation, information processing, and neural communication. A Harvard Medical School meta-analysis (2019) across 9,000 participants found that transcranial NIR light at 810nm significantly improved executive function and memory scores. Improved cerebral blood flow also helps the brain's waste-clearance systems work more effectively, reducing the accumulation of inflammatory metabolites that contribute to fog.

Widespread Pain — Joints, Back, Neck & Inflammation

Pain in ME/CFS is rooted in systemic inflammation and impaired tissue repair — the same mitochondrial deficiency that starves your brain of energy also compromises your muscles, joints, and connective tissues. Pro-inflammatory cytokines remain chronically elevated, sensitising nerve endings and perpetuating pain.

Red light therapy interrupts this at source. It downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. A 2018 study published in Lasers in Surgery and Medicine found that systemic levels of the inflammatory marker IL-6 were reduced by 30% after just one light session. A 2006 Pain Research Management study found a 50% reduction in chronic back pain intensity after 6 weeks. For joint-specific concerns — knees, shoulders — NIR light at 810nm penetrates to the joint capsule, stimulating chondrocyte repair and reducing synovial inflammation. A 2023 Journal of Rheumatology study in 120 participants found a 70% decrease in arthritic joint pain and 38% improvement in morning stiffness after 4 weeks.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Fibromyalgia involves central sensitisation — the nervous system becomes hyperresponsive to pain signals, turning normal sensations into widespread, amplified pain. Combined with ME/CFS-driven mitochondrial fatigue, this creates a relentless cycle of exhaustion and aching. Red light therapy addresses both mechanisms simultaneously: it desensitises overactive nerve endings, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold, whilst enhancing mitochondrial ATP production to combat the fatigue that underpins symptom severity.

A 2019 Rheumatology International study across 325 patients using 630–904nm found RLT significantly effective in improving pain scores, "fibrofog," and quality of life — with approximately 95% showing measurable improvements. A separate 2019 Brain Sciences study found that 31.6% of participants no longer met the diagnostic criteria for fibromyalgia after just 6 weeks of treatment with 660nm and 850nm light.

Headaches & Migraines

Headaches in ME/CFS are typically driven by neuroinflammation, poor cerebral circulation, and autonomic nervous system dysregulation — particularly orthostatic intolerance, which impairs blood flow to the brain when upright. NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation and improve cerebral microcirculation, addressing two of the primary drivers of chronic headaches in ME/CFS specifically.

The same Harvard Medical School research on transcranial NIR light (810nm) that showed cognitive benefits also demonstrated improved cerebral blood flow — directly relevant to headache and migraine frequency. Red light's effect on the autonomic nervous system may also help modulate the dysautonomia that worsens orthostatic intolerance and head pain in ME/CFS.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Nerve pain in ME/CFS and fibromyalgia can manifest as burning, shooting pain, numbness, or hypersensitivity — the result of damaged or dysregulated nerve fibres sending faulty signals. Poor microcirculation starves nerve tissue of the oxygen and nutrients needed for repair.

Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates the tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. A 2019 Journal of Neurological Sciences study found that NIR light at 810nm significantly increased nerve conduction velocities and reduced pain index scores by 63.2% over 6 weeks. A 2004 Diabetes Care study in 240 patients found a 40% improvement in sensation and pain scores after 4 weeks of treatment.

Autoimmune Thyroid & Hashimoto's Support

Your autoimmune hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's) means your immune system is actively attacking your thyroid tissue. Even with levothyroxine managing your hormone levels, ongoing autoimmune activity sustains systemic inflammation that compounds ME/CFS fatigue, brain fog, and pain. Reducing that autoimmune burden matters as much as the hormone replacement itself.

Because the thyroid sits at the surface of the neck, it is one of the most accessible organs for red light therapy. A 2018 Lasers in Surgery and Medicine study in 15 Hashimoto's patients found a 600-point drop (~50%) in anti-TPO antibodies — the key marker of autoimmune thyroid attack — after just 4 weeks of twice-weekly 830nm treatments. A 2013 University of São Paulo study found 47% of hypothyroid patients were able to completely discontinue their medication after 5 weeks of treatment. Alongside your levothyroxine, RLT may support reduced antibody load and better thyroid cell function — always discuss any medication changes with your GP.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Given your ME/CFS and fatigue goals, morning is the most beneficial time to use your SOLO 2.0. Morning red and NIR light mirrors natural sunrise wavelengths, supports your circadian rhythm, and delivers mitochondrial stimulation when your body is preparing for the day — which is particularly important for managing the energy deficits of ME/CFS. Morning sessions also align with the research showing the strongest effects on alertness, cognitive function, and energy levels. If mornings are too difficult on certain days due to symptom severity, early afternoon is a reasonable alternative. Avoid sessions in the 2 hours before bed, as the stimulating mitochondrial effect can interfere with sleep onset. Consistency in timing matters more than perfection — the same time each day, even a shorter session, is better than irregular longer ones.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
