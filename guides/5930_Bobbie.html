The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Chronic pain is driven by pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding damaged tissues and sensitising nerve endings. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep into spinal tissue, joints, and muscle layers to downregulate these inflammatory signals whilst increasing blood flow. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for tissue repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. Your body's natural pain-relieving mechanisms activate more efficiently, providing measurable relief without reliance on medication.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Arthritis involves both cartilage breakdown and inflammatory compounds accumulating in joint fluid. Red light penetrates the joint capsule, reducing inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes—the cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen that support tissue healing and break inflammatory cycles preventing repair. The result is reduced morning stiffness, improved mobility, and decreased pain during movement—particularly significant for osteoarthritis where your body can finally access its natural healing mechanisms.

Mood, Energy & Mental Clarity

Depression and seasonal mood changes involve both mitochondrial dysfunction and neurotransmitter imbalance. Red and NIR light enhances mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production. Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. This supports serotonin synthesis and circadian rhythm regulation—particularly important for seasonal patterns—resulting in measurably improved mood, reduced anxiety, and enhanced mental clarity within days to weeks.

Skin Improvement & Tone

Uneven skin tone stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow. Red light stimulates fibroblasts for collagen production whilst improving microcirculation, boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to skin layers. This accelerates cellular turnover, clears old pigmented cells, and promotes fresh cell regeneration. The result is more even tone, improved brightness, and visibly healthier skin texture over 4-6 weeks.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

Timing enhances specific benefits. For pain relief and joint function, morning or early afternoon sessions give your tissue time to respond before activity—or evening sessions allow recovery overnight. For mood and energy, morning sessions are ideal, mirroring natural sunrise wavelengths and supporting your circadian rhythm and serotonin production. For seasonal mood support, consistency throughout the day is most important. The best time is one you'll stick to consistently—aim for the same time daily when possible.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs emitting near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface as the red lights you can see. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming all LEDs are active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spine), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, mood), a slightly greater distance can allow even coverage.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a pain protocol looks different to a skin protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with a full-power panel like the SURGE 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
