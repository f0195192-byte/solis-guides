The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Red light therapy tackles eczema on multiple fronts. First, it accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to rebuild your protective "shield," helping your skin retain moisture more effectively. Second, it calms overactive nerve endings that trigger the relentless itch sensation, breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. Finally, red and near-infrared wavelengths reduce inflammatory cytokines driving the condition, creating a calmer skin environment. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching—addressing the root dysfunction rather than just suppressing symptoms with steroids that disrupt your skin's long-term health.

Psoriasis

Psoriasis is an autoimmune condition where your skin cells go into overdrive, multiplying at 10 times the normal rate. Red light therapy normalises this dysregulated proliferation, essentially telling overactive cells to slow down to a healthy growth rate. Simultaneously, it reduces the infiltration of inflammatory T-cells—the immune cells driving the problem in the first place. This addresses the autoimmune dysfunction itself rather than just managing plaques. Clinical studies show significant clearance of scaly plaques that have previously resisted conventional therapies, with many patients seeing flattening within 4 weeks.

Muscle Recovery & Tension

Near-infrared light penetrates deep into muscle tissue, enhancing ATP production in muscle cells and giving them more energy to repair and recover. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients while removing inflammatory compounds that perpetuate tension. Red light simultaneously reduces inflammatory response in muscle tissue. The result is reduced muscle soreness, improved recovery, and relief from chronic tension patterns—particularly valuable when muscle tension accompanies skin conditions like eczema and psoriasis.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

For eczema and psoriasis, morning or early afternoon sessions work best—this allows your skin time to respond during waking hours when you can monitor any reactions. For muscle tension, any time works, though pairing it with your skin protocols is efficient. Avoid sessions immediately before bed, as the light energy might be stimulating. Consistency matters more than timing, so choose a time you'll stick to. Matthew, aim for the same time each session to build it into your routine and maximise results.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 108 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your skin and muscle tissue where red light alone cannot reach. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
