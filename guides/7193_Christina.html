The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Pain is fundamentally an inflammatory response. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood painful tissues, restricting blood flow and sensitising nerve endings. Red and near-infrared light downregulates these pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst simultaneously increasing blood flow to damaged areas. This dual mechanism delivers oxygen and nutrients needed for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste perpetuating pain. Enhanced circulation also activates your body's natural pain-relieving mechanisms, offering measurable relief without relying solely on medication.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Fibromyalgia involves central nervous system sensitisation—your brain amplifies normal signals into pain—combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing crushing fatigue. Red light addresses both. It desensitises overactive nerve endings, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold, whilst directly enhancing mitochondrial ATP production throughout your entire body. This boosts cellular energy that combats fatigue whilst simultaneously reducing pain intensity. Clinical studies show approximately 95% of fibromyalgia patients experience measurable improvements in pain, fatigue, and quality of life.

Menopause & Hormonal Balance

Surgical menopause causes abrupt hormonal shifts affecting your hypothalamus (brain's temperature regulation centre), autonomic nervous system, and neurotransmitter balance—driving hot flushes, mood changes, and sleep disruption. Red light stimulates beta-endorphin release (your brain's natural mood-lifters) whilst modulating autonomic nervous system function, helping your regulatory centres adapt more smoothly. Enhanced cerebral blood flow supports cognitive clarity whilst improved mitochondrial function in hormone-producing tissues helps maintain balance. This supports your body's transition without additional hormonal disruption.

Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis involves tissue outside your uterus creating chronic inflammation and pain. Near-infrared light penetrates pelvic tissue to reduce inflammatory markers like IL-6 whilst modulating immune responses in affected areas. This provides non-pharmacological pain relief that complements your current care. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients supporting natural healing processes whilst reduced inflammation helps break the pain cycle that perpetuates tissue damage and sensitisation.

Energy & Mitochondrial Support

Chronic fatigue reflects mitochondrial dysfunction—your cellular powerplants aren't producing sufficient ATP (the energy currency fuelling every biological process). Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway, dramatically increasing ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. This means your cells simply have more fuel to power recovery, daily function, and healing processes that are often compromised by fatigue.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing matters. Morning sessions (within 1–2 hours of waking) support energy and hormonal balance by aligning with natural circadian rhythm—particularly beneficial for menopause symptoms and fibromyalgia-related fatigue. For acute pain flare-ups, treat when pain peaks. For pelvic pain, daily sessions during your cycle work best, timed around when discomfort typically arrives. Evening sessions 1–2 hours before bed can help menopause symptoms and support sleep quality without disrupting circadian rhythm. The best time is ultimately the one you'll stick to consistently—consistency matters more than timing.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. These invisible wavelengths penetrate deepest into your tissues and are working just as powerfully beneath the surface for pain relief and mitochondrial support. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
