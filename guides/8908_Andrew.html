The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Pain originates from your body's inflammatory response to tissue damage. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood affected areas, creating swelling, restricting blood flow, and sensitising nerve endings. Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep into joints and muscle tissue where your knee and sports injuries are located. These wavelengths work by downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst simultaneously increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. Your body's natural pain-relieving and healing mechanisms activate more efficiently, reducing pain intensity measurably without medication.

Fat Loss & Body Composition

Stubborn fat persists because fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides—your body struggles to access and burn them, particularly in problem areas like the abdomen. Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. This mobilised fat then becomes accessible to your body for burning during exercise. When combined with exercise and nutrition, your body can preferentially access and burn fat from treated areas instead of it remaining locked away. Applied to specific zones like your abdomen, red light enables enhanced fat loss from those exact locations when paired with movement and proper diet.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your pain management goal, consistency matters most—use the panel daily at whatever time fits your routine best. Many users prefer morning or early afternoon sessions to manage pain throughout the day. For your fat loss goal, timing is more strategic: use the panel 10–15 minutes immediately before exercise or activity. This maximises the fat mobilisation window and ensures your body burns the mobilised fat during your workout. The best schedule combines daily knee treatment at any convenient time, plus pre-exercise abdominal sessions 3–4 times per week.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your knee joint and abdominal tissue. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
