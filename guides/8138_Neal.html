The science
How red light therapy helps

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss in male pattern baldness occurs because follicles lose their growth phase and enter dormancy. Red light therapy wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, kickstarting them back into active growth mode and extending the duration of this growth phase. The near-infrared wavelengths penetrate your scalp to trigger nitric oxide release in follicle cells, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient and oxygen delivery to each follicle. This dual mechanism — reactivating dormant follicles whilst improving their nutrient supply — leads to measurable increases in hair density, thicker individual strands, and a healthier scalp environment, without medications or their associated side effects.

Pain Relief & Arthritis

Arthritis means protective cartilage breaks down whilst inflammatory compounds accumulate in joint fluid. Thirty-five years of degenerative changes have reduced your range of motion and amplified pain, particularly with movement. Red light penetrates deep into your joint capsules, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate pain cycles whilst stimulating chondrocytes — the cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial oxygen and nutrients supporting this healing process. Near-infrared wavelengths are particularly effective for deep tissue damage, reaching structures conventional therapies cannot. The result is reduced morning stiffness, improved mobility, and decreased pain during movement, addressing the root inflammatory problem rather than just masking symptoms.

Sleep Quality & Rest

Poor sleep comes from circadian dysregulation — your body's internal clock no longer properly signals rest-wake timing, and evening cortisol remains elevated when it should be declining. Red light therapy works with your biology to reset this cycle. Unlike blue light from screens (which suppresses melatonin by up to 85%), red and near-infrared wavelengths stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally. Evening red light exposure signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down, whilst enhanced mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating circadian rhythms means these systems operate more efficiently. The result is faster sleep onset, improved sleep depth, longer restorative phases, and better morning alertness.

Eye Health & Near Vision

Your retina contains some of the most metabolically demanding cells in your body — photoreceptors require enormous ATP to convert light into neural signals. As you age, mitochondrial decline in retinal cells reduces your ability to focus at close distances (presbyopia) whilst oxidative stress damages light-sensitive tissue. Red light penetrates to your retina, enhancing mitochondrial function in photoreceptors directly. This boosts cellular energy for demanding vision processes whilst reducing oxidative stress that accelerates age-related vision decline. The result is slowed progression of presbyopia, improved near-vision clarity, and reduced age-related macular degeneration risk through enhanced cellular function rather than decline.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific goals, Neal, timing matters. For hair growth, morning or early afternoon is ideal — consistency matters more than timing. For neck and back pain, morning before activity or evening after work both work well — daily use is more important than the specific time. For sleep support, use your SOLO 2.0 1–2 hours before bed — this timing is critical for melatonin signalling. For eye health, use it immediately after waking to align with natural sunrise. Your sleep goal suggests evening sessions matter most, so prioritise that window. The best overall time is the one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
