The science
How red light therapy helps

Female Fertility & Egg Quality

Female fertility fundamentally depends on egg quality—a mitochondrial issue. Each egg requires enormous ATP for maturation, fertilisation, and embryo development. As women age, mitochondrial function in eggs naturally declines, reducing quality and increasing miscarriage risk. Near-infrared light penetrates ovarian tissue to directly enhance mitochondrial function in developing eggs. Boosted ATP gives oocytes energy for demanding maturation processes whilst improved mitochondrial health supports better chromosome organisation and higher-quality embryos. Enhanced blood flow supports optimal hormone production and uterine lining development. By starting now, Emily, you're optimising cellular foundations for 2029.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Period pain stems from excessive prostaglandins and inflammatory compounds in the uterine lining, whilst irregular cycles reflect hormonal dysregulation and poor endometrial function. Red light reduces inflammatory markers like IL-6 and modulates immune responses in pelvic tissue, providing measurable pain relief. By enhancing blood flow to reproductive organs and supporting mitochondrial function in hormone-producing glands, red light helps restore natural endocrine balance, resulting in more regular cycles and reduced menstrual discomfort without pharmaceutical intervention.

Muscle Recovery & Athletic Performance

Intense circus performance creates micro-tears in muscle fibres (particularly hips and shoulders) and metabolic waste accumulation causing delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS). Red light therapy accelerates tissue repair by enhancing ATP production in muscle cells, giving damaged fibres energy to rebuild efficiently. Improved circulation removes inflammatory compounds whilst reduced inflammation speeds recovery windows. The result is shorter downtime between performances, reduced chronic injury pain, and better athletic endurance—critical for your demanding physical practice.

Hair Health & Thickness

Hair loss and thinning happen when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of growth phase, and when poor circulation means your scalp isn't getting the nutrients it needs. For hair hanging practitioners, scalp stress from tension and reduced blood flow compounds this problem. Red light therapy wakes up stem cells in hair follicles, kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase. It triggers nitric oxide release that dilates blood vessels, dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle whilst reducing scalp inflammation from tension stress. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker strands, and a healthier scalp environment.

Acne & Clear Skin

Acne involves excessive oil production, bacterial colonisation, and deep inflammation causing painful, scarring breakouts. Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where acne starts, downregulating excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses that create angry, painful spots. Unlike harsh topicals that strip skin, red light addresses inflammation without damage, allowing your skin to regulate itself properly. The result is faster healing, reduced scarring, and fewer new breakouts.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For fertility and hormone support, evening sessions (1–2 hours before bed) work well—they support natural melatonin production and align with your body's reproductive rhythms. For muscle recovery, post-workout is ideal—within 1 hour of finishing your circus practice. For acne and skin concerns, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths. For hair health, any consistent time works. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to, and stacking multiple protocols (e.g., fertility + acne in one evening session) maximises efficiency.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, reproductive organs), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, acne), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a fertility or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during close facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
