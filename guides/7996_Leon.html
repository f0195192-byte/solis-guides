The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis) & TSW

Eczema and TSW are fundamentally skin barrier problems: your epidermis can't retain moisture, inflammatory signals run unchecked, and hypersensitive nerve endings drive relentless itching. Red light therapy works at multiple levels. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to synthesise structural proteins that rebuild your protective "shield." Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings that misinterpret normal sensations as intense itching, directly breaking the itch-scratch cycle. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reduce inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-alpha driving your flares. Clinical trials show 79% of patients with eczema reported reduced itching, with 47% experiencing significant severity reduction—critical for TSW recovery where conventional treatments often worsen the condition.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation forms when melanin-laden cells remain in your skin after flaring. Poor circulation means these pigmented cells aren't efficiently cleared. Red light therapy improves microcirculation dramatically, delivering more oxygen and nutrients to accelerate cellular turnover. The 630nm wavelength in particular reduces inflammatory cytokines responsible for ongoing pigmentation, while enhanced blood flow ensures dead, pigmented cells are replaced quickly by fresh, clear ones. Over time, this creates visibly more even, radiant skin tone as the damage from years of flaring gradually resolves.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Lichenification and scar tissue form when your body rushes to heal, laying down collagen chaotically instead of in the organised patterns of healthy skin. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition while preventing excessive fibrosis. It stimulates fibroblasts to rebuild skin structure properly, and for existing scars, boosts collagen production to gradually "lift" and level thickened areas. Enhanced cellular ATP gives your repair cells energy to work efficiently, accelerating the transition from damaged, thickened skin to normalised texture. Studies show wounds heal 40% faster with red light therapy, and scar induration (hardness) reduces by up to 57.9%.

Sleep Quality & Restless Nights

Chronic itching fragments your sleep, preventing the deep restorative phases your body desperately needs. Red light therapy addresses this directly. Evening red light exposure stimulates your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally—unlike blue light from screens that suppresses it. Simultaneously, it enhances mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating your circadian rhythm, helping your sleep-wake cycle stabilise. By reducing nocturnal itching through calmed nerve endings and reduced inflammation, your sleep architecture improves, allowing you to achieve deeper, more restorative sleep phases. Studies show 79% improvement in sleep latency and 13% improvement in overall sleep quality after just two weeks.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin healing goals, morning sessions are ideal—red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports circadian alignment. However, your sleep support protocol is absolutely essential at 1–2 hours before bed; this evening timing naturally triggers melatonin production. For your barrier repair and scar protocols, any time during the day works, though consistency matters more than timing. The critical rule: use your sleep protocol every evening for maximum impact on restless nights. Morning sessions can vary, but don't skip the evening protocol—that's where your sleep quality will transform.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your skin and tissue. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active. These invisible wavelengths are doing crucial work on inflammation and barrier repair that you simply can't see, but your skin absolutely can feel.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
