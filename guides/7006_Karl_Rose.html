The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Soreness

Intense physical work creates micro-tears in muscle fibres whilst metabolic waste like lactic acid accumulates, causing delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS). Red and near-infrared light penetrate deep into muscle tissue, directly stimulating mitochondrial ATP production — the cellular energy your muscles need to repair those micro-tears efficiently. Enhanced ATP output accelerates protein synthesis for muscle rebuilding whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds and metabolic waste products. The result is dramatically reduced soreness, faster recovery between physical demands, and better resilience for sustained high-intensity work.

Pain Relief & Spinal Health

Spinal disc degeneration involves both tissue damage and chronic inflammation in the vertebral discs and surrounding structures. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach disc tissue and nerve roots, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate pain cycles. Simultaneously, enhanced mitochondrial function gives damaged spinal tissues energy for repair and regeneration. Improved blood flow to the spine delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst reducing swelling that compresses nerves. This addresses pain at its source rather than masking it, supporting genuine tissue healing whilst reducing nerve-related symptoms like radiating pain or tingling.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

For muscle recovery, post-activity or post-work sessions are ideal — your muscles are primed to respond to the light's repair signals. Apply within 1 hour of intense physical work for best results. For spinal pain management, consistency matters more than timing, though morning sessions on cervical spine and evening sessions on lumbar spine align well with your daily activity and recovery cycles. Morning treatments also prepare your spine for the physical demands of your work ahead.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during close treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or positioning the panel slightly further away can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
