The science
How red light therapy helps

Fat Loss & Body Composition

Red and near-infrared light creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes through a process called lipophoresis, causing them to release stored triglycerides into your bloodstream where they become available as fuel. Combined with exercise, your body can now access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. Applied to specific zones before training, red light helps preferentially release fat from those exact locations. Research shows that combining red light therapy with exercise leads to 444% more fat loss in treated areas compared to exercise alone, whilst also improving metabolic efficiency through enhanced mitochondrial ATP production in muscle tissue.

Cellulite Appearance

Cellulite forms when subcutaneous fat protrudes through weakened collagen structures, creating dimpling. Red light stimulates fibroblasts to synthesise new, organised collagen fibres, strengthening the structural matrix beneath skin. Simultaneously, enhanced circulation improves microvascular blood flow to cellulite-affected areas, delivering oxygen and nutrients that support tissue remodelling. Over time, this reinforced collagen structure and improved tissue health reduce the visible dimpling characteristic of cellulite. The effect is gradual but measurable—addressing the root structural issue rather than temporary topical approaches.

Perimenopause Support

Perimenopause involves dysregulation of your hypothalamus—the brain's temperature control centre—creating vasomotor instability. Red light therapy stimulates beta-endorphin release (natural mood-lifters) whilst modulating autonomic nervous system function, helping your brain adapt to declining oestrogen more smoothly. Enhanced mitochondrial function supports the energy production your body demands during hormonal transition, whilst improved cerebral blood flow helps cognitive symptoms like brain fog. By working with your body's natural hormonal systems rather than against them, red light complements HRT and testosterone support, reducing symptom severity and improving overall quality of life during this transition.

Knee Pain Management

Chronic knee pain stems from inflammatory compounds accumulating in joint synovial fluid whilst protective cartilage degrades. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst stimulating chondrocytes—the cells responsible for cartilage repair and maintenance. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients essential for this repair process, whilst reduced inflammation breaks the cycle that prevents healing. Your SUPREME 2.0's broader wavelength coverage paired with your existing knee-specific treatments creates synergistic support: targeted NIR from your bandage reaches deep, whilst the full-spectrum SUPREME addresses systemic inflammation contributing to ongoing pain. Research shows 70% reduction in arthritic joint pain after consistent treatment.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your goals, timing is strategic. For fat loss, use your SUPREME 30 minutes before exercise to mobilise stored fat most effectively—timing around your workout trumps time of day. For perimenopause support, morning sessions support circadian rhythm regulation and stable cortisol throughout the day, helping manage hormonal fluctuations. For sleep quality and night-time hot flush reduction, use 2 hours before bed. For your knees, morning and post-activity sessions target when inflammation peaks. Consistency matters more than perfect timing—the best protocol is one you'll sustain. If you must choose one time, morning sessions support energy, mood, and hormone stability most broadly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active. These invisible wavelengths are what penetrate deepest into your tissues for fat mobilisation, inflammation reduction, and joint repair.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the SUPREME's power. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
