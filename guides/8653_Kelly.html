The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief — Back & Spine Pain

Back pain results from pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding tissues, restricting blood flow and creating chronic discomfort. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep into spinal muscles and tissues, downregulating these inflammatory compounds whilst dramatically increasing blood flow. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste. NIR wavelengths reach spinal tissues where chronic pain originates, effectively interrupting the pain cycle and supporting tissue healing.

Pain Relief — Shoulder & Neck Pain

Shoulder and neck tension involves tight muscles, inflammation, and restricted blood flow that creates characteristic stiffness and discomfort. Red light therapy enhances microcirculation in these areas whilst reducing inflammatory markers. The 660nm wavelength is particularly effective for surface-level muscle tension, whilst deeper NIR penetration reaches underlying tissue damage. Enhanced ATP production in muscle cells gives tissues energy to relax and repair, breaking the tension cycle.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Fibromyalgia involves central nervous system hypersensitivity amplifying pain signals, combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing severe fatigue. Red light therapy addresses both core issues simultaneously. It desensitises overactive nerve endings that misinterpret signals as pain, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold. Simultaneously, it enhances mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting cellular ATP production that combats the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia. This dual mechanism provides relief where conventional treatments typically offer only partial symptom management.

Headaches & Migraines

Migraines and tension headaches involve vascular dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and muscle tension in the scalp and neck. Red light therapy enhances blood flow to the brain whilst reducing neuroinflammation that triggers migraines. NIR wavelengths penetrate deeply to relax tight cervical muscles whilst reducing inflammatory compounds driving pain. Enhanced mitochondrial function in brain cells supports your natural pain-relief mechanisms, reducing both headache frequency and intensity without pharmaceutical side effects.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss occurs when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of the growth phase, and poor circulation means your scalp isn't receiving the nutrients needed for healthy growth. Red light therapy tackles both problems. First, it wakes up the stem cells in your hair follicles, kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase. Second, it triggers the release of nitric oxide in your scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker individual strands, and a healthier scalp environment.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For pain relief and recovery, morning or early afternoon sessions give your tissue time to respond and heal before further activity. For migraines, many users find using the panel at the first sign of a headache provides rapid relief. For sleep quality and energy support, use earlier in the day rather than close to bedtime to support your natural circadian rhythm. For hair growth, any consistent time works — the best time is the one you'll stick to reliably, as hair growth benefits require consistent, long-term use.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial or scalp treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
