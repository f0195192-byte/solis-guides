The science
How red light therapy helps

Post-Surgical Pain & Recovery

Post-surgical inflammation is your body's necessary response to tissue damage, but excessive inflammation becomes the primary driver of pain and slowed healing. Red and near-infrared light penetrate deep surgical tissue to reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in damaged cells. This gives your tissues the energy they need to repair efficiently. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients to the surgical site whilst removing inflammatory waste, directly reducing pain intensity and accelerating the healing timeline. Research shows that consistent red light therapy applied immediately post-surgery can reduce inflammation markers by 30% after just one session, and significantly accelerate tissue closure and functional recovery.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Surgical trauma damages nerve fibres, creating abnormal pain signals—burning, tingling, or shooting sensations—due to both direct nerve injury and poor microcirculation around damaged nerves. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to stimulate regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release, which dilates blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves desperately need for repair whilst removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating pain signals. Clinical studies show 40% improvement in foot sensation and pain scores within 4 weeks, and 63% reduction in pain index scores within 6 weeks of consistent treatment—particularly significant for post-surgical neuropathy where conventional pain management offers only temporary relief.

Muscle Recovery & Mobility

Surgical swelling restricts movement and limits physiotherapy progress by creating fluid retention around damaged tissue. Red light therapy enhances ATP production in muscle and connective tissue, giving cells more energy to repair micro-damage and restore normal function. It simultaneously reduces inflammatory response and fluid retention around muscle tissue, allowing actual structural recovery to show through. Improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds whilst enhancing the body's natural clearance systems. Studies show 50% reduction in post-exercise muscle soreness markers and measurable improvements in swelling reduction when applied consistently—allowing you to progress through physiotherapy with less pain and faster functional restoration.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For post-surgical recovery and pain relief, the best time is morning or early afternoon, giving your tissue time to respond before activity. Morning sessions also support your body's natural healing rhythms. If you're combining red light therapy with physiotherapy, apply it immediately before or after your rehab session when your tissues are primed for healing. Avoid very late evening use for pain-focused goals, as it may interfere with sleep. Consistency matters more than timing—use it when you can stick to a schedule.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into surgical tissue where red light alone cannot reach. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
