The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red light activates fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin, your skin's foundational support structure. By boosting cellular ATP energy, red light therapy gives these cells fuel to repair damaged collagen fibres and build new ones efficiently. The result is measurably reduced wrinkle depth, improved skin firmness, and restored youthful texture. Studies show 90% of participants experienced reduced wrinkle depth after just 8 sessions, with 31.6% decreases in wrinkle volume after 10 treatments.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Red light therapy addresses the root cause: sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow. It stimulates new blood vessel formation, dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to your skin's surface. With enhanced circulation, old pigmented cells from sun damage get cleared away faster and replaced by fresh, clear cells. The 630nm wavelength specifically reduces inflammatory cytokines driving redness and blotchiness, resulting in visibly more even tone and brightness.

Skin Texture & Glow

Red and NIR light work synergistically to accelerate your skin's natural renewal cycle. Red wavelengths stimulate surface cell turnover whilst NIR penetrates deeper to enhance mitochondrial function throughout skin layers. Enhanced cellular energy means faster repair, improved circulation delivers more nutrients, and reduced inflammation creates that luminous, refined appearance. The result is smoother, more radiant skin with noticeably improved texture clarity.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen and elastin production declines with age as fibroblasts lose energy efficiency. Red light directly stimulates these cells by enhancing cytochrome c oxidase in mitochondria, boosting ATP production. This gives fibroblasts the energy to actively synthesise new collagen and elastin rather than simply maintaining existing structures. Increased collagen means restored skin firmness and elasticity — genuine structural improvement, not temporary plumping effects.

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

SAD develops when reduced winter daylight disrupts neurotransmitter production and circadian rhythm regulation. Red light therapy enhances mitochondrial function in mood-regulating brain regions, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter synthesis. It also modulates serotonin production and supports pineal gland function for natural melatonin regulation. Clinical studies show a single session of NIR light therapy reduced anxiety scores by 63% and depression by 54%, with significant mood improvements in 3-4 weeks of consistent use.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin goals, morning or early afternoon sessions work beautifully — red light enhances natural skin renewal cycles and provides a confidence boost at the start of your day. For your SAD support, morning is ideal — the natural red light mirrors sunrise and recalibrates your circadian rhythm, supporting steady mood and energy throughout the day. Avoid sessions 2+ hours before bed if you're sensitive, as the brightness could affect sleep. Consistency matters more than timing — use the device when you'll commit to it regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and means they're working perfectly. The NIR lights are delivering powerful therapeutic effects beneath your skin's surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
