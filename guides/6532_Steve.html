The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Depression and anxiety involve disrupted neurotransmitter balance and neuroinflammation in brain regions controlling mood. Red and near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production like serotonin and dopamine. Simultaneously, NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways whilst enhancing cerebral blood flow, delivering oxygen and nutrients crucial for emotional stability. In long COVID, where persistent viral effects damage mitochondrial function, this cellular energy boost is particularly valuable for restoring mood regulation.

Brain Fog & Mental Clarity

Brain fog in long COVID stems from mitochondrial dysfunction in neurons and glial cells—your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of your weight, making it incredibly vulnerable to energy deficits. Near-infrared light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway, dramatically increasing ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress damaging mitochondrial function. Boosted ATP production supports memory formation, information processing, and neural communication. Enhanced energy helps your brain's clearance systems remove metabolic waste whilst improved cerebral blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients, lifting cognitive fog and restoring clarity.

Energy & Motivation

Chronic fatigue in long COVID reflects persistent mitochondrial dysfunction. Your mitochondria are cellular powerplants producing ATP, the energy currency fuelling every biological process. When mitochondrial efficiency declines due to viral damage, ATP production drops catastrophically and you feel exhausted regardless of rest. Red and NIR light directly stimulates cytochrome c oxidase, dramatically increasing ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that perpetuates mitochondrial damage. This cellular energy restoration is foundational—without it, motivation, physical capacity, and cognitive function remain compromised. Better-functioning mitochondria mean more fuel for everything your body and mind need to do.

Sleep Quality & Chronic Fatigue

Long COVID disrupts sleep through multiple mechanisms: neuroinflammation affecting sleep-wake regulation, mitochondrial dysfunction reducing energy for proper sleep cycles, and circadian rhythm dysregulation. Red and NIR light stimulates your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating sleep cycles. Evening red light exposure signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down. Simultaneously, boosted cellular energy means your sleep regulatory systems function efficiently. Better sleep combined with mitochondrial restoration creates a powerful feedback loop—improved sleep supports further mitochondrial recovery whilst better mitochondrial function enables deeper, more restorative sleep.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your mood and energy goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, maximising the cognitive and mood-lifting effects. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. For chronic fatigue recovery like yours, consider both a morning session for energy boost and an evening session for sleep preparation. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to, but morning sessions are particularly valuable for long COVID as they help reset your body's energy and mood systems aligned with natural daylight.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with a full-size panel like the SUPREME 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
