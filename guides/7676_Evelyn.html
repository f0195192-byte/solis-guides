The science
How red light therapy helps

Sleep Quality & Circadian Regulation

Your sleep-wake cycle is controlled by your circadian rhythm — a biological clock sensitive to light. Modern life disrupts this: blue light from screens suppresses melatonin, confusing your brain about the time of day. Red and near-infrared light work differently. They stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in brain cells that regulate sleep. When you use red light in the evening (not blue light from screens), you're signalling "sunset" to your brain. This initiates natural wind-down, improves sleep onset, and deepens restorative sleep phases. Enhanced cellular energy means your brain's sleep-regulatory systems function optimally, breaking the cycle of waking through the night.

Anxiety & Mood Support

Anxiety involves disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production like serotonin and GABA (your brain's calming neurotransmitter). Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate your skull to reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. The result is measurably reduced anxiety, improved emotional resilience, and better stress management — particularly important because anxiety often perpetuates sleep problems.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Period pain and irregular cycles reflect hormonal dysregulation and pelvic inflammation. Near-infrared light penetrates reproductive tissue to reduce inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing blood flow to reproductive organs. This improves hormone production and uterine function. Enhanced mitochondrial ATP in reproductive cells helps them respond to hormonal signals more efficiently, supporting more regular cycles and less pain. Red light also helps regulate circadian rhythms, which deeply influence reproductive hormone timing — essentially "resetting" the clock that coordinates your cycle.

Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis causes tissue growth outside the uterus, creating chronic inflammatory pain. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep pelvic tissue to directly reduce inflammatory markers like IL-6 whilst modulating immune responses in pelvic tissue. This reduces pain without the side effects of pharmaceutical treatments. Enhanced cellular energy supports tissue repair whilst improved circulation helps remove inflammatory compounds perpetuating the pain cycle. For many women, red light therapy provides genuine relief where conventional treatments offer limited effectiveness.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Timing is everything for your goals, Evelyn. For sleep, use your SOLO 2.0 in the evening 1–2 hours before bed — this signals "sunset" to your brain and promotes natural melatonin production. For anxiety and mood, morning sessions work best, mirroring natural sunrise and setting your circadian rhythm for the whole day. For period pain, daytime sessions work any time. The key is consistency: pick your times and stick to them so your body learns to expect the light at those moments.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
