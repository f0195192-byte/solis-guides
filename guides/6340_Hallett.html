The science
How red light therapy helps

Back & Spine Pain

Back pain is driven by inflammation in spinal tissues and reduced blood flow delivering oxygen and nutrients needed for repair. Near-infrared light penetrates deep into spinal tissues, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing ATP production in cells. Improved mitochondrial function means tissues have energy to heal. Enhanced circulation flushes out inflammatory compounds that perpetuate pain cycles, allowing your spine to rebuild and stabilise.

Knee & Joint Pain

Joint pain results from cartilage breakdown and inflammatory compounds accumulating in synovial fluid. Red light reaches the joint capsule, reducing inflammatory markers whilst stimulating chondrocytes (cartilage repair cells). Improved blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients essential for tissue recovery. Unlike pain medications that mask symptoms, this addresses the root inflammatory and tissue damage that causes pain, allowing actual repair rather than temporary relief.

Shoulder & Neck Pain

Neck and shoulder pain typically involves muscle tension, inflammation, and reduced circulation. Red and NIR light penetrate muscle and connective tissue layers, reducing inflammatory response whilst improving blood flow. Enhanced cellular ATP means your muscles can relax and recover more efficiently. This addresses both acute tension and the chronic inflammation that makes pain persist long after initial injury.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Neuropathy involves damaged nerve fibres sending faulty pain signals combined with poor microcirculation starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients. Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers nutrients nerves need whilst removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating pain signals, reducing burning and tingling sensations and improving overall nerve function.

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Fine lines form as collagen and elastin production slow with age, collapsing the skin's support structure. Red light activates fibroblasts—the cells that build collagen—providing them ATP energy to produce new collagen fibres efficiently. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers nutrients to skin cells whilst accelerating cell turnover. The result is genuine structural improvement—reduced wrinkle depth and improved skin firmness from within, not temporary surface effects.

Acne & Breakouts

Acne involves overactive oil glands, bacterial colonisation, and deep inflammation creating painful breakouts and scarring. Red light penetrates to sebaceous glands to downregulate excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses. Unlike harsh topicals that strip skin or antibiotics that disrupt your microbiome, red light addresses inflammation without causing damage, supporting your skin's natural healing and regulatory capacity.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For pain relief and recovery goals, morning or early afternoon sessions give your tissues time to respond and repair throughout the day. For skin goals, morning sessions before makeup allow maximum absorption. Consistency matters most—the best time is the one you'll use daily. If you're managing chronic pain, daily use requires finding a convenient slot in your routine you can maintain long-term.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spine), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
