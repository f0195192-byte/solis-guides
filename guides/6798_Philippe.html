The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Post-Workout Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres and metabolic waste accumulation (lactic acid), causing delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24–48 hours post-workout. Red and NIR light therapy intervenes by reducing markers of muscle damage whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds. The result is shorter recovery windows, reduced soreness intensity, and better performance gains from your training.

Injury Recovery & Rehabilitation

Tissue repair depends on sufficient cellular energy and well-regulated inflammation. Red light penetrates deep to reach injured tissues, enhancing mitochondrial function and ATP production — giving repair cells the fuel they need. Simultaneously, it downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines that can delay healing whilst stimulating growth factors that accelerate tissue regeneration. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients crucial for rebuilding damaged tissue. This addresses the root cellular dysfunction rather than masking pain, supporting genuine recovery.

Muscle Tension & Tightness

Muscle tension originates from sustained contraction, poor circulation, and inflammatory accumulation in fascia and muscle tissue. Red and NIR wavelengths reach deep muscle layers, improving mitochondrial function and triggering vasodilation through nitric oxide release. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory compounds and metabolic waste. The result is improved muscle elasticity, reduced stiffness, and better range of motion — restoring proper muscle function and comfort.

Anti-Ageing & Skin Rejuvenation

Collagen and elastin production decline with age as fibroblasts lose energy and efficiency. Red light activates these cells, supercharging them with ATP and stimulating both collagen synthesis and elastin production. This directly reduces fine line appearance, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. The result is measurably smoother skin with visible reduction in wrinkle depth.

Skin Texture, Glow & Radiance

Dull, textured skin results from sluggish cellular turnover and poor blood flow. Red light therapy stimulates angiogenesis—the formation of new blood vessels in your skin—dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery. With better circulation, your cells have the energy they need to regenerate faster. Old, damaged cells are cleared quickly and replaced by fresh ones. The result is visibly improved skin tone, enhanced brightness, and that characteristic luminous glow that comes from optimal cellular turnover and healthy microcirculation.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis STUDIO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day. For muscle recovery, immediately after training is ideal — your muscles are primed for repair and the therapy enhances that natural window. For injury rehabilitation and tension relief, morning or early afternoon works well when tissues have time to respond. For skin and anti-ageing benefits, consistency matters more than timing. Morning sessions align with your natural circadian rhythm. The best time is ultimately the one you'll use consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your STUDIO 2.0 has 480 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with a large device like the STUDIO 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
