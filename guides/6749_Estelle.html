The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Post-Workout Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres and accumulates metabolic waste like lactic acid—this causes delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24-48 hours post-workout and limits training frequency. Red light therapy intervenes directly in this cycle. Applied before or after training, it reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds. The result is shorter recovery windows, reduced soreness, and better performance gains—your body rebuilding stronger without the extended downtime that typically limits training progress.

Mood, Anxiety & Stress Relief

Depression, anxiety, and chronic stress involve disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production. Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. This creates measurably improved mood scores, reduced anxiety symptoms, and better stress resilience within the first few sessions.

Brain Fog, Mental Clarity & Energy

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of your weight—neurons are incredibly energy-hungry. When mitochondrial function declines, cognitive performance suffers: memory weakens, focus deteriorates, brain fog sets in. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull, enhancing mitochondrial function in neurons and glial cells. Boosted ATP production supports memory formation, information processing, and neural communication whilst improved cerebral blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients. The result is improved executive function, better mental clarity, enhanced focus, and sustained energy throughout the day.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your muscle recovery goal, immediately post-workout (within 1 hour) is optimal when inflammation is at its peak. For mood and stress relief, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, helping stabilise mood throughout the day. For energy and mental clarity, morning use provides sustained focus and vitality. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (muscles, organs), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns or systemic benefits, a slightly greater distance can be more effective.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues have different optimal dose windows, which is why a muscle recovery protocol looks different to a mood protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during close-range treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
