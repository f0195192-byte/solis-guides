The science
How red light therapy helps

Hair Growth & Male Pattern Baldness

Hair loss occurs when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of actively growing, and when poor scalp circulation starves follicles of nutrients. Red light therapy wakes up stem cells in hair follicles, essentially kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase of your hair cycle. Near-infrared wavelengths trigger the release of nitric oxide in scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. For already-lost follicles that have completely disappeared, results will be limited. For miniaturised or dormant follicles still present, clinical trials show 35–51% increases in hair counts. Realistic expectation: improvement in existing hair strength and thickness, potential regrowth of weakened follicles, and slower progression of further loss.

Skin Barrier Repair & Eczema

Eczema and severe dryness involve a broken skin barrier: your skin can't hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create itching. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively. It also calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. Enhanced circulation delivers nutrients for barrier restoration whilst reduced inflammation allows your skin to function properly again. Clinical trials show 79% of patients reported reduced itching and 47% reductions in severity scores (SCORAD).

Anti-Ageing, Hyperpigmentation & Skin Rejuvenation

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces fine lines, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. For hyperpigmentation and uneven tone, red light improves microcirculation and accelerates cellular turnover, helping uneven pigmented cells get cleared away quickly and replaced by fresh, clear cells. The 630nm wavelength in particular has been shown to reduce inflammatory cytokines that contribute to blotchiness, resulting in a more balanced, luminous complexion.

Acne, Rosacea & Inflammatory Skin

Acne involves bacteria, overactive oil glands, and deep inflammation. Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands, downregulating excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses. For rosacea, red light is a "cold" therapy—no heat, no thermal damage, no aggravating sensitive skin. Instead, it modulates inflammatory mediators at the cellular level, reducing persistent redness and calming blood vessel reactivity. Unlike harsh topicals or antibiotics that strip skin or disrupt your microbiome, red light addresses inflammation without damage. Clinical studies show 77.9% reduction in inflammatory acne spots and 75% reduction in facial redness after consistent treatment.

Keratosis Pilaris

Keratosis pilaris stems from excess keratin blocking hair follicles and inflammatory response in those follicles. Red light reduces localised inflammation, improves blood flow to affected areas to support healing, and helps normalise skin cell turnover so keratin doesn't accumulate. Enhanced microcirculation and reduced inflammatory markers lead to smoother, less red skin over time.

Fat Loss Support & Body Composition

Stubborn fat resists diet and exercise because fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides. Red light creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. Combined with exercise, your body can access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. Applied to specific zones, red light helps release fat from those exact locations. Applied before exercise, it optimises your body's ability to mobilise and burn stored fat during the workout. Research shows 444% more fat loss in treated areas compared to exercise alone when combined properly.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits for your goals. For hair growth and scalp health, morning sessions after showering mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm. For skin rejuvenation and eczema management, morning or early afternoon allows your skin to heal and regenerate. For fat loss, timing your session 15–30 minutes before exercise optimises fat mobilisation for burning during your workout. For general energy support, morning use aligns with your natural energy production rhythms. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to, and the specific timing matters less than regular, sustained use.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR (near-infrared) LEDs that emit light at 810nm, 830nm, and 850nm wavelengths, which are completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeper into tissues than red light. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
