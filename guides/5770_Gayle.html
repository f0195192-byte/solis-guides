Your focus areas
Your health & wellness goals

Pain Relief — Back & Spine Pain

Chronic back and spine pain involves persistent inflammation and restricted blood flow to spinal tissues, nerve roots, and surrounding muscles. It can range from dull, aching stiffness to sharp, radiating discomfort that limits daily movement and quality of life.

Knee & Joint Pain

Joint pain typically stems from inflammation within the joint capsule, cartilage breakdown, or tendon stress. Knees are particularly susceptible due to the load they bear, leading to stiffness, swelling, and reduced range of motion.

Arthritis

Arthritis involves the progressive breakdown of joint cartilage combined with chronic inflammation in the synovial fluid. It causes characteristic morning stiffness, aching, and reduced mobility — particularly after periods of rest.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain

Fibromyalgia is a condition in which the central nervous system becomes hypersensitive, amplifying pain signals throughout the body. Combined with mitochondrial dysfunction, it causes widespread chronic pain, crushing fatigue, cognitive fog, and disrupted sleep — a uniquely challenging condition to manage.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Neuropathic pain results from damaged or dysfunctional nerve fibres that send faulty signals — causing burning, shooting pain, tingling, or numbness. Poor circulation to nerve tissue compounds the problem, starving nerves of the oxygen and nutrients needed for repair.

Hashimoto's Thyroiditis (Female Health)

Hashimoto's is an autoimmune thyroid condition in which the immune system attacks thyroid tissue, progressively reducing hormone production. It drives fatigue, weight gain, hair loss, brain fog, and low energy — and unlike most conditions, it involves an underlying immune dysregulation that standard hormone replacement doesn't address.

Energy & Chronic Fatigue

The exhaustion associated with fibromyalgia, Hashimoto's, and chronic illness isn't simply tiredness — it reflects genuine mitochondrial dysfunction. When ATP production drops, every system in the body underperforms, leading to the relentless fatigue that makes daily life so difficult.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair thinning linked to thyroid conditions like Hashimoto's happens when follicles are starved of circulation and hormonal support, becoming stuck in the resting phase rather than the active growth phase. This is a distressing but addressable side effect of the underlying condition.

Fat Reduction & Body Composition

Weight gain associated with hypothyroidism and Hashimoto's reflects sluggish metabolism and impaired fat mobilisation. When metabolic rate is suppressed, the body holds onto stored fat even with consistent effort — making conventional approaches frustratingly ineffective alone.

The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Pain is your body's inflammatory response to tissue damage. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood the area, creating swelling, restricting blood flow, and sensitising nerve endings — turning what should be temporary into chronic, debilitating pain.

Red light therapy interrupts this cycle at source. It downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst simultaneously increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep enough to reach spinal tissues, joint capsules, and deep muscle layers where chronic pain originates. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle.

Clinical evidence is strong: a 2006 study in Pain Research Management found a 50% reduction in chronic back pain compared to placebo, and a 2018 study demonstrated a 30% reduction in systemic inflammatory markers after just one session. For Gayle, whose back and spine pain has been a constant companion alongside fibromyalgia, this represents a meaningful and evidence-backed route to relief.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Arthritis means the protective cartilage in your joints is breaking down whilst inflammatory compounds accumulate in the synovial fluid. Your joints lose their shock absorption, become inflamed, and the characteristic morning stiffness sets in.

Red light penetrates to the joint capsule itself, reducing inflammatory markers whilst stimulating chondrocytes — the cells responsible for cartilage repair and maintenance. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients to support this repair process, breaking the inflammatory cycle that prevents healing.

A 2023 study in the Journal of Rheumatology found that patients with rheumatoid arthritis experienced a 70% decrease in joint pain and a 38% improvement in morning stiffness after 4 weeks of treatment. Unlike conventional treatments that primarily mask symptoms, red light therapy supports actual joint tissue repair.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndromes

Fibromyalgia means your central nervous system has become hypersensitive — your brain amplifies pain signals, turning normal sensations into widespread chronic pain. Combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing severe fatigue, you're left exhausted and aching with no clear injury to treat. For Gayle, who has lived with fibromyalgia since 2006, this is deeply familiar territory.

Red light therapy addresses both core issues simultaneously. It desensitises overactive nerve endings that are misinterpreting signals as pain, essentially recalibrating your pain threshold. Simultaneously, it enhances mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting cellular ATP production that combats the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia.

The research is striking: a 2019 study in Rheumatology International involving 325 participants found that approximately 95% showed measurable improvements in pain scores, "fibro-fog," and quality of life. A separate 2019 Brain Sciences study found that 31.6% of participants improved so significantly they no longer met diagnostic criteria for fibromyalgia after just 6 weeks. These results don't represent minor tweaks — they represent genuine transformation.

Neuropathy & Nerve-Related Pain

Neuropathy — whether from chronic illness, metabolic dysfunction, or systemic inflammation — means damaged nerve fibres sending faulty signals: burning sensations, shooting pain, numbness, or persistent tingling. Poor microcirculation compounds the problem by starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients needed for repair.

Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates the tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves need, whilst removing inflammatory compounds that perpetuate pain signals.

A 2019 study in the Journal of Neurological Sciences found significantly increased nerve conduction velocities and a 63.2% reduction in pain index scores after 6 weeks of treatment. This is especially relevant for Gayle given the nerve pain associated with chronic conditions like fibromyalgia.

Hashimoto's Thyroiditis & Autoimmune Support

Hashimoto's is unlike most conditions your endocrinologist treats — it involves an active immune attack on your own thyroid tissue. Conventional treatment replaces the hormones your thyroid can no longer produce, but doesn't address the autoimmune attack itself or support remaining thyroid function. Gayle's endocrinologist may have been cautious about red light therapy, but the clinical evidence specifically for Hashimoto's is compelling.

Red light reaches the thyroid directly given its neck location. It enhances mitochondrial function in thyroid cells, improving hormone synthesis, whilst modulating the immune response driving the autoimmune attack. Studies show it reduces anti-thyroid antibodies — the very markers that reflect ongoing immune destruction of thyroid tissue.

A landmark 2013 study at the University of São Paulo found that 47% of Hashimoto's patients were able to completely discontinue their thyroid medication after 10 treatments. A 2018 study in Lasers in Surgery and Medicine documented a 600-point (~50%) drop in anti-TPO antibodies after 2 treatments per week for 4 weeks. The evidence here is not speculative — it's direct and specific to Hashimoto's.

Energy Levels & Mitochondrial Support

The exhaustion Gayle describes — the relentless fatigue that makes everything feel harder — is not a character failing or something to push through. It reflects genuine mitochondrial dysfunction. When your mitochondria can't produce adequate ATP — the energy currency that powers every cell — you feel depleted regardless of how much you rest.

Red light therapy directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, the key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. Essentially, you're restoring the power plants at the cellular level.

Research published in Scientific Reports in 2023 showed that a single red light session boosted mitochondrial ATP production by up to 70%. A 2012 Journal of Athletic Training study found a 30% increase in energy efficiency and morning alertness. For someone with fibromyalgia and Hashimoto's — both conditions involving significant mitochondrial dysfunction — this is a foundational benefit.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair thinning linked to thyroid dysfunction happens when follicles become stuck in their resting (telogen) phase rather than progressing to active growth (anagen). Poor scalp circulation compounds this, leaving follicles starved of the nutrients they need to produce strong, healthy hair.

Red light therapy addresses both problems. The 630–660nm wavelengths specifically stimulate stem cells within hair follicles, essentially waking them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase. It also triggers nitric oxide release in scalp cells, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle.

The clinical evidence is consistent: a 2017 University of Murcia study involving 100+ participants found a 51% increase in terminal hair counts in women treated every other day for 17 weeks. The Wellman Center for Photomedicine found a 37% increase in hair count compared to placebo over 26 weeks. These are not subtle improvements — they're measurable, meaningful changes that matter.

Fat Reduction & Body Composition

Weight that won't shift despite effort is one of the most demoralising features of hypothyroidism and Hashimoto's — and the reason is physiological, not motivational. When metabolism is suppressed, fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides, making them resistant to conventional diet and exercise approaches.

Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into the bloodstream where it becomes available as fuel. When combined with even light movement, your body can then access and burn this mobilised fat rather than it being reabsorbed. Applied to specific zones, it allows preferential fat mobilisation from targeted areas.

A 2011 Obesity Research study found 444% more fat loss in treated areas compared to exercise alone, and a University of São Paulo study documented a 2.15cm reduction in waist circumference after just 4 weeks. For Gayle, wanting to prove her endocrinologist wrong, these numbers represent a genuine path forward.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For Gayle's goals, morning sessions are particularly valuable. Red and NIR light mimics natural sunrise wavelengths, helping to reset your circadian rhythm and supporting energy production for the day ahead — which matters greatly when dealing with the fatigue of fibromyalgia and Hashimoto's. Morning sessions also set the anti-inflammatory effect in motion before the day's activity.

For the thyroid protocol specifically, time of day is less critical — just be consistent with your 2–3 sessions per week. For hair growth, any consistent time works; the key is regularity. For fat mobilisation, the session must immediately precede movement to be most effective. Evening use (1–2 hours before bed) can support melatonin production and improve sleep quality, which is valuable when pain and fatigue disrupt sleep.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating several centimetres into tissue where the therapeutic effects occur. To confirm they're active, point a smartphone camera at the panel — you'll see a faint purple glow confirming the NIR LEDs are on.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
