The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Your eczema is driven by a broken skin barrier and an overactive immune response that creates persistent inflammation and hypersensitivity. Red and near-infrared light work at the cellular foundation. The 830nm and 850nm wavelengths penetrate deeply into dermal tissue where most of your discomfort originates, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-alpha that perpetuate the inflammatory cycle. Simultaneously, red light accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts and enhancing blood flow to deliver nutrients and oxygen. This calms overactive nerve endings responsible for itching and spasms whilst restoring skin's protective function. Clinical trials show 79% of eczema patients reported reduced itching and 47% saw severity reductions within weeks of consistent treatment.

Sleep Quality & Sleep Onset

Red light supports sleep through multiple pathways. Evening exposure stimulates your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally, signalling your body it's time to wind down. Unlike blue light from screens which suppresses melatonin by up to 85%, red and NIR wavelengths enhance it. Additionally, red light enhances mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating your sleep-wake cycle, meaning your circadian rhythm operates more efficiently. By reducing the skin discomfort keeping you awake, you remove a major sleep disruptor. Studies show 79% reduction in time to fall asleep and 13% improvement in overall sleep quality within 2 weeks of evening red light exposure.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your eczema, morning or afternoon sessions work best—there's no circadian preference since you're targeting inflammation control. For sleep, use your SOLO 2.0 in the evening 1–2 hours before bed. This timing is critical: red light in the evening supports melatonin production and circadian alignment, helping you wind down naturally. Morning or afternoon sessions for sleep support can actually be counterproductive as they may energise rather than prepare you for rest. If you're treating both concerns, you could do a midday eczema session (20cm, 10 min) and an evening sleep session (30cm, 15 min)—the two protocols work well together.

Why can't I see all the lights on my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Your SOLO 2.0 contains 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. These invisible NIR wavelengths are actually the most therapeutically powerful for your deep dermal eczema and the neurological aspects of poor sleep. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
