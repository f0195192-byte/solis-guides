The science
How red light therapy helps

Acne & Breakouts

Red light penetrates deeply to reach the sebaceous glands where acne originates. It downregulates excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses, calming angry, painful spots from within. Unlike harsh topicals that strip skin or antibiotics that disrupt your microbiome, red light addresses inflammation without damage. The result is faster healing, reduced scarring, and fewer new breakouts, allowing your skin to regulate itself properly.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Uneven pigmentation stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin, dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With better circulation, your cells regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, pigmented cells from sun damage get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, clear cells. Your skin tone becomes more even and brightness improves, transforming a dull, blotchy complexion into something genuinely radiant, all naturally.

Skin Texture & Glow

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly improves skin texture and restores a more youthful appearance. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating natural repair and renewal cycles. The result is noticeably smoother, firmer skin with visible radiance as your skin's structural integrity improves.

Post-Workout Soreness (DOMS)

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres whilst metabolic waste like lactic acid accumulates, causing delayed-onset muscle soreness that peaks 24-48 hours post-workout. Red light therapy applied before or after training reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds. The result is shorter recovery windows and reduced soreness, allowing you to train more frequently without extended downtime.

Athletic Performance & Endurance

Red and near-infrared light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. During training, enhanced ATP production means your muscles have more available energy for sustained effort. Better mitochondrial efficiency translates to improved endurance, delayed fatigue onset, and enhanced performance gains — your cells simply have more fuel to power demanding physical efforts.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time, but timing enhances specific benefits for your goals. For skin goals (acne, pigmentation, texture), evening sessions work well — they don't interfere with makeup or your morning routine, and your skin has time to respond overnight. For athletic performance and pre-workout energy, morning or pre-training sessions are ideal, as red and NIR light mirror sunrise wavelengths and prime your mitochondria for optimal performance. For post-workout recovery, use within 1 hour of finishing your session when muscle repair is most active. Aim for consistency — the best time is one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
