The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem combined with overactive nerve endings that perpetuate the itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy tackles this at source by accelerating skin barrier repair — helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively — whilst simultaneously calming hypersensitive nerve endings that are triggering excessive itching. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups and significantly improved sleep from reduced night-time itching. Unlike steroids which suppress symptoms and damage barrier function over time, red light supports your skin's actual ability to function properly again.

Collagen & Skin Elasticity

Prolonged steroid use inhibits collagen production, leaving your skin thin, fragile, and prone to damage. Red light reactivates your fibroblasts — the cells responsible for building collagen and elastin — essentially restoring your skin's natural repair capacity. Red wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate these cells directly, whilst near-infrared enhances circulation to deliver the nutrients needed for collagen synthesis. This rebuilds the structural integrity that steroids eroded, creating genuinely stronger, more resilient skin rather than just treating symptoms.

Hair Growth & Male Pattern Baldness

Hair loss occurs when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of the growth phase, and when poor circulation starves your scalp of the nutrients it needs. Red light therapy tackles both. It wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, essentially kickstarting them back into growth mode, whilst triggering the release of nitric oxide in your scalp cells — which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density and thicker individual strands.

Vascular & Circulation Support

Your chillblains are fundamentally a vascular problem — poor blood flow combined with excessive vasoconstriction in response to cold. Red light therapy stimulates endothelial cells (which line your blood vessels) to produce nitric oxide — a potent vasodilator that improves blood vessel function and dramatically enhances circulation. Enhanced blood flow means better oxygen and nutrient delivery to tissues whilst reduced inflammatory response means your blood vessels become less reactive to cold triggers. This addresses the root vascular dysfunction rather than just masking inflammation with steroids.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing matters. For eczema and circulation support, morning or early afternoon sessions work best — red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and helps your vascular system regulate properly throughout the day, making you less reactive to cold and stress triggers. For hair growth, any time works but consistency is key. For skin barrier repair and collagen building, evening sessions complement your repair cycles. The best time is ultimately the one you'll stick to — consistency beats perfect timing.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
