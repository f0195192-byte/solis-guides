The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem — your skin cannot retain moisture, inflammatory signals run unchecked, and hypersensitive nerve endings create relentless itching. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to produce structural proteins that restore your protective "shield." Crucially, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch-reflex cycle that worsens inflammation. NIR wavelengths penetrate deeply to reduce inflammatory cytokines throughout affected tissue whilst enhancing blood flow for faster healing. Clinical studies show fewer flare-ups, significantly reduced itch intensity, and measurably improved skin function — addressing the root dysfunction rather than just suppressing symptoms with topical steroids.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy changes this by encouraging orderly, structured collagen deposition — the way healthy skin actually forms. It prevents excessive cell accumulation responsible for raised, keloid scars whilst for indented scars, boosted collagen production literally helps "lift" and level the skin's surface. Enhanced mitochondrial function in repair cells accelerates wound closure whilst reduced inflammation prevents tissue damage that worsens scarring. The result is faster healing with visibly better scar appearance and reduced long-term marking.

Skin Texture & Glow

Dull, uneven skin stems from sluggish cellular turnover and poor blood flow to your skin's surface. Dead, damaged cells hang around too long whilst fresh ones struggle to break through. Red light therapy stimulates formation of new blood vessels (angiogenesis), dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every skin layer. With better circulation, your cells regenerate faster and more efficiently. Old, damaged cells are cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, healthy cells. Your skin tone becomes more even, brightness improves, and that dull, blotchy complexion transforms into something genuinely radiant and luminous.

Collagen & Elasticity

As we age, collagen and elastin production slows and the skin's support structure breaks down. Red light activates your fibroblasts — the cells responsible for building and maintaining collagen and elastin — and supercharges them with ATP (cellular energy). This gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged fibres and build fresh ones more efficiently. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. The result is measurably smoother, firmer skin with visibly reduced wrinkle depth and improved overall elasticity.

Female Hair Thinning

Hair loss happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of the growth phase, and when poor circulation means your scalp isn't getting the nutrients it needs. Red light therapy tackles both problems. It wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, essentially kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase of your hair cycle. It also triggers nitric oxide release in scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. Clinically proven increases in hair density and thicker individual strands — supporting your body's natural growth mechanisms, optimised at the cellular level.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin and eczema goals, morning sessions are ideal — red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and aligns with your circadian rhythm, which helps regulate skin barrier function and immune responses. Morning use also means you start your day with reduced inflammation and better skin readiness for the day ahead. That said, any consistent time works — the best time is the one you'll stick to. Just avoid using directly before bed as the mild alertness from light exposure might interfere with sleep onset for some people.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
