The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Healing Post-Surgery

Surgical trauma triggers inflammatory responses that create pain and slow healing. Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deeply into healing tissue to reduce inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing ATP production in damaged cells. This gives tissue the energy needed to rebuild collagen and close wounds efficiently. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst reducing post-operative swelling and pain. Clinical studies show wounds heal 40% faster with red light therapy, whilst post-surgical scar induration reduces by up to 58%, creating smoother, less visible scarring. For facial and body surgery recovery, this means shorter downtime, less pain medication dependency, and better aesthetic outcomes.

Hair Growth & Scalp Health

Hair loss occurs when follicles stall in the resting phase instead of growth phase, and when poor circulation starves the scalp of nutrients. Red light therapy wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, kickstarting them back into active growth mode and extending the growth phase of your hair cycle. It triggers nitric oxide release in scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. Clinical trials show 35–51% increases in hair count, particularly for female-pattern thinning. Thicker individual strands and a healthier scalp environment develop without chemicals, hormones, or side effects—just your body's natural growth mechanisms, optimised.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For post-surgical pain relief and healing, morning sessions are ideal—they align with your body's natural repair rhythm and set the healing tone for the day. For hair growth, any consistent time works, though many users prefer evening sessions. Consistency matters far more than timing—the best time is one you'll stick to every single day or 3–4 times weekly without fail.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and means they're working powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming the NIR LEDs are active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (surgical wounds, tissue repair, hair follicles), you want to be closer to maximise penetration and therapeutic effect.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why post-surgical protocols look different to hair treatment protocols.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during close-range treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. For post-surgical areas, follow your surgeon's guidance on wound care first. For scalp treatment, hair can be present but part it so light reaches skin directly. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure treated areas are free from barriers to allow maximum absorption.
