The science
How red light therapy helps

Long COVID & Post-Viral Fatigue

Long COVID's crushing fatigue stems from mitochondrial dysfunction—viral damage prevents cells from producing ATP efficiently. Red and near-infrared light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondrial respiratory chain, restoring ATP production to near-normal levels. Simultaneously, NIR penetrates brain tissue to reduce neuroinflammation causing cognitive impairment and brain fog. Enhanced cellular energy means your damaged energy systems gradually "reboot," returning function to tissues affected by the virus. Clinical evidence shows marked improvements in cognitive clarity, exercise tolerance, and fatigue severity within weeks of consistent use.

POTS (Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome)

POTS involves autonomic dysregulation and impaired vascular function. Red light therapy enhances mitochondrial function in nerve cells regulating your autonomic nervous system, helping restore balance between sympathetic and parasympathetic responses. The therapy stimulates nitric oxide production in endothelial cells lining blood vessels, improving vascular function and blood pressure stability. Enhanced ATP production in cardiac tissue supports more efficient heart function, reducing the excessive tachycardia characteristic of POTS. Better mitochondrial energy in your autonomic regulatory centres helps your body maintain stable blood pressure during positional changes.

Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS)

MCAS is fundamentally an inflammatory condition where mast cells release excessive histamine and other inflammatory mediators. Red light therapy modulates immune responses by enhancing mitochondrial function in mast cells and immune-regulating T-cells, reducing the inflammatory cascade. The therapy downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha) that perpetuate mast cell activation whilst enhancing anti-inflammatory responses. By reducing systemic inflammation and stabilising immune cell function, red light therapy helps prevent flare-ups and reduces symptom severity. The effect is gradual immune rebalancing rather than acute suppression.

Muscle Recovery & Systemic Resilience

Enhanced mitochondrial ATP production supports tissue repair whilst reduced inflammatory markers minimise post-activity flare-ups. Red light accelerates muscle tissue recovery following exertion, improving your capacity to engage in activity without triggering symptom exacerbation. This enhanced recovery capacity builds systemic resilience, gradually expanding your tolerance for activity and reducing the post-exertional malaise common in these conditions.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis STUDIO 2.0?

For your conditions, morning sessions are ideal for Long COVID and POTS support, as red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports circadian rhythm alignment—this is particularly important for autonomic stability. Morning use also ensures maximum energy support throughout your day. For MCAS, consistent timing (morning or afternoon) helps your immune system adapt to the therapy predictably. Avoid sessions less than 2 hours before bed, as the stimulating effect might interfere with sleep. The best time is whenever you can be consistent—your system benefits most from daily or regular exposure at a fixed time.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your STUDIO 2.0 has 480 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your tissues to reach your mitochondria and autonomic nervous system centres. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the STUDIO 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Enhanced mitochondrial function in brain tissue supports neurotransmitter production and reduces neuroinflammation, helping alleviate depression, anxiety, and brain fog—particularly common in Long COVID and chronic inflammatory conditions.

Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure enhances oxygen and nutrient delivery to all tissues, supporting cardiovascular stability and potentially reducing POTS-related symptoms of dizziness and poor oxygen perfusion.
Pain reduction
Red light therapy reduces inflammatory cytokines and modulates pain signalling, helping manage pain associated with muscle tension, inflammation from MCAS, or general body aches from Long COVID.
Immune balance
By enhancing mitochondrial function in immune cells and modulating excessive inflammatory responses, red light supports appropriate immune function—neither under-responding to threats nor over-responding with excessive inflammation.
Exercise tolerance
Gradually improved muscle recovery and reduced post-exertional flare-ups may allow increasing activity levels over weeks to months—a key goal for Long COVID management where activity intolerance is severely limiting.
