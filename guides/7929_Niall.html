The science
How red light therapy helps

Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis is driven by excessive pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-alpha that accumulate in pelvic tissue, triggering the pain cycle. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep into pelvic organs to reach affected tissue directly. They downregulate these inflammatory mediators whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in immune cells, modulating overactive responses that perpetuate tissue damage. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory waste, breaking the pain-inflammation cycle. The result is genuine relief without the side effects of hormonal suppression.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Severe period pain reflects dysregulated inflammatory response and hormone signalling disruption. Red light therapy improves blood flow to reproductive organs, supporting more balanced hormone production and uterine function. Reduced inflammatory response means less uterine hypersensitivity and pain during menstruation. By normalising circulatory patterns and reducing pelvic inflammation, red light helps your cycle regulate naturally—often reducing pain intensity whilst supporting more predictable, manageable periods.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain

Fibromyalgia involves central nervous system hypersensitivity where your brain amplifies pain signals, combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing severe fatigue. Red and NIR light desensitises overactive nerve endings whilst dramatically increasing ATP production in cells throughout your body. Enhanced mitochondrial function combats the crushing fatigue whilst recalibrated pain perception means normal sensations no longer register as pain. This dual action addresses both the exhaustion and widespread pain characteristic of fibromyalgia.

Abdominal Bloating & Digestive Comfort

Endometriosis-associated bloating stems from intestinal inflammation and compromised gut barrier function. Red light penetrates abdominal tissue, reducing intestinal inflammation and supporting barrier repair. Enhanced mitochondrial function in intestinal cells improves nutrient absorption and gut motility whilst reduced inflammation helps restore healthy microbial balance. The result is reduced bloating, improved digestive comfort, and better nutrient absorption—directly addressing the physical discomfort disrupting your quality of life.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your endometriosis and pelvic pain, any consistent time works, though morning sessions can help combat pain-related fatigue. For your fibromyalgia component, morning is ideal—red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and boosts energy to counter afternoon crashes when pain perception typically worsens. For period pain, use daily during menstruation whenever suits your routine. Consistency matters more than timing—pick a time you'll stick to every session day.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into pelvic and abdominal tissue where your pain originates. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
