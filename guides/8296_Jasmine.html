The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Skin Barrier Repair

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem where your skin cannot retain moisture and inflammatory signals run out of control. Red light penetrates to the dermal layer, accelerating skin barrier repair by enhancing collagen and lipid synthesis—the structural molecules your skin uses to seal itself. Simultaneously, near-infrared wavelengths reduce the infiltration of inflammatory T-cells and calm overactive nerve endings responsible for the relentless itch. Enhanced cellular energy in skin cells means they have the ATP needed to rebuild protective barrier function efficiently. Clinical evidence shows significant reduction in itching, visible improvement in skin symptoms, and fewer flare-ups with consistent red light exposure.

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) occurs when damaged skin cells remain stuck in the inflammatory cycle, leaving behind excess melanin deposits. Red light therapy accelerates cellular turnover by stimulating fibroblasts and keratinocytes, essentially telling old, pigmented cells to shed faster. The 630nm wavelength in particular reduces inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate both inflammation and excess pigment production. Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers oxygen and nutrients to support this cellular renewal, whilst modulating the immune response that originally triggered the hyperpigmentation. The result is gradual fading of dark spots and restoration of even skin tone.

Mood, Anxiety & Nervous System Regulation

Red and near-infrared light penetrate to your brain, enhancing mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation and nervous system balance. This boosts ATP production supporting neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, dopamine, GABA) whilst reducing neuroinflammation that drives anxiety and depression. NIR wavelengths in particular activate the parasympathetic nervous system—your body's natural "rest and digest" state—whilst improving cerebral blood flow and oxygen delivery. This neurological shift reduces stress-related muscle tension as your nervous system recalibrates toward calmness. Enhanced mitochondrial function also directly improves energy levels and mental clarity, complementing mood improvement.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For mood and nervous system goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, boosting energy and mental clarity throughout the day. For anxiety management and stress relief, both morning and afternoon work well. For sleep support and evening calm, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. For eczema and skin barrier repair, consistency matters more than timing—pick a time you'll stick to daily. Aim for the same time each day when possible to train your nervous system.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
