The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis Relief

Red light therapy tackles eczema on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively—crucial for eczema-prone skin. It also calms overactive nerve endings directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens the condition. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching. Rather than masking symptoms with steroids, red light therapy supports your skin's natural ability to function properly again.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

By improving microcirculation and accelerating cellular turnover, red light therapy helps even out uneven pigmentation and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. The 630nm wavelength in particular has been shown to reduce inflammatory cytokines that contribute to redness and discoloration, resulting in a visibly more balanced, luminous complexion over time. For darker skin tones, this effect is particularly pronounced as improved circulation delivers more oxygen to melanin-producing cells, helping normalise pigment distribution.

Skin Texture & Glow

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly improves skin smoothness and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. The result is visibly brighter, more radiant skin with improved overall luminosity and a healthier glow from within.

Hair Growth & Hair Thinning

Red light therapy wakes up hair follicles stuck in the resting phase, essentially kickstarting them back into growth mode and extending the active growth phase of your hair cycle. It triggers the release of nitric oxide in your scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker individual strands, and a healthier scalp environment—all without chemicals or side effects.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For eczema and skin concerns, morning sessions are ideal to calm inflammation and support barrier repair throughout the day. For hyperpigmentation and texture, evening sessions work beautifully as your skin naturally repairs overnight, allowing enhanced collagen synthesis while you sleep. For hair growth, morning sessions stimulate follicles at the start of the day. Overall, the best time is the one you'll stick to consistently—red light therapy works with your biology whenever you use it, but morning aligns with your natural circadian rhythms for optimal effect.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Going further
How else can it help you?

Beyond your primary goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver additional wellness benefits that many users notice over time:

Red and NIR light can help regulate melatonin secretion and circadian rhythm, supporting deeper, more restorative sleep—especially when used in the morning or early evening. Better sleep also supports skin healing and hair growth cycles.
Mood & stress
Mitochondrial stimulation and reduced inflammation can support improved mood and stress resilience. This is particularly relevant for eczema, as chronic stress often triggers flare-ups.
Wound healing
Red light accelerates tissue repair by stimulating collagen synthesis and reducing inflammation, helping minor cuts, scrapes, or eczema-damaged skin heal faster and with less visible scarring.
Circulation
Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to tissues throughout the body, supporting recovery and overall cellular health—particularly beneficial for maintaining scalp health.
Immune support
By improving mitochondrial function and reducing systemic inflammation, regular sessions may help support your body's natural immune responses—relevant for managing autoimmune-related skin conditions like eczema.
