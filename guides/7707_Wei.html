The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces wrinkle depth, improves skin firmness, and restores texture, particularly important as your skin rebuilds post-TSW. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle your compromised barrier desperately needs during recovery.

Pigmentation & Skin Tone Balance

By improving microcirculation and accelerating cellular turnover, red light therapy helps even out the uneven pigmentation characteristic of topical steroid withdrawal — where some areas remain darker whilst others are lighter. The 630nm wavelength in particular reduces inflammatory cytokines that drive post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation and the redness you're experiencing. This results in a more balanced, unified skin tone across your hands, arms, wrists, and neck as new, evenly-toned cells replace the transitional tissue from withdrawal.

Skin Barrier Repair & Eczema Support

Topical steroid withdrawal compromises your skin barrier, making it unable to hold moisture and leaving it hypersensitive and inflamed. Red light therapy accelerates barrier repair by stimulating keratinocyte production and improving skin's natural lipid synthesis. It calms overactive nerve endings that create itching, directly reducing the itch-scratch cycle that perpetuates inflammation. For your hormonal flares, red light reduces inflammatory cytokines triggered by hormonal surges, helping your wrists, chin, and sensitive areas remain stable throughout your cycle.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scar formation involves chaotic collagen deposition. Red light encourages orderly collagen reorganisation whilst preventing excessive cells responsible for raised, fibrotic tissue. For your wrists and neck — still in active healing from TSW — enhanced collagen production helps tissues rebuild with better organisation, reducing visible scarring and improving skin texture as you progress through final recovery stages. NIR penetration reaches deeper layers, supporting tissue maturation throughout healing phases.

Hormonal Balance & Perimenopause Support

Perimenopause hormonal fluctuations trigger inflammatory cytokine surges that worsen eczema flares and compromise your barrier further. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in skin cells, giving them energy to maintain barrier integrity during hormonal swings. It modulates immune responses and reduces the inflammatory cascade triggered by oestrogen fluctuations. The result is more stable skin throughout your cycle, fewer hormone-triggered flares on your chin and other sensitive areas, and support for your body's natural transition through perimenopause without worsening skin inflammation.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For your skin recovery and pigmentation goals, morning sessions are ideal — they mirror sunrise wavelengths and support your natural circadian rhythm during TSW recovery. For hormonal balance and perimenopause support, morning sessions help regulate your endocrine system throughout the day. For barrier repair and flare management, consistency matters more than timing — use it whenever flares occur, ideally the same time daily so your routine becomes automatic. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to, especially given your complex recovery timeline.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeper into your skin layers to support collagen synthesis and tissue healing. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
