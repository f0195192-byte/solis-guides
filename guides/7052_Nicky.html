The science
How red light therapy helps

Fat Loss & Stubborn Belly Fat

Stubborn fat cells tightly hold stored triglycerides that resist your body's access and burning, particularly in the abdomen. Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. Combined with exercise, your body can actually mobilise and burn fat from these exact locations. Applied before movement, red light enables preferential fat burning from treated areas when paired with activity.

Back & Spine Pain Relief

Pain from trapped nerves and chronic back issues stems from pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding the area, restricting blood flow and sensitising nerve endings. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues and nerve roots where chronic pain originates. Red light downregulates inflammatory markers whilst dramatically increasing blood flow, delivering oxygen and nutrients needed for repair and removing inflammatory waste perpetuating the pain cycle. Enhanced circulation also helps relieve trapped nerve compression through reduced inflammation.

Knee & Joint Pain Relief

Osteoarthritis means protective cartilage breaks down whilst inflammatory compounds accumulate in synovial fluid, causing stiffness and pain. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing systemic inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes — cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen supporting repair processes and breaking the inflammatory cycle that prevents healing, resulting in reduced stiffness and improved mobility.

Shoulder & Neck Tightness

Chronic muscle tension and restricted blood flow in shoulders and neck limit range of motion whilst perpetuating tightness. Red light enhances ATP production in muscle tissue, improving energy for relaxation and repair. Improved circulation delivers oxygen and removes metabolic waste that contributes to muscle tension and soreness, whilst reduced inflammation helps restore normal tissue function and flexibility.

Perimenopause Support

Declining oestrogen disrupts your hypothalamus, the brain's temperature and hormonal regulation centre, whilst affecting neurotransmitter balance responsible for mood and sleep. Red light stimulates beta-endorphin release and enhances mitochondrial function in hormone-regulating glands, supporting energy and mood. Enhanced cellular energy helps your body's systems adapt more smoothly to hormonal changes whilst improved circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for stable energy throughout perimenopause.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For fat loss, apply your SOLO 2.0 15–20 minutes before exercise to mobilise fat efficiently. For pain relief, morning or early afternoon sessions give your tissue time to respond and inflammation to reduce before activity. For perimenopause support and energy, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and help stabilise hormones throughout the day. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
