The science
How red light therapy helps

Circulation & Arterial Health

Arterial stiffness occurs when endothelial cells lining blood vessel walls lose their ability to produce nitric oxide—the molecule that allows vessels to relax and dilate properly. Red light therapy directly stimulates endothelial cells to produce more nitric oxide, allowing arteries to relax and blood to flow more freely. Enhanced circulation delivers more oxygen and nutrients to peripheral tissues, reducing the heaviness and pain you experience in your legs. Improved blood flow also supports the natural removal of metabolic waste that accumulates in vessels and tissues.

Post-Viral Recovery & Immune Support

Persistent mould infection and post-viral fatigue result from immune cells struggling against ongoing infection and lingering mitochondrial damage. Red and near-infrared light directly boosts ATP production in immune cells like macrophages and T-lymphocytes, giving them more energy to mount effective immune responses. Simultaneously, it reduces the inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate brain fog and fatigue. Enhanced mitochondrial function in brain tissue helps clear metabolic waste and reduce neuroinflammation, improving cognitive clarity. The same mechanism supports respiratory tissue healing, helping clear the persistent cough and congestion.

General Vitality & Longevity

Aging is fundamentally a mitochondrial problem. As we age, our mitochondrial efficiency declines, producing less ATP and more oxidative damage that accelerates aging across all tissues. Red light therapy activates cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, dramatically increasing ATP production whilst simultaneously reducing oxidative stress. This restores your cells' capacity for repair, regeneration, and optimal function. Enhanced energy supports brain health, cardiovascular resilience, muscle maintenance, and the sustained stamina required for your demanding driving work—essentially optimising the cellular foundation of healthy aging.

Prostate Health

The prostate depends on robust mitochondrial function and healthy vascular supply for optimal function. Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep into pelvic tissue, enhancing ATP production in prostate cells whilst improving blood flow through enhanced nitric oxide production. This supports natural prostate function, healthy urinary flow, and cellular resilience. Enhanced cellular energy also helps maintain proper prostate size and function as we age.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your situation, morning sessions are ideal. Red light therapy mirrors natural sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm, boosting immune function and energy for your full-time driving work. Morning sessions also help combat brain fog and fatigue, giving you mental clarity for the road. You can do additional sessions in the afternoon if needed, but avoid evening sessions within 2 hours of bedtime unless specifically using red light for sleep support. Consistency matters more than timing—the best time is whenever you'll use it regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during full-body treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
