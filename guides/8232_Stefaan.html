The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. Clinical research shows 90% of subjects saw reduced wrinkle depth after just 4 weeks of consistent use.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Red light therapy addresses eczema on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively. It also calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. NIR light modulates inflammatory T-cells infiltrating affected skin whilst enhancing cellular energy for faster healing. Studies show 79% of patients reported reduced itching and 47% saw significant reduction in eczema severity scores within weeks.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means your facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, dilating excessively and creating persistent redness. Red light therapy works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. As a "cold" therapy with no heat, it won't aggravate sensitive skin. The result is reduced persistent redness, calmer blood vessel reactivity, and fewer trigger responses. Your skin's inflammatory threshold increases, meaning everyday triggers have less impact.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition, the way healthy skin is actually structured, whilst preventing excessive cells responsible for raised, keloid scars. For indented scars, boosted collagen production literally helps "lift" and level the skin's surface. For fresh wounds, red light accelerates repair cell migration to the wound site and enhances healthy tissue formation. Research shows wounds heal 40% faster with daily use and post-surgical scars reduce in hardness by up to 58%.

Back & Spine Pain, Knee & Joint Pain

Pain is your body's inflammatory response to tissue damage. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood affected areas, creating swelling and restricting blood flow. Red light therapy interrupts this at the source by downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues, joints, and deep muscle layers where chronic pain originates. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. Clinical studies show 50% reduction in chronic back pain intensity compared to placebo groups.

Shoulder & Neck Pain, Arthritis & Fibromyalgia

In arthritis, protective cartilage breaks down whilst inflammatory compounds accumulate in synovial fluid. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes—cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen supporting this repair process. For fibromyalgia, red light therapy desensitises overactive nerve endings that misinterpret normal sensations as pain. Simultaneously, it enhances mitochondrial function throughout your body, boosting cellular ATP production that combats the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia. Research shows 70% decrease in arthritic joint pain after 4 weeks and 95% of fibromyalgia patients experienced measurable improvements in pain and exhaustion.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Neuropathy means damaged nerve fibres send faulty signals, creating burning sensations, shooting pain, numbness, or "pins and needles" feeling. Poor microcirculation compounds the problem by starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients needed for repair. Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates the tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves need whilst removing inflammatory compounds that perpetuate pain signals. Clinical studies show 40% improvement in foot sensation and pain scores after 4 weeks, and 63% reduction in pain index scores after 6 weeks of treatment.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For skin goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and prepare your skin for the day ahead. For pain relief and joint stiffness, morning sessions help reduce morning rigidity whilst evening sessions can wind down inflammation before rest. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
