The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Chronic pain from fibromyalgia, arthritis, and ME/CFS stems from pro-inflammatory cytokines flooding affected tissues and sensitised nerve endings misinterpreting signals as pain. Red and near-infrared light penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues, joints, and muscle layers where chronic pain originates. These wavelengths downregulate pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst removing inflammatory waste perpetuating pain cycles. Near-infrared penetrates particularly deep, reaching the central nervous system to desensitise overactive nerve pathways characteristic of fibromyalgia. The result is measurable pain reduction and improved mobility without pharmaceutical dependency.

Energy & Mitochondrial Support

ME/CFS represents fundamental mitochondrial dysfunction—your cellular powerplants cannot produce sufficient ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency fuelling every biological process. Red and NIR light are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's respiratory chain, directly boosting ATP output. This restores your cells' ability to function, repair, and generate sustained energy. Increased mitochondrial output also reduces oxidative stress damaging mitochondrial function further—essentially breaking the fatigue cycle. For someone with ME/CFS, restoring even modest mitochondrial efficiency can dramatically improve daily function and quality of life.

Mood, Mental Clarity & Nervous System Regulation

Depression, brain fog, and low mood involve neuroinflammation and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling cognition and emotional regulation. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons and glial cells, boosting ATP production for neurotransmitter synthesis and neural communication. Enhanced cerebral blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients whilst NIR reduces neuroinflammation disrupting mood and cognitive pathways. For SAD (seasonal affective disorder), red light regulates circadian rhythms and supports serotonin synthesis when daylight is limited. The result is improved focus, clearer thinking, better mood stability, and improved nervous system regulation—critical for managing chronic illness without additional medication burden.

Skin & Hair Health

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for collagen and elastin production. This directly improves skin firmness and elasticity whilst enhanced circulation delivers nutrients supporting hair follicle function. Perimenopause involves hormonal shifts that slow collagen synthesis and weaken both skin and hair; red light therapy stimulates these natural production pathways to counteract age-related decline. The result is improved skin tone, reduced fine lines, and stronger hair growth.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your goals, timing is particularly important. Morning sessions (within 1–2 hours of waking) are ideal for pain management, energy support, and mood/SAD support. Red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths, supporting your circadian rhythm and serotonin production when you need it most. For pain, morning sessions reduce stiffness before it accumulates throughout the day. For ME/CFS fatigue, morning energy support gives you resources for daily activities. Evening sessions (1–2 hours before bed) are also beneficial—they reduce pain-driven tension helping you sleep better whilst supporting circadian regulation. The best time is consistency: choose a routine you can maintain daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into tissues where pain originates and where mitochondrial dysfunction from ME/CFS occurs. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spine, brain tissue), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (facial skin), a slightly greater distance allows even coverage.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a pain or recovery protocol.

For your specific situation with ME/CFS and fibromyalgia, consistency and appropriate dosing matter more than aggressive intensity. The protocols above are designed to deliver therapeutic benefit without overwhelming your system.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Going further
How else can it help you?

In addition to your primary goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver a range of broader wellness benefits particularly relevant to your situation:

Sleep quality
Red and NIR light regulate melatonin secretion and circadian rhythm. For ME/CFS and fibromyalgia, where sleep disruption is common, improved sleep quality directly reduces pain sensitivity and supports mitochondrial recovery during rest.
Neuropathy relief
NIR light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that improves blood flow to nerve tissue. Beneficial for nerve pain often accompanying fibromyalgia and chronic illness.
Autoimmune support
Red light modulates overactive immune responses by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines. For ME/CFS, which often involves immune dysregulation, this helps restore appropriate immune function without suppressing normal defence.
Circulation & oxygen delivery
Enhanced microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to tissues throughout the body. Particularly important for ME/CFS where cellular oxygen utilisation is impaired.
Hormone balance
During perimenopause, red light supports natural hormone production pathways whilst reducing hormonal dysregulation symptoms like night sweats and mood instability.
