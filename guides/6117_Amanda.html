The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Skin Rejuvenation

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to activate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for collagen and elastin production. By supercharging these cells with ATP, red light therapy accelerates collagen synthesis and repairs damaged fibres, directly reducing wrinkle depth and restoring skin firmness. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients, supporting the natural renewal cycle your skin needs at any age.

Acne & Breakouts

Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where it downregulates excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses that create painful, scarring spots. Unlike harsh topicals, red light reduces inflammation from within without damaging your skin's protective barrier. This allows your skin to regulate itself naturally, healing faster whilst reducing both new breakouts and post-inflammatory scarring.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea involves hyper-reactive facial blood vessels and excessive inflammatory mediators. Red light therapy is uniquely suited for this because it's a "cold" therapy—no heat, no aggravation. Instead, it works at the cellular level by balancing inflammatory markers and modulating vessel reactivity. The result is reduced persistent redness and fewer trigger responses, allowing your skin's inflammatory threshold to increase naturally.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing inflammatory compounds that restrict movement whilst stimulating chondrocytes — cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial oxygen and nutrients, supporting the repair process and breaking the inflammatory cycle that prevents healing. This is particularly valuable for morning stiffness, helping restore mobility and function.

Mitochondrial Health & Energy

Red and NIR light are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This direct stimulation dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. Essentially, you're optimising your cellular powerplants to produce more energy more efficiently — the foundation for sustained vitality, mental clarity, and healthy ageing.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your goals, Amanda, timing matters. For skin rejuvenation and acne management, morning sessions (after cleansing, before your day) are ideal — the enhanced circulation will support all-day skin health. For rosacea, use mornings or very early evening (1–2 hours before bed) when there's less external heat triggering flare-ups. For arthritis, use in the morning to tackle stiffness before activity, or evening to prepare joints for rest. For energy and mitochondrial support, morning sessions mirror your natural circadian rhythm and set you up for sustained clarity throughout the day. Consistency matters most — the best time is the one you'll stick to daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep to reach joints, organs, and systemic tissues. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
