The science
How red light therapy helps

Acne & Breakouts

Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where acne originates, downregulating excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses. Unlike harsh topicals that strip and irritate, red light calms angry, painful spots from within by reducing the pro-inflammatory cytokines driving breakouts. The 660nm wavelength in particular reduces bacterial colonisation whilst the 630nm supports faster healing of existing lesions. The result? Fewer new breakouts, faster healing, and less scarring—your skin learns to regulate itself properly.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Red light therapy tackles eczema on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective shield to retain moisture effectively. It also calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens flare-ups. The NIR wavelengths penetrate deeper to reduce systemic inflammation whilst improving blood flow for accelerated healing. Research shows fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less nighttime itching—supporting your skin's ability to function properly again, not just managing symptoms.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of growth phase. Red light therapy wakes up the stem cells in your hair follicles, kickstarting them back into active growth mode. Simultaneously, it triggers nitric oxide release in your scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. Enhanced circulation means your recovering scalp gets the oxygen and nutrients needed to rebuild density after methotrexate damage. Clinically proven increases in hair count and thicker individual strands within 12-20 weeks.

Muscle Recovery

Red light therapy applied post-exercise reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production in muscle tissue gives your cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, reducing the soreness that typically limits training frequency. Improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds whilst boosting collagen synthesis—the structural protein needed for strong recovery. Applied during or immediately after Pilates, you'll experience shorter recovery windows and better performance gains as your body rebuilds stronger without extended downtime.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your goals, timing can enhance specific benefits. For acne and eczema, morning or midday sessions work well—your skin has the full day to benefit from reduced inflammation. For hair growth, any consistent time works as long as you maintain frequency. For muscle recovery post-Pilates, use the panel immediately after your session whilst inflammation is still active and your muscles are primed for repair. For overall energy support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm. The best time is the one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into skin and muscle tissue. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (muscles, joints, follicles beneath the scalp), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (acne on skin surface, eczema barrier repair), specific distances are calculated to deliver optimal energy without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why your acne protocol (10 cm, 20 min) looks different to your eczema protocol (20 cm, 10 min). One targets deep sebaceous inflammation, the other prioritises skin barrier repair at optimal energy levels.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For facial treatments (acne, eczema on face), use on completely bare skin. For scalp treatments, part your hair to expose the scalp directly.
