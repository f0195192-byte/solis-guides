The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is a broken skin barrier problem: your skin can't hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create that relentless itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively. The 660nm wavelength in particular has been shown to reduce inflammatory cytokines driving the flare, whilst the 830nm near-infrared penetrates deeper to reach inflammation in the dermal layers. Clinical studies show it also calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. The result? Fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching, and genuine barrier repair—not just symptom suppression.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars and damaged skin from weeping areas form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic pattern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition—the way healthy skin is actually structured—whilst preventing excessive cells responsible for raised scarring. For damaged areas, boosted collagen production literally helps rebuild healthy tissue. Red light accelerates repair cell migration to wounded sites and enhances healthy tissue formation. Your body's natural repair mechanisms become significantly more efficient, with cuts and damaged areas closing faster and healing with less visible scarring.

Skin Texture & Glow

By improving microcirculation and accelerating cellular turnover, red light therapy helps restore a more even, luminous complexion. Enhanced blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients to skin cells, supporting the natural renewal cycle. As your skin barrier repairs and inflammation reduces, surface texture smooths and that natural radiance returns—powered by genuine cellular improvement rather than temporary plumping.

Collagen & Elasticity

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles whilst improving skin firmness and restoring elasticity. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. For eczema-damaged skin, this is particularly important: increased collagen production helps restore the structural integrity your barrier needs to function properly again.

Muscle Recovery

Red and NIR light are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase in the mitochondria, directly boosting ATP production in muscle tissue. Intense exercise or physical stress (including the strain from repeated scratching) creates micro-tears and metabolic waste accumulation. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds and lactic acid. The result? Reduced soreness, faster recovery, and relief from muscle tension and tightness.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For eczema specifically, morning sessions work best. Red light therapy can help reset your immune response and reduce inflammatory signals early in the day, giving your skin support when you need it most. For sleep quality (which eczema often disrupts), you can use a second session 1–2 hours before bed — the evening exposure supports melatonin production, helping you get the restorative sleep your skin desperately needs during healing. Avoid using immediately before or after hot showers, as heat can aggravate eczema. Aim for consistency — the best time is one you'll stick to every session.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath your skin's surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (muscles, joint inflammation), you want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin healing during a flare), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without overwhelming sensitive, inflamed tissue.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. For eczema especially, you want anti-inflammatory benefits without triggering a reaction from overexposure. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why your eczema protocol looks different from your muscle recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the SUPREME. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For eczema, absolutely avoid any products on affected areas immediately before treatment, though you can apply barrier creams and moisturisers immediately after sessions.
