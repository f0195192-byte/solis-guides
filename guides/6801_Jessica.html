The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Post-Workout Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres whilst metabolic waste accumulates, causing delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24-48 hours post-workout. Red light therapy reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds that perpetuate soreness. The result is shorter recovery windows, reduced DOMS severity, and better performance gains from your training.

Athletic Performance & Endurance

Athletic performance depends on mitochondrial efficiency in muscle tissue. Red and NIR light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, boosting ATP production that powers muscle contraction and sustained effort. Enhanced mitochondrial function means your muscles have more available energy for explosive power and endurance work. Simultaneously, improved circulation delivers more oxygen during activity whilst reducing fatigue markers that limit intensity. This translates to measurably better performance metrics, greater training volume tolerance, and improved power output.

Injury Recovery & Rehabilitation

Tissue repair requires substantial cellular energy and well-coordinated inflammatory responses. Red light therapy accelerates the repair cascade by enhancing ATP production in damaged tissue, giving repair cells the energy they need to rebuild efficiently. It simultaneously modulates inflammation, maintaining the initial response necessary for healing whilst preventing excessive, chronic inflammation that slows recovery and impairs function restoration. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach ligaments, tendons, and muscle tissue where injuries originate, supporting genuine structural repair rather than symptom management.

Muscle Tension & Tightness

Persistent tension reflects poor circulation and inflammatory metabolite accumulation in muscle tissue, reducing flexibility and creating the "tight" sensation. Red light therapy enhances blood flow to affected areas through nitric oxide stimulation, delivering oxygen and removing inflammatory compounds that contribute to stiffness. Enhanced ATP in muscle cells supports proper muscle relaxation cycles whilst improved circulation allows metabolic waste clearance. The result is improved range of motion, reduced perceived tightness, and better muscle function.

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle. The result is visibly smoother skin with genuine structural improvement from within.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, creating persistent redness and discomfort. Red light therapy is uniquely suited because it works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. The therapy's "cold" nature—minimal heat generation—ensures no aggravation of already-sensitive skin. Enhanced cellular function helps stabilise vascular reactivity, reducing the persistent redness and improving your skin's inflammatory threshold.

Skin Texture & Glow

Luminous skin depends on efficient cellular turnover and robust blood flow. Red light therapy stimulates both: improved circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst accelerated cell renewal means old, dull surface cells get cleared and replaced by fresh ones. Enhanced collagen synthesis and reduced inflammation create a clearer, more refined texture. The combination produces the characteristic "glow" of healthy, well-functioning skin.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen is your skin's structural foundation, responsible for firmness and elasticity. Red light directly activates fibroblasts—collagen-producing cells—giving them ATP energy to synthesise new collagen fibres efficiently. This isn't superficial plumping but genuine structural rebuilding. As collagen density increases, skin becomes visibly firmer and more elastic, restoring the supple texture of younger skin. NIR penetration reaches deeper dermis layers, supporting elastin production alongside collagen for comprehensive structural support.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits for your goals. For muscle recovery and athletic performance, post-workout sessions (or morning sessions before training) are ideal—your tissues are primed for response and recovery. For skin benefits, morning or evening both work well; morning sessions support skin repair during the day, whilst evening applications prepare skin for overnight restoration. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency—the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
