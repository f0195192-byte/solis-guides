The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Atopic Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem — your skin cannot hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create that relentless itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy addresses this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts to rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. The 630nm and 660nm wavelengths reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst improving microcirculation. Near-infrared penetration reaches deeper inflammatory markers. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching — not masking symptoms, actually supporting your skin's ability to function properly again.

Shoulder & Neck Pain

Pain in your shoulder and neck region is your body's inflammatory response to tissue damage or chronic stress. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood the area, creating swelling, restricting blood flow, and sensitising nerve endings. Near-infrared wavelengths (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) penetrate deep into your muscle and connective tissue where chronic pain originates. Red light therapy downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues, delivering oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. The result is measurable pain reduction, improved mobility, and faster tissue repair without reliance on pain medication.

Post-Surgical Pain & Recovery

After surgery, your body initiates tissue repair but this process is energy-intensive and can be sluggish. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in healing tissue, dramatically boosting ATP production — the cellular fuel needed for efficient repair. Simultaneously, it reduces post-surgical inflammation and swelling whilst improving microcirculation to deliver the oxygen and nutrients tissues need to rebuild. Enhanced cellular energy means your body can complete the repair process faster with less pain, reducing the extended recovery period that typically follows surgical intervention.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your eczema and pain relief goals, timing enhances specific benefits. Morning sessions support skin barrier repair and circadian rhythm alignment. For shoulder and neck pain, early afternoon or evening sessions allow your tissue time to respond before sleep, helping you rest more comfortably. For post-surgical recovery, daily consistency matters more than timing — use whenever fits your schedule. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to. Avoid using red light therapy within 2 hours of your immunosuppressant medications if you take them orally, to avoid any potential interaction timing concerns.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
