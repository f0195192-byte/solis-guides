The science
How red light therapy helps

Psoriasis

Red light therapy works by normalising your skin cell proliferation cycles — the autoimmune process that causes psoriasis accelerates skin cell multiplication by up to 10 times the normal rate. Red wavelengths (630-660nm) penetrate to the epidermis and reduce the infiltration of pro-inflammatory T-cells driving the condition, whilst simultaneously slowing the overactive cell turnover back to healthy rates. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deeper, reducing systemic inflammatory markers like TNF-alpha and IL-6 that sustain the autoimmune response. Studies show clearance rates of 60-100% of scaly plaques within 4 weeks, with particular effectiveness for guttate psoriasis. This complements your previous UV therapy experience but without UV's cumulative skin damage or limited wavelength spectrum.

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to reach the prefrontal cortex and limbic system, the brain regions responsible for anxiety regulation and emotional processing. It enhances mitochondrial function in neurons, boosting ATP production needed for neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, GABA, dopamine) that directly reduce anxiety. Simultaneously, NIR light reduces neuroinflammation — chronic inflammation in brain tissue that perpetuates anxiety and mood dysregulation. A single 10-minute session has been shown to reduce anxiety scores by 63% and depression by 54%. For perimenopause-related anxiety, improved mitochondrial function in your hypothalamus (which regulates both mood and hormonal response) helps stabilise emotional reactivity as your oestrogen fluctuates.

Brain Fog, Mental Clarity & Cognitive Function

Your brain uses 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of its weight — neurons are exceptionally energy-hungry. Brain fog and cognitive impairment from ABI often reflect compromised mitochondrial function in neurons and reduced blood flow to affected regions. Near-infrared light (810nm and 830nm) penetrates deep into brain tissue, directly activating cytochrome c oxidase in neuronal mitochondria. This boosts ATP production, giving your neurons more energy for memory formation, information processing, and neural communication. Enhanced cerebral circulation improves oxygen and nutrient delivery whilst reduced neuroinflammation helps clear metabolic waste that accumulates and impairs cognitive function. Meta-analyses show a 19% boost to cognitive performance, with 40-50% reduction in amyloid and tau plaques in memory-related brain regions after consistent treatment.

Energy & Fatigue

Chronic fatigue at cellular level reflects mitochondrial dysfunction — your mitochondria simply aren't producing adequate ATP (adenosine triphosphate), your cells' fundamental energy currency. Red and near-infrared wavelengths are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase, a critical enzyme in your mitochondrial respiratory chain. This absorption directly boosts ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. Studies show 30% improvement in energy efficiency and a 70% boost in mitochondrial ATP production from consistent sessions. For you, this is particularly important given the cumulative metabolic demands of ABI recovery, perimenopause, and managing chronic conditions with restricted dietary options.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing optimises your specific benefits. For psoriasis, energy, and cognitive support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm and mitochondrial function. For anxiety management, you can use morning for mood lift or early afternoon for stress relief — avoid the 2 hours before bed to not interfere with sleep. For sleep support itself, use 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to daily or regularly.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
