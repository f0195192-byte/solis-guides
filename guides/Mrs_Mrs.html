The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Fibromyalgia involves excessive pro-inflammatory cytokines that your central nervous system amplifies into widespread pain. Red and near-infrared light directly downregulates these cytokines — reducing systemic inflammation at its source. Enhanced blood flow to painful tissues delivers oxygen and removes inflammatory waste perpetuating your pain cycle. NIR wavelengths penetrate deep to reach nerve tissue and spinal areas where chronic pain originates, desensitising overactive nerve endings that misinterpret normal sensations as severe pain. The result is measurable reduction in pain intensity without pharmaceutical side effects.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Inflammation in your joint capsules restricts movement and causes stiffness, particularly in the morning. Red light penetrates to reach chondrocytes — cells responsible for cartilage repair — whilst simultaneously reducing the inflammatory compounds accumulating in synovial fluid. Enhanced circulation delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen supporting tissue repair. This addresses the inflammatory cycle preventing natural healing, with morning sessions being especially effective for addressing your morning stiffness.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Your paresthesia reflects damaged or hypersensitive nerve fibres sending faulty signals. Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve axons whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates tiny blood vessels feeding nerve tissue. This enhanced circulation delivers the oxygen and nutrients nerves desperately need whilst removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating pain signals. The result is reduced burning and tingling sensations with improved nerve function.

Energy & Mitochondrial Support

Your crushing fatigue reflects mitochondrial dysfunction — your cells simply cannot produce sufficient ATP for normal function. Red and NIR light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress damaging mitochondrial function. Enhanced cellular energy means your brain, muscles, and organs finally have fuel to function properly, directly combating the fatigue that worsens throughout your day.

Fat Loss & Body Composition

Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. When combined with movement, your body can access and burn this mobilised fat instead of it remaining locked away. Applied before exercise to treatment areas, red light supports preferential fat burning from stubborn zones whilst enhanced mitochondrial function in muscle tissue supports lean muscle development and metabolic health.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For fibromyalgia and your specific pain pattern, timing is crucial. Your morning pain peak (until 1 pm) makes morning sessions ideal — red light directly addresses your worst pain when it's most severe. Start your general pain relief protocol between 8–11 am when pain is highest. Your energy sessions work best mid-morning (10–11 am) or early afternoon (1–2 pm) to prevent the fatigue decline you experience by end of day. Evening sessions 1–2 hours before bed support better sleep quality, which is critical for fibromyalgia management. Consistency matters more than perfect timing — use the times that fit your routine and you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. These NIR wavelengths penetrate deepest into your tissues where chronic pain, nerve damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction originate. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
