The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts — the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle.

Psoriasis

Psoriasis is an autoimmune condition where your skin cells go into overdrive, multiplying at 10 times the normal rate. Red light therapy normalises your skin cell proliferation cycles, essentially telling overactive cells to slow down to a healthy growth rate. Simultaneously, it reduces the infiltration of inflammatory T-cells, the immune cells causing the problem in the first place. Clinical studies show significant clearance of plaques that previously resisted conventional therapies.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means your facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, dilating excessively in response to triggers. Red light is a "cold" therapy — no heat, no thermal damage — working at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. The result is reduced persistent redness and calmer blood vessel reactivity, with your skin's inflammatory threshold increasing over time.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Uneven pigmentation stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow to your skin's surface. Dead, pigmented cells hang around too long while fresh ones struggle to break through. Red light therapy stimulates the formation of new blood vessels in your skin (angiogenesis), dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery. With better circulation, your cells have the fuel they need to regenerate faster. Old, pigmented cells get cleared away quickly, replaced by fresh, clear cells.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen and elastin production naturally declines with age, causing your skin to lose firmness and resilience. Red light activates your fibroblasts — the cells responsible for building and maintaining skin structure — by supercharging them with ATP (cellular energy). This gives them the fuel needed to repair damaged collagen fibres and build fresh ones more efficiently. The result is measurably smoother skin with actual structural improvement from within, not just surface effects.

Knee & Joint Pain

Joint pain stems from inflammatory compounds accumulating in synovial fluid whilst protective cartilage breaks down. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes — cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen, supporting this repair process and breaking the inflammatory cycle preventing healing. The result is reduced stiffness, improved mobility, and decreased pain during movement.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For skin and anti-ageing goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm. For joint pain, morning sessions help manage stiffness before daily activity. For rosacea, evening use allows your skin to stabilise overnight away from potential triggers. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
