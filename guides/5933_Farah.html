The science
How red light therapy helps

PCOS Support & Hormonal Balance

PCOS roots itself in mitochondrial dysfunction within ovarian cells and insulin-resistant tissues. Near-infrared light penetrates ovarian tissue directly, enhancing mitochondrial function in developing follicles and improving egg quality at the cellular level. Enhanced ATP production helps restore normal ovulation cycles whilst improved cellular energy in metabolic tissues supports insulin sensitivity, directly addressing the insulin resistance underlying PCOS. Additionally, red light modulates inflammatory responses in ovarian tissue, reducing the chronic inflammation that perpetuates cyst formation and hormonal imbalance. The result is improved ovulation, more regular cycles, and better hormonal regulation without pharmaceutical intervention.

Thyroid & Hashimoto's Support

In autoimmune thyroiditis, your immune system attacks thyroid tissue whilst mitochondrial dysfunction in thyroid cells impairs hormone synthesis. Red and NIR light reach your thyroid directly due to its neck location, enhancing mitochondrial function in thyroid follicular cells to improve hormone production efficiency. Simultaneously, NIR light modulates the overactive immune response characteristic of Hashimoto's by reducing pro-inflammatory T-cell infiltration and lowering anti-thyroid antibody production. The combined effect supports your thyroid's capacity to function whilst reducing the autoimmune attack driving tissue destruction—maintaining hormone production even as inflammation decreases.

Inflammation & Water Retention Management

Chronic inflammation and fluid retention stem from systemic cytokine elevation and poor microcirculation. Red light therapy reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-alpha whilst enhancing mitochondrial function throughout your body, reducing the oxidative stress that perpetuates inflammation. Improved circulation from enhanced nitric oxide production helps drain fluid pooling in tissues, whilst better cellular energy means your body's natural detoxification and lymphatic systems function more efficiently. The result is measurable reduction in both inflammatory markers and visible water retention, with improvements often noticeable within 1-2 weeks of consistent use.

Stress Response & Cortisol Regulation

Elevated cortisol disrupts all your other hormonal systems, driving inflammation, fluid retention, and metabolic dysfunction. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in your adrenal glands and brain regions controlling stress response, giving your body's regulatory systems more energy to maintain homeostasis. NIR light also modulates your autonomic nervous system, shifting you from sympathetic (fight-or-flight) dominance towards parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) balance. Enhanced cellular energy in hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis tissues helps normalize cortisol rhythms, supporting healthy circadian patterns where cortisol peaks in morning and declines toward evening. The result is reduced overall cortisol burden, better stress resilience, and improved regulation of all stress-dependent hormonal systems.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your goals, Farah, timing matters significantly. Morning sessions (within 1-2 hours of waking) are ideal for stress and cortisol support—red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths that help establish healthy circadian cortisol patterns. For thyroid and hormonal support, mornings or early afternoons work well. For inflammation and water retention, consistency matters more than timing, though many users find evening sessions leave them feeling less puffy by morning. Use your daily cortisol session in the morning, then layer in inflammation sessions at whatever time fits your schedule. The best protocol is the one you'll actually stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your tissues where your thyroid, ovaries, and systemic tissues are located. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
