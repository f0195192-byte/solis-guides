The science
How red light therapy helps

Menopause Symptom Relief

Early menopause means your hypothalamus has lost hormonal feedback and become hypersensitive, triggering inappropriate temperature regulation signals. Red light therapy works by stimulating beta-endorphin release in the brain — your body's natural mood-stabiliser and pain reliever — whilst modulating autonomic nervous system function. This helps your brain's regulatory centres adapt to changing hormones more smoothly. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reduce neuroinflammation that amplifies hot flush intensity, whilst enhanced blood flow supports better thermoregulatory signalling. The result is fewer and less severe hot flushes, improved mood stability, and better sleep quality.

Hormonal Balance & Stress Recovery

Burnout creates chronic cortisol dysregulation: your adrenal glands become exhausted, unable to maintain healthy stress hormone rhythms. This disrupts blood sugar regulation, feeding back to worsen inflammation and fatigue. Red light therapy directly enhances mitochondrial function in adrenal and hypothalamic tissues, restoring energy production needed for proper hormone synthesis and release. By boosting ATP production in endocrine cells, your body can re-establish normal cortisol rhythms — high in morning, low at night — which regulates downstream metabolic processes including blood sugar control. Reduced neuroinflammation further supports hormonal signalling pathways, helping your nervous system exit chronic stress mode.

Bone Health & Osteoporosis Recovery

Osteoblasts — the bone-building cells — are metabolically demanding cells requiring substantial ATP to synthesise new bone matrix. Early menopause removes oestrogen's protective effect on bone density, accelerating osteoclast (bone-breaking) activity. Red light penetrates to bone tissue, directly stimulating osteoblast activity by enhancing their mitochondrial function. This shifts the bone remodelling balance back towards formation rather than breakdown. Enhanced blood flow to bone tissue delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen supporting repair. Near-infrared wavelengths have been shown to increase alkaline phosphatase activity — a marker of active bone formation — directly supporting the regeneration process essential for reversing osteoporosis.

Systemic Inflammation & Energy Recovery

Chronic inflammation drives mitochondrial dysfunction: inflammatory cytokines like TNF-alpha and IL-6 impair cellular energy production, creating the fatigue you're experiencing. Red light therapy interrupts this vicious cycle by directly activating cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, dramatically boosting ATP production throughout your body. Simultaneously, it downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines, reducing the inflammatory suppression of mitochondrial function. With more ATP available, your cells have energy for repair, clearance of inflammatory waste, and restoration of normal function. This addresses the cellular root of your fatigue — not just masking symptoms but actually restoring your body's capacity to generate and use energy efficiently.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

For your menopause and hormonal goals, timing is especially important, Emma. For hormonal balance and stress recovery, morning sessions are ideal — they align with your natural cortisol rise and help reset disrupted endocrine rhythms. Use your torso protocol at sunrise or within the first 1-2 hours of waking. For menopause symptoms like hot flushes, early morning or early evening (2+ hours before bed) works best — avoid midday when body temperature naturally peaks. For energy and systemic inflammation recovery, daily morning sessions provide the most benefit. For bone health, any time works, but combining your session with exercise within 1-2 hours amplifies results. Consistency is more important than perfect timing — the best time is the one you'll maintain daily.

Why can't I see all the lights on my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and means they're working perfectly. The NIR lights are delivering powerful therapeutic wavelengths beneath the surface even though you can't see them. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with a large panel like your SUPREME. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
