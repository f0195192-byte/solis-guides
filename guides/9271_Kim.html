The science
How red light therapy helps

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate your dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for collagen and elastin production. During perimenopause, oestrogen decline accelerates collagen breakdown. Red light directly reverses this by activating fibroblasts to rebuild damaged collagen fibres. This reduces fine line depth, restores skin firmness, and improves texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural renewal cycle that slows with age and hormonal changes.

Acne & Breakouts

Red light penetrates to the sebaceous glands where acne begins, downregulating excess oil production whilst modulating inflammatory responses that drive breakouts. Unlike harsh treatments that damage skin, red light addresses inflammation from within, calming painful spots and reducing scarring risk. The 630nm wavelength is particularly effective for reducing the inflammatory cytokines that perimenopause-triggered hormonal acne creates, allowing your skin to regulate itself.

Eczema & Dermatitis

Your skin barrier is broken during eczema flares—it cannot retain moisture and inflammatory signals run unchecked. Red light therapy accelerates barrier repair whilst calming overactive nerve endings that perpetuate the itch-scratch cycle. It reduces infiltration of inflammatory T-cells whilst enhancing skin's natural immune regulation. During perimenopause, when barrier dysfunction worsens, this targeted support addresses root dysfunction rather than simply suppressing symptoms.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

Uneven pigmentation stems from sluggish cell turnover and poor blood flow. Red light stimulates angiogenesis—the formation of new blood vessels in your skin—dramatically boosting oxygen and nutrient delivery to every layer. With better circulation, your cells regenerate faster, clearing away old pigmented cells and replacing them with fresh ones. The result is even skin tone, reduced age spots, and genuinely radiant complexion without harsh treatments.

Skin Texture & Glow

Cellular energy production declines during perimenopause, slowing renewal and leaving skin looking dull. Red and NIR light boost ATP production in skin cells, giving them energy for rapid turnover and repair. Enhanced mitochondrial function means better cellular respiration, improved waste clearance, and luminous skin that reflects light more effectively. The visible glow you notice isn't just surface-level—it's cellular health improving from within.

Collagen & Elasticity

Collagen production declines by roughly 1% per year after 25, and perimenopause accelerates this collapse due to oestrogen's role in fibroblast activation. Red light directly activates fibroblasts with ATP energy, essentially supercharging their ability to synthesise new collagen and elastin. This isn't temporary plumping—it's structural rebuilding. Improved elasticity restores firmness and reduces sagging that often accompanies hormonal transition.

Chronic Pain Relief — Back, Spine, Neck & Hips

Chronic pain from bulging discs and long-standing inflammation means pro-inflammatory cytokines continuously activate pain receptors. Near-infrared wavelengths (810-850nm) penetrate deep into spinal tissues, joint capsules, and musculature where your pain originates. Red light therapy downregulates these pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing blood flow to deliver oxygen and remove inflammatory waste perpetuating pain cycles. Enhanced ATP production in damaged tissues supports repair mechanisms, addressing root dysfunction rather than masking symptoms. Studies show 50% pain reduction after consistent treatment.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Post-surgical numbness and neuropathy stem from damaged nerve axons and poor microcirculation starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients needed for repair. Near-infrared light stimulates regeneration of damaged nerve fibres whilst triggering nitric oxide release that dilates blood vessels feeding nerves. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and growth factors supporting nerve healing. Reduced inflammatory compounds that perpetuate pain signals means relief alongside actual tissue recovery, not just symptom suppression.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits for your needs, Kim. For skin goals (anti-ageing, acne, texture), morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm—a particular bonus during perimenopause when hormonal disruption affects circadian regulation. For pain relief, morning or early afternoon sessions give your tissue time to respond before activity and reduce night pain, supporting better sleep. For perimenopause symptom management, consistency matters most—the best time is the one you'll stick to daily. Many users find morning pain relief + evening skin sessions ideal for comprehensive support.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep to reach your spinal tissues, nerves, and joint structures. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spinal tissues), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a pain or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
