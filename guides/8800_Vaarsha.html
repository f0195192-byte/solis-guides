How red light therapy helps

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Management

Fibromyalgia involves both hypersensitive nerve endings misinterpreting normal sensations as pain, and profound mitochondrial dysfunction. Red and near-infrared light addresses both root causes. The wavelengths penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues and nerve cells, desensitising overactive nerve endings that amplify pain signals. Simultaneously, they enhance mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting ATP production that combat the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia. This recalibrates your pain threshold while restoring energy production, allowing your nervous system to function normally again rather than remaining stuck in hypersensitivity mode.

Lower Back Pain & Joint Inflammation

Back and joint pain stem from inflammatory compounds accumulating in affected tissues while protective structures lose mobility and strength. Red light penetrates deep to reach spinal tissues and joint capsules, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines while stimulating repair cells. Near-infrared wavelengths reach deep muscle layers and connective tissue, enhancing blood flow for nutrient and oxygen delivery. This combination interrupts the inflammatory cycle preventing healing, restores tissue repair capability, and significantly improves mobility—particularly for conditions where conventional treatments only mask symptoms rather than supporting actual tissue recovery.

Fat Loss & Muscle Recovery

Red light creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, releasing stored triglycerides into your bloodstream where they become available fuel for your body to burn, particularly when combined with exercise. When applied before workouts, it enhances ATP production in muscles, giving them energy to perform efficiently. Post-workout, it accelerates waste product removal and reduces muscle damage markers, shortening recovery windows and reducing soreness (DOMS). Enhanced mitochondrial function supports efficient fat mobilisation from stubborn areas while simultaneously building and preserving lean muscle, achieving better body composition without the extensive downtime that typically limits training frequency.

Scalp Health & Hair Restoration

Hair loss occurs when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of growth phase, combined with poor circulation starving scalp of nutrients. Red light wakes up stem cells in hair follicles, kickstarting them into growth mode and extending the active growth phase. The wavelengths trigger nitric oxide release in scalp cells, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle. For scalp inflammation (eczema/psoriasis), red light accelerates barrier repair and calms overactive nerve endings, reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker strands, and a healthier scalp environment.

Skin Rejuvenation & Pigmentation

Your Lichen Simplex Chronicus patch responds to the same collagen-stimulating mechanism that improves scarring and skin texture. Red light activates fibroblasts to build fresh collagen while reducing inflammatory cytokines that maintain the leathery appearance. For hyperpigmentation and uneven tone, improved microcirculation and accelerated cellular turnover help even out uneven pigmentation. The 630nm wavelength reduces inflammatory compounds contributing to redness and blotchiness. Simultaneously, enhanced cellular energy and circulation support your body's natural healing of stretch marks through collagen remodelling and skin regeneration.

Sleep Quality & Hormonal Balance

Modern life disrupts circadian rhythms through blue light exposure suppressing melatonin production. Red and near-infrared wavelengths work with your biology by stimulating your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally, signalling "sunset" to your brain and initiating natural wind-down. Enhanced cellular energy means sleep regulatory systems function efficiently for deeper restorative phases. For hormonal balance, red light modulates your autonomic nervous system and enhances mitochondrial function in hormone-producing glands, supporting smoother hormonal transitions and reduced PMS severity. The combined effect restores natural sleep-wake cycles and hormonal equilibrium without pharmaceutical intervention.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Timing depends on your specific goals, Vaarsha. For fibromyalgia pain management and energy boost, morning sessions (within 2 hours of waking) mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm—critical when chronic fatigue is a major issue. For muscle recovery and fat loss, pre-workout or immediate post-workout sessions are most effective. For sleep support and hormonal balance, use red light only in the evening, 1–2 hours before bed—this is crucial for circadian alignment. Never use evening red light and morning red light on the same day, as the morning energy-boosting effect can interfere with sleep quality. Instead, alternate: morning sessions on some days, evening sessions on others, or separate them by at least 10 hours.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR (near-infrared) LEDs that emit at 810nm, 830nm, and 850nm—these wavelengths are completely invisible to the human eye. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeper into muscle, joint tissue, and bone where your pain and inflammation originate. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active. For your fibromyalgia, joint inflammation, and scalp treatment, those invisible NIR wavelengths are doing much of the therapeutic work.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spine), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, scalp activation), a slightly greater distance can allow even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or pain protocol. Your fibromyalgia requires consistent, moderate dosing to recalibrate your nervous system without overstimulation; your back pain needs concentrated, deeper penetration; and your scalp needs close, direct contact to reach follicles through your thick hair.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For your scalp treatment with long, thick hair, parting the hair to expose the scalp directly is essential for the light to reach follicles and skin. For facial treatment, cleanse your face before sessions.

Your additional questions answered

Sauna Pairing: How to safely combine with your Solis SOLO

Timing: Use red light therapy BEFORE sauna sessions, not after. Here's why: Sauna heat dilates blood vessels and raises core body temperature. Red light therapy also enhances blood flow. Using both simultaneously can cause excessive vasodilation and overheating, potentially reducing the benefits of both therapies and causing discomfort, especially with fibromyalgia fatigue.

Optimal protocol: Do your red light therapy first (10–20 minutes depending on the goal), then wait 15–30 minutes before entering the sauna. This spacing allows your body to process the light stimulus while avoiding overlap of intense circulatory demands.

Frequency: On days you use your sauna pod, choose ONE therapy to prioritise. For example: Monday RLT + sauna, Wednesday RLT alone, Friday RLT + sauna. This prevents cumulative overstimulation while maintaining therapeutic benefit.

Hydration: Sauna increases fluid loss. If combining therapies, increase water intake before and after to support optimal recovery and prevent dehydration-related fatigue worsening your fibromyalgia symptoms.

Red Light Massager Pairing: Coordinating with your SOLO 2.0

Strategy: Use the handheld red light massager for targeted, intensive treatment of specific problem areas (painful joints, muscle knots, tight spots), and use the SOLO 2.0 for broader systemic or area-based treatment. Don't treat the same spot with both devices on the same day—this risks overdosing that specific tissue.

Sample rotation: Monday: SOLO 2.0 for fibromyalgia pain (chest/torso). Tuesday: Handheld massager for lower back or ankle inflammation. Wednesday: SOLO 2.0 for scalp. Thursday: Handheld for specific joint tightness or trigger point. Friday: SOLO 2.0 for fat loss and recovery. This rotation ensures comprehensive coverage without overtreatment.

Intensity consideration: Handheld massagers typically operate at close distance and higher intensity. Use for 5–10 minutes per area. The SOLO 2.0 at the recommended distances provides gentler, broader stimulation. Together, they offer both targeted and systemic benefit without overlap.

Long Hair Application: Treating Your Scalp Effectively

The challenge: Your extremely long, thick hair blocks light from reaching your scalp. Direct skin contact is essential.

Technique: Divide your scalp into 4–5 sections. Use claw clips or hair ties to secure most of your hair out of the way, exposing one section at a time. For example:

Section 1 (Crown/top parting): Part hair down the middle from forehead to nape, secure sides with clips. Treat the exposed middle stripe for 10 minutes.

Section 2 (Left side): Part hair to the right, exposing left side from temple to ear to back. Treat for 10 minutes.

Section 3 (Right side): Part hair to the left, exposing right side similarly.

Section 4 (Nape/back): Tie or clip most hair up high, treat the back scalp area.

Frequency: Do one section per session, 3–4 times per week. This means you're exposing all scalp areas 3–4 times per week total (each area covered once per session rotation), which is optimal for hair restoration without overwhelming your fibromyalgia fatigue.

Positioning: Hold the SOLO 2.0 10 cm from the scalp, angled slightly to ensure even coverage. The panel's size means you can treat a decent area in one session, so 10-minute rotations work well.

Practical tip: Do scalp treatments after showering when your hair is clean but still damp (not soaking)—the moisture helps position your hair out of the way more easily, and cleaner scalp maximises light penetration.

Morning: His breakfast time → You do your morning fibromyalgia protocol while he eats (he's occupied, you're nearby but focused). Storage: Store the panel in a closed cupboard or shelf he can't access. It's sturdy and non-toxic, but it's expensive equipment and a curious toddler could damage the cable or LEDs.

Maximum Daily Exposure & Workout Timing

Total daily limit: There is no strict "maximum" for red light therapy, but 60–90 minutes total per day across all sessions is a sensible guideline for optimal results without risk of diminishing returns. The biphasic dose-response principle means too much reduces effectiveness, not because of toxicity, but because your cells reach saturation and stop responding as efficiently.

Your situation (pre-workout + post-workout on same day): YES, you can safely do red light therapy during your workout and again post-workout on the same day, but here's how to structure it:

Pre-workout (10–15 minutes before exercise): Abdomen/legs protocol at 15 cm for 20 minutes to mobilise fat. This is distinct from your post-workout recovery window.

Post-workout (within 1 hour of exercise): Treat the same areas (abdomen/legs) for another 10 minutes at 15 cm, OR treat different areas (lower back, ankles) for 15 minutes at 10 cm for inflammation/pain relief. The key is not exceeding 60 minutes total on that body area in one day.

Total on workout day: 20 min pre + 10–15 min post = 30–35 minutes focused on fat loss and recovery. Add your morning fibromyalgia protocol (10 min) = ~45 minutes total. This is well within safe limits and won't cause overexposure.

Benefits of same-day pairing: Pre-workout red light prepares tissue, mobilises fat, and enhances mitochondrial energy for performance. Post-workout accelerates recovery, reduces soreness, and minimises inflammation—they're complementary, not redundant. Research shows this pairing actually enhances fat loss compared to either protocol alone.

Caution for fibromyalgia: Do NOT do your morning fibromyalgia protocol + evening sleep protocol + workout protocols on the same day. That's ~55 minutes of RLT plus intense exercise, which could trigger a fibromyalgia flare from overstimulation. Instead, on heavy workout days, skip the morning fibromyalgia protocol and use the evening sleep protocol separately. Rotate: workout days focus on fat loss/recovery; rest days include fibromyalgia and sleep protocols.
