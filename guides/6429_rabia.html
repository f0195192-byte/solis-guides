The science
How red light therapy helps

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of the growth phase, and when poor circulation means your scalp isn't getting the nutrients it needs. Red light therapy tackles both problems. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the scalp to stimulate stem cells in hair follicles, essentially kickstarting them back into active growth mode and extending the growth phase of your hair cycle. Simultaneously, it triggers the release of nitric oxide in scalp cells, which dilates blood vessels and dramatically increases nutrient delivery to each follicle. Enhanced ATP production in follicle cells gives them the energy needed to support healthier, thicker hair growth. The result? Clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker individual strands, and a healthier scalp environment—all without chemicals, side effects, or hormonal disruption.

Anti-Ageing & Fine Lines

Red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the dermis to stimulate fibroblasts—the cells responsible for producing collagen and elastin. This directly reduces the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, improves skin firmness, and restores a more youthful texture. Enhanced circulation from NIR exposure delivers more nutrients to skin cells, accelerating the natural repair and renewal cycle.

Hyperpigmentation & Dark Spots

By improving microcirculation and accelerating cellular turnover, red light therapy helps to even out uneven pigmentation and surface tone. The 630nm wavelength in particular has been shown to reduce inflammatory cytokines that contribute to blotchiness, resulting in a visibly more balanced, luminous complexion over time. Old, pigmented cells get cleared away quickly and replaced by fresh, clear cells.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy encourages orderly collagen deposition, the way healthy skin is actually structured, whilst preventing the excessive cells responsible for raised scars. For indented scars, boosted collagen production literally helps "lift" and level the skin's surface. For fresh wounds, red light accelerates repair cell migration and enhances healthy tissue formation, resulting in faster closure with less visible scarring.

Skin Texture & Glow

Red and NIR light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase in mitochondria, dramatically increasing ATP production in skin cells. This enhanced cellular energy accelerates skin renewal, improves nutrient absorption, and optimises every repair process. The result is visibly improved skin texture, a natural luminous glow, and skin that functions optimally at the cellular level.

COMBINING RED LIGHT THERAPY WITH MICRONEEDLING
The synergy: Microneedling creates controlled micro-injuries that trigger your skin's natural healing response, stimulating collagen production. Red light therapy amplifies this response by providing the cellular energy (ATP) your skin needs to repair efficiently and produce collagen faster. Together, they work on the same goal—deeper, more sustainable collagen remodelling—making this an exceptional combination.
Timing protocol: Apply red light therapy after microneedling, not before. Here's why: microneedling creates inflammation that needs immediate cellular support. Red light's ATP boost gives your skin the energy to repair the micro-channels efficiently, reducing healing time and amplifying collagen production. Wait 24 hours after microneedling, then use RLT daily for 5–7 days. This window is when your skin is most responsive to light therapy's regenerative effects.
For scalp microneedling: The same principle applies. Microneedle your scalp (using a device appropriate for that area), then use your SOLO 2.0 at 10 cm distance for 10 minutes daily for 5–7 days post-treatment. This accelerates follicle regeneration and maximises the healing response. Resume your regular 3–4× weekly RLT schedule after this intensive window.
Important caution: Do not apply red light directly to freshly microneedled skin on the same day. Wait 24 hours. Also, if your microneedling caused any bleeding or significant inflammation, wait until the skin has calmed (48 hours) before beginning RLT to avoid over-stimulating the healing response.
Expected results: This combination typically produces visible results faster than either treatment alone. For hair, expect noticeable reduction in shedding within 4–8 weeks and new growth within 12–16 weeks. For skin, combined microneedling + RLT often shows visible texture and radiance improvements within 2–4 weeks, with deeper scar and pigmentation improvements by 6–8 weeks.
Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For hair growth and skin goals, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm. Consistency matters most—the best time is the one you'll stick to. If you're doing scalp treatments, morning after shampooing works perfectly. For facial treatments, any time works, but many prefer mornings or evenings as part of their skincare routine.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (scalp follicles, deeper skin layers), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin tone, texture), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a scalp protocol looks different to a facial or scar protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For scalp treatment, hair should be clean and dry, with the scalp exposed (part your hair if needed to allow direct light contact).
