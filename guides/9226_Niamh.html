The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem: your skin can't hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create that relentless itch-scratch cycle you've been experiencing. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts responsible for rebuilding your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing itching and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens flare-ups. The 660nm wavelength in your SOLO 2.0 penetrates to the dermis where structural repair happens, whilst the 630nm works on surface inflammation and redness. Near-infrared wavelengths reach deep to reduce inflammatory cytokines driving the immune overreaction. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching—not masking symptoms with steroids, but supporting your skin's ability to function properly again.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means your facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, dilating excessively and creating persistent redness and that uncomfortable burning sensation. Your skin's inflammatory response is essentially stuck in overdrive. Red light therapy is uniquely suited for this because it's a "cold" therapy—no heat, no thermal damage, no aggravating already-sensitive skin. Instead, it works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. The result is reduced persistent redness, calmer blood vessel reactivity, and fewer trigger responses. Your skin's inflammatory threshold increases, meaning everyday stressors that used to cause flare-ups have less impact. Genuine improvement for notoriously difficult-to-treat rosacea, without harsh medications.

Scarring & Wound Healing

Scars form when your body rushes to close wounds, laying down collagen in a chaotic, disorganised pattern. Red light therapy changes this by encouraging orderly collagen deposition, the way healthy skin is actually structured, whilst preventing excessive cells responsible for raised scarring. For the weeping and damaged areas from your severe flare-ups, boosted collagen production and enhanced circulation literally help rebuild healthy tissue. For fresh wounds or persistent irritation, red light accelerates repair cell migration to the damaged site and enhances healthy tissue formation. Your body's natural repair mechanisms become significantly more efficient, with faster healing and reduced infection risk.

Muscle Recovery & Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres whilst metabolic waste accumulates—this causes delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24-48 hours post-workout and limits training frequency. Red light therapy applied before or after training reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells more energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds. The result is shorter recovery windows, reduced soreness, and better performance gains—your body rebuilding stronger without extended downtime.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your skin concerns, evening sessions are particularly beneficial—they help calm inflammation before bed, reduce itching overnight, and can improve sleep quality (eczema flare-ups often worsen with poor sleep). Many users find that an evening eczema session significantly reduces night-time scratching. For muscle recovery, use immediately post-workout whenever you train. For general consistency, the best time is the one you'll stick to—but evening works beautifully for your primary skin goals.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Mitochondrial stimulation supports steady energy production, particularly valuable as chronic eczema can be exhausting. Better cellular energy means better recovery overall and more resilience during stress periods when flare-ups typically worsen.
