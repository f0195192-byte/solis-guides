The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Atopic Dermatitis

Red and near-infrared light therapy tackles eczema on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair, helping rebuild your protective "shield" to retain moisture effectively and resist inflammatory triggers. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings that are misinterpreting signals as itching, directly reducing the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. The wavelengths reduce inflammatory cytokines driving the itch-scratch cycle whilst enhancing blood flow to deliver healing nutrients. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, reduced inflammation, and significantly improved sleep from less night-time itching.

Rosacea & Redness

Rosacea means your facial blood vessels are hyper-reactive, dilating excessively in response to triggers. Red light therapy is uniquely suited for this because it's a "cold" therapy — no heat, no thermal damage, no aggravating already-sensitive skin. It works at the cellular level by balancing reactive oxygen species and modulating the inflammatory mediators responsible for flaring. Enhanced circulation helps normalise blood vessel reactivity, whilst reduced inflammation lowers your skin's sensitivity threshold. The result is reduced persistent redness, calmer reactions to triggers, and genuine improvement for notoriously difficult-to-treat rosacea without harsh medications.

Anxiety, Stress & Low Mood

Depression and anxiety involve disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production. Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients whilst NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. Enhanced cellular energy helps your brain's stress-resilience mechanisms function more effectively, allowing you to process emotional challenges without becoming overwhelmed.

Energy, Brain Fog & Mental Clarity

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of your weight — neurons are incredibly energy-hungry. When mitochondrial function declines, cognitive performance suffers: memory weakens, focus deteriorates, brain fog worsens. Red and NIR light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This stimulation dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress. The result is improved executive function, better memory recall, enhanced focus and mental clarity, and protection against cognitive decline.

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

SAD involves disrupted neurotransmitter balance and circadian rhythm dysfunction during low-light seasons. Red light therapy works by enhancing mitochondrial function in neurons regulating mood whilst supporting natural melatonin production — red wavelengths signal "sunrise" to your brain, initiating proper circadian alignment. Unlike blue light which destroys melatonin, red and NIR wavelengths stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally. This supports both improved mood and better sleep quality, addressing SAD's dual burden of low mood and disrupted sleep.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your goals, timing is important. For anxiety, mood, energy, and SAD, morning sessions are ideal — red light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm, giving you sustained mood and energy throughout the day. For eczema and rosacea, timing is more flexible, though morning or early evening works well to avoid interfering with other skincare routines. For sleep quality, use your panel 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Going further
How else can it help you?

In addition to your primary goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver broader wellness benefits that many users notice over time:

Sleep quality
Red and NIR light can help regulate melatonin secretion and circadian rhythm, supporting deeper, more restorative sleep — especially when used in the morning or early evening to reinforce your natural sleep-wake cycle.
Wound healing
Red light accelerates tissue repair by stimulating collagen synthesis and reducing inflammation, helping minor cuts, scrapes, or scars heal faster and with less visible scarring — particularly beneficial if eczema causes skin breakdown.
Circulation
Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to tissues throughout the body, supporting recovery and overall cellular health, with visible benefits for skin radiance and healing.
Immune support
By improving mitochondrial function and reducing systemic inflammation, regular sessions may help support your body's natural immune responses over time — particularly relevant for autoimmune-related skin conditions like eczema.
Nervous system regulation
Enhanced mitochondrial function in nerve tissue supports parasympathetic activation, helping shift you out of stress mode into rest-and-digest state for better nervous system resilience and emotional regulation.
