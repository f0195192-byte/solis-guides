The science
How red light therapy helps

Sleep Quality & Circadian Regulation

Your sleep disruption stems from circadian dysregulation—your body's internal rhythm is misaligned, preventing continuous sleep architecture. Red light therapy directly stimulates your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in brain regions regulating sleep-wake cycles. Unlike blue light from screens which suppresses melatonin by up to 85%, red and near-infrared wavelengths signal "sunset" to your brain, promoting natural wind-down. Evening red light exposure aligns your circadian rhythm with your sleep schedule, improving sleep onset, reducing night-time awakenings, and deepening restorative sleep phases where true recovery occurs.

Chronic Fatigue & Energy Recovery

Persistent fatigue despite medication reflects mitochondrial dysfunction—your cellular powerplants aren't producing ATP efficiently. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep into cells to activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in mitochondrial energy production. This directly boosts ATP synthesis, the fuel powering every biological process from muscle movement to cognitive function. With enhanced mitochondrial efficiency, your cells have more energy for repair, recovery, and daily vitality. Combined with improved sleep from better circadian alignment, you experience cumulative improvement in sustained energy levels and reduced afternoon crashes.

Thyroid & Autoimmune Support

Your Graves disease involves immune cells attacking remaining thyroid tissue. Red light therapy modulates this overactive immune response by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-alpha and IL-6 whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in immune cells like T-lymphocytes and macrophages. Better-functioning immune cells distinguish threats more accurately, reducing attacks on your own tissue whilst maintaining normal defence. Additionally, the near-infrared wavelengths penetrate your neck to reach thyroid tissue directly, enhancing cellular ATP in remaining thyroid cells and supporting optimal hormone synthesis alongside your medication. This complementary approach addresses autoimmune dysfunction whilst optimising remaining gland function.

Post-Lyme Disease Recovery

Acute Lyme infection triggers systemic inflammation and mitochondrial stress as your immune system fights the pathogen. This inflammatory cascade persists even as antibiotic treatment clears bacterial infection, leaving tissue damage and cellular energy deficiency in its wake. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep tissues to reduce inflammatory markers and restore mitochondrial function throughout your body. Enhanced ATP production supports your immune system's resolution phase and accelerates tissue repair. By reducing neuroinflammation, it helps address brain fog characteristic of Lyme disease, whilst improved circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for complete recovery. This support is particularly valuable during and after high-dose antibiotic treatment, which itself can impair mitochondrial function.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SUPREME 2.0?

Timing optimises specific benefits for your situation. For sleep and circadian regulation (your primary goal), use the panel 1–2 hours before bed—this signals "sunset" to your brain and naturally promotes melatonin production, improving sleep onset and continuity. For energy support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and strengthen circadian alignment, boosting sustained energy throughout the day. For thyroid and autoimmune support, any time works, though morning or early afternoon is convenient. For post-Lyme recovery, daily sessions during your antibiotics course support your immune system's work; continue in evening if sleep support is your secondary priority. Consistency matters more than perfection—the best time is one you'll stick to reliably.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SUPREME 2.0 has 154 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface as the visible red ones. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active. All therapeutic benefit comes from these invisible wavelengths penetrating deep into tissue.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the SUPREME 2.0's large coverage area. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
