The science
How red light therapy helps

Fat Loss & Body Composition

Stubborn fat resists diet and exercise because fat cells tightly hold triglycerides. Red light therapy creates temporary pores in fat cell membranes, causing them to release stored fat into your bloodstream where it becomes available fuel. Combined with exercise, your body can access and burn this mobilised fat from problem areas like your belly. Clinical studies show 444% more fat loss in treated areas when combined with exercise, and consistent 2+ cm reductions in waist circumference after just 4–6 weeks. The mechanism is straightforward: light-induced membrane permeability plus movement equals fat mobilisation and loss from exactly where you want it.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss during menopause happens when follicles get stuck in the resting phase instead of growth phase, compounded by poor circulation meaning your scalp isn't getting nutrients it needs. Red light therapy tackles both. It wakes up stem cells in your hair follicles, kickstarting them back into active growth and extending the growth phase of your hair cycle. Simultaneously, it triggers nitric oxide release in scalp cells, dilating blood vessels and dramatically increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle. Clinical evidence shows 35–51% increases in hair density after 16–17 weeks. For menopause-related thinning specifically, the improved circulation and mitochondrial support address the underlying nutritional deficit driving hair loss.

Sleep Quality & Mood Support

Modern life and menopause disrupt your natural sleep-wake cycle through circadian dysfunction and mitochondrial decline in mood-regulating brain cells. Unlike blue light which suppresses melatonin by up to 85%, red and NIR wavelengths stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in mood and sleep regulatory centres. Evening red light signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down. For mood, enhanced cellular energy in neurons responsible for neurotransmitter production improves serotonin and dopamine synthesis—addressing the neurological root of menopause-related depression rather than masking symptoms. Studies show 79% decreases in time to fall asleep and 63% reductions in anxiety after just 1–2 weeks.

Thyroid & Metabolic Support

Your thyroid regulates metabolism, energy, and countless bodily functions. Thyroid dysfunction means insufficient hormone production and sluggish metabolism—creating fatigue and weight gain particularly during menopause. Red light reaches your thyroid directly due to its neck location. It enhances mitochondrial function in thyroid cells, improving hormone synthesis efficiency. Enhanced ATP production in metabolic tissues supports steady energy and more efficient fat burning. Clinical studies show 47% of patients with thyroid dysfunction were able to reduce or discontinue medications, with significant metabolic improvements. For menopause, the combination of improved thyroid function plus enhanced mitochondrial energy means your metabolism operates more efficiently despite hormonal changes, supporting fat loss goals fundamentally.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Timing enhances specific benefits for your goals. For fat loss, use the SOLO 2.0 within 1 hour before exercise — morning sessions work best if you exercise in the morning. For hair growth, consistency matters most — morning or evening, same time daily. For sleep and mood, use the evening session 1–2 hours before bed (around 8–9 pm ideally) to trigger natural melatonin and wind-down, then add a morning session for mood support to amplify serotonin production throughout the day. For thyroid support, morning or early afternoon works best — thyroid function naturally peaks in the morning and your sessions will align with this rhythm. The best time is the one you'll stick to consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
