The science
How red light therapy helps

Eczema & Atopic Dermatitis

Eczema is fundamentally a broken skin barrier problem: your skin cannot hold moisture, inflammatory signals run wild, and hypersensitive nerve endings create the relentless itch-scratch cycle. Red light therapy tackles this on multiple fronts. It accelerates skin barrier repair by stimulating fibroblasts responsible for rebuilding your protective lipid layer, helping restore your skin's ability to retain moisture effectively. Simultaneously, it calms overactive nerve endings, directly reducing the itching sensation and breaking the scratch reflex that worsens eczema. Clinical trials show fewer flare-ups, significantly reduced inflammation, and improved sleep from less night-time itching—not masking symptoms, but actually supporting your skin's ability to function properly again.

Sleep Quality & Circadian Regulation

Poor sleep stems from disrupted circadian rhythms, particularly blue light exposure from screens that suppresses melatonin production by up to 85%. Red and near-infrared wavelengths work differently—they stimulate your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally whilst enhancing mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating your sleep-wake cycle. Evening red light exposure signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down, whilst improved cellular energy means your sleep regulatory systems function more efficiently. The result is faster sleep onset, deeper restorative phases, and better morning alertness.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your eczema, morning or early afternoon sessions allow your skin time to respond before nighttime when itching often intensifies. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed—this mirrors sunset wavelengths and naturally supports melatonin production. Aim for consistency. Many users with your goals benefit from a morning eczema session plus an evening sleep-focused session. The best timing is what you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For surface-level skin concerns like your eczema, a slightly greater distance (20 cm) can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation, and importantly, keeps skin cool to avoid irritation. For sleep support targeting your brain, 30 cm is comfortable for extended sessions.

Time compensates for intensity. Your eczema sessions are shorter (10 min) because you're treating a focused area. Sleep sessions are longer (15 min) because you're using a greater distance and want sustained melatonin support before bed.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play—red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Your eczema-prone skin benefits from moderate, consistent doses rather than intense sessions that might trigger sensitivity.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense. If you find it uncomfortable during your face sessions, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help—both work equally well.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: Since your sessions are only 10–15 minutes, closing your eyes is usually sufficient and comfortable.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas (your arms, legs, torso, and face) are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For your eczema, this means treating immediately after a shower on bare skin.

Going further
How else can it help you?

In addition to your eczema and sleep goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver a range of broader wellness benefits particularly relevant to managing chronic skin conditions:

Immune support
By improving mitochondrial function and reducing systemic inflammation, red light therapy can support your body's natural immune responses. This is particularly valuable for atopic conditions where immune dysregulation drives eczema flare-ups.
Mood & anxiety
The itching and discomfort of chronic eczema often affects mood and stress levels. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in mood-regulating brain regions, and many users report improved emotional resilience and reduced anxiety—which itself reduces cortisol-driven flare-ups.
Circulation
Enhanced microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to affected skin tissue, supporting the barrier repair process and accelerating healing of compromised areas.
Wound healing
Red light accelerates tissue repair by stimulating collagen synthesis and reducing inflammation, helping any scratched or irritated areas heal faster with less visible scarring or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
