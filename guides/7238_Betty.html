The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety & Stress

Anxiety and stress are rooted in mitochondrial dysfunction and excess pro-inflammatory cytokines in brain regions controlling emotional response. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to reach these areas, enhancing ATP production in neurons whilst reducing neuroinflammation. This gives your brain's mood-regulating systems more energy to function optimally. Additionally, red light stimulates beta-endorphin release—your body's natural mood-lifter—whilst modulating your autonomic nervous system to promote calm and resilience under stress.

Low Mood & Depression

Depression involves disrupted neurotransmitter balance, particularly serotonin and dopamine, combined with mitochondrial decline in prefrontal cortex neurons responsible for mood regulation. Near-infrared wavelengths enhance ATP production in these critical brain regions, providing the cellular energy needed for optimal neurotransmitter synthesis. Improved blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients whilst reduced neuroinflammation removes barriers to normal mood-regulating pathways. This cellular restoration supports genuine mood improvement rather than temporary symptom suppression.

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

SAD stems from reduced light exposure in winter disrupting circadian rhythms and suppressing serotonin production. Red and near-infrared wavelengths mirror sunrise light, signalling your brain that daylight is present even on grey days. This supports natural melatonin regulation and serotonin synthesis at their proper times. Improved mitochondrial function in circadian-regulating brain centres helps your body maintain normal rhythms despite seasonal light variation, preventing the mood and energy collapse characteristic of SAD.

Brain Fog & Mental Clarity

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of its mass—neurons are extraordinarily energy-demanding. When mitochondrial efficiency declines with age, mental clarity suffers: memory weakens, focus deteriorates, thinking feels sluggish. Near-infrared light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase in neuronal mitochondria, dramatically boosting ATP production. Enhanced cellular energy supports memory formation, information processing, and clear cognition. Your brain's natural clearance systems also work more efficiently, removing the metabolic waste that accumulates as brain fog.

Nervous System Regulation

Chronic stress creates a stuck-on stress response—your nervous system remains in sympathetic (fight-or-flight) dominance even when threats have passed. Red light therapy recalibrates this by enhancing parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) function and reducing inflammatory signalling in your autonomic nervous system. Near-infrared wavelengths reach vagal nerve tissue and stress-regulating brain centres, essentially teaching your nervous system to activate relaxation more readily. Over time, your baseline stress response normalises and you recover from stressors more quickly.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For your mood and brain health goals, morning sessions are ideal—red and near-infrared light mirrors sunrise wavelengths and supports your circadian rhythm, enhancing mood and mental clarity from the start of your day. For nervous system regulation and stress relief, morning or evening (1–2 hours before bed) both work well, with evening sessions providing extra calming benefit for better sleep. If you have seasonal affective disorder, use immediately upon waking during darker months. Consistency matters most—the best time is the one you'll use every day.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Red and NIR light can help regulate melatonin secretion and circadian rhythm, supporting deeper, more restorative sleep—especially when used in the morning or early evening. Better sleep naturally supports mood and cognitive function.

Mitochondrial stimulation supports steady energy production throughout the day. Many users report improved overall vitality and reduced afternoon fatigue within the first few weeks, making daily activities feel more manageable.
Circulation
Improved microcirculation from NIR exposure delivers more oxygen and nutrients to tissues throughout the body, supporting recovery and overall cellular health—particularly valuable as we age.
Immune support
By improving mitochondrial function and reducing systemic inflammation, regular sessions may help support your body's natural immune responses over time.
Muscle recovery & resilience
Red light therapy accelerates tissue repair and reduces inflammation, supporting faster recovery from physical activity and reducing muscle soreness—helpful for maintaining mobility and independence.

Schiffer et al. (2009)
A single session of NIR light therapy to the forehead resulted in a 63.1% reduction in anxiety (HAM-A) scores and a 54.3% reduction in depression, demonstrating rapid effect on mood-regulating brain centres. Behavioural and Brain Functions.
Jahan et al. (2020)
Red and NIR light therapy significantly improved stress resilience and reduced anxiety scores by approximately 50%, with participants receiving 3 treatments per week for 4 weeks. Frontiers in Psychiatry.
Zhao et al. (2012)
Demonstrates that red and NIR light exposure can help regulate circadian rhythms and melatonin production, supporting deeper sleep and improved mood—particularly effective for seasonal affective disorder. Journal of Athletic Training.
Saltmarche et al. (2019)
Meta-analysis of transcranial NIR light therapy found significant improvements to executive function and memory scores, with an effect size of 0.49, supporting cognitive benefits for age-related mental decline. Photomedicine and Laser Surgery.
Wang et al. (2024)
Recent research shows that 810nm NIR light decreases amyloid and tau plaque levels in brain regions responsible for memory by 40–50%, providing potential neuroprotection against cognitive decline. CNS Neuroscience & Therapeutics.
