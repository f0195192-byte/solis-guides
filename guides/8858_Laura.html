The science
How red light therapy helps

Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis creates chronic pelvic pain through tissue inflammation and ectopic tissue growth. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate pelvic tissue to reach the inflammatory zone directly. Red light reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-alpha that drive pain whilst improving blood flow to damaged tissue. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients supporting healing of inflammatory lesions. The result is measurably reduced pelvic pain intensity without pharmaceutical side effects.

Fertility & Egg Quality

Egg quality is fundamentally a mitochondrial issue — each oocyte requires enormous ATP for maturation, fertilisation, and early embryo development. As women age, mitochondrial function in eggs declines, deteriorating egg quality and increasing miscarriage risk. Near-infrared light penetrates ovarian tissue to enhance mitochondrial function directly in developing eggs. Boosted ATP gives oocytes energy for demanding maturation processes whilst improved mitochondrial health means better chromosome organisation, more efficient DNA repair, and higher-quality embryos. Enhanced blood flow to reproductive tissue supports optimal hormone production and uterine lining development.

Period Pain & Cycle Regulation

Period pain and irregular cycles reflect uterine inflammation and hormonal dysregulation. Red light therapy reduces inflammatory signals in reproductive tissue whilst stimulating improved blood flow to the uterus. Enhanced circulation means better hormone delivery and more efficient oxygen supply to contracting uterine muscle. Near-infrared wavelengths reach deep pelvic tissue, modulating the immune response that contributes to dysmenorrhea. By reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines and improving tissue oxygenation, red light naturally regulates cycle regularity and reduces pain severity.

Pain Relief — Back, Spine & Neck

Chronic back, spine, and neck pain stems from tissue inflammation, poor circulation, and damaged muscle or connective tissue. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach spinal tissues, intervertebral discs, and deep muscle layers where chronic pain originates. Red light downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates pain cycles. Improved mitochondrial function in damaged cells gives them energy for healing, reducing pain intensity and improving mobility without medication.

Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain

Fibromyalgia means central nervous system hypersensitivity amplifies pain signals, turning normal sensations into widespread chronic pain. Combined with mitochondrial dysfunction causing severe fatigue, the result is exhaustion and aching with no clear injury to treat. Red light therapy addresses both. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate neurological tissue, desensitising overactive nerve endings and recalibrating your pain threshold. Simultaneously, red light enhances mitochondrial function throughout your entire body, boosting cellular ATP production that combats the crushing fatigue characteristic of fibromyalgia. Enhanced energy means your nervous system can regulate more efficiently.

Headaches & Migraines

Migraines involve vascular dysregulation (abnormal blood vessel dilation and constriction), neuroinflammation in brain tissue, and mitochondrial energy decline in neurons. Red light stimulates endothelial cells in cerebral blood vessels to produce nitric oxide, a potent vasodilator that stabilises blood flow. Near-infrared wavelengths reach deep brain tissue, reducing neuroinflammation that contributes to migraine pain. Enhanced mitochondrial function in neurons means better energy for pain regulation and improved neural communication. The result is reduced migraine frequency and severity.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits. For pelvic pain and period support, morning sessions help reduce inflammation before it builds throughout the day. For fertility and egg quality, any consistent time works — what matters is regularity. For headaches and migraine prevention, morning use leverages the circadian alignment with natural light exposure. For pain relief and fibromyalgia, morning sessions support energy production that sustains you through the day, with evening sessions reducing pain and supporting sleep. For sleep support, use 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Aim for consistency — the best time is the one you'll stick to.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
