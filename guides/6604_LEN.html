The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Injury Rehabilitation

Red and near-infrared light penetrate deeply into muscle and connective tissue to enhance mitochondrial function, providing cells with more ATP energy for efficient repair. This accelerates the body's natural healing response following injury. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients to damaged tissue while removing inflammatory waste products that slow recovery. For meniscus and joint injuries specifically, improved cellular energy supports collagen synthesis and tissue remodelling, helping your knee rebuild structural integrity. Studies show significantly faster recovery timelines when light therapy is combined with rehabilitation protocols.

Pain Relief & Joint Function

Red light therapy works by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines—chemical signals that drive persistent pain and joint inflammation. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate to deep joint structures, reducing synovial inflammation and improving cartilage cell function. Enhanced blood flow to the joint capsule delivers nutrients for repair while removing inflammatory compounds perpetuating your pain cycle. The 810nm and 830nm wavelengths in your SOLO 2.0 are particularly effective for deep joint penetration. Clinical research shows 50-70% pain reduction in meniscus and arthritis cases with consistent use, allowing you to rebuild function without relying solely on pain medication.

Muscle Tension & Tightness

Compensatory muscle tension develops when protecting an injured joint—surrounding muscles tighten to stabilise the area, creating their own pain cycle. Red light reduces tension by enhancing ATP production in muscle tissue, allowing tight muscles to relax more effectively while improving circulation that releases metabolic waste causing stiffness. The improved blood flow also delivers nutrients that support normal muscle function, helping patterns gradually normalise as your knee heals. This breaks the protective tension cycle whilst your joint rebuilds.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

For injury recovery and pain relief, morning or early afternoon sessions are ideal because they give your tissues time to respond and rebuild before rest. For sleep support, use 1–2 hours before bed when the melatonin-supporting effect is most powerful. Since your primary goal is knee recovery, consistency matters more than timing — the best time is the one you'll stick to daily. Many users find morning sessions most convenient so they complete treatment before daily activities.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your knee joint and tissues. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active. These invisible wavelengths are what makes deep tissue penetration possible for your meniscus recovery.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
