The science
How red light therapy helps

Mood, Anxiety & Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

SAD and chronic low mood stem from disrupted neurotransmitter balance, neuroinflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in brain regions controlling mood. Red and near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons and glial cells, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production including serotonin and dopamine. Increased cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients while NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation disrupting mood pathways. For SAD specifically, red light helps regulate circadian rhythms and supports melatonin and serotonin synthesis when daylight is limited. The result is measurably improved mood, reduced anxiety, and better stress resilience—addressing the root cellular dysfunction rather than masking symptoms.

Hair Growth & Thickness

Hair loss and thinning occur when follicles get stuck in the resting phase and don't receive adequate blood flow and nutrients needed for growth. Red light therapy wakes up stem cells in hair follicles, kickstarting them back into the active growth phase and extending how long they stay there. Near-infrared wavelengths trigger nitric oxide release in scalp cells, dramatically dilating blood vessels and increasing nutrient delivery to each follicle. This enhanced circulation delivers more oxygen, minerals, and growth factors while removing inflammatory waste. Enhanced mitochondrial function in follicle cells also gives them more energy for hair protein synthesis and structural development. The result is clinically proven increases in hair density, thicker individual strands, and improved follicle health—particularly significant for chemotherapy-induced or stress-related hair loss where your follicles simply need cellular energy and circulation support to recover.

Pain Relief & Joint Function

Chronic pain from osteoarthritis stems from inflammatory compounds accumulating in joint fluid while cartilage protection breaks down. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach joint capsules, reducing inflammatory cytokines while stimulating chondrocytes—the cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced ATP production gives these cells energy to rebuild damaged cartilage tissue, while improved blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients supporting this repair process. Simultaneously, red light modulates pain signalling in nerve endings, reducing the perception of pain whilst your tissues actually heal. For muscle cramps and neck pain, enhanced mitochondrial function in muscle tissue combined with reduced inflammation addresses the root cause rather than just masking symptoms with medication. The result is measurable pain reduction, improved mobility, and genuine tissue repair—particularly valuable when surgery isn't an option.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

For your specific goals, timing is particularly important. For mood and SAD, morning sessions are optimal—red light mirrors natural sunrise wavelengths and helps regulate your circadian rhythm and serotonin production throughout the day. Use your SURGE first thing in the morning, ideally within an hour of waking, to anchor your natural wake cycle. For hair growth, any time works, though many users combine this with their morning mood session for efficiency. For pain relief, evening sessions are ideal—treating your knee and neck in the late afternoon or early evening allows your tissues time to respond before bed, often leading to better sleep. The goal is consistency: the best time is the one you'll stick to daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into your tissues. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
