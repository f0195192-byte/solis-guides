The science
How red light therapy helps

Sleep Quality & Circadian Regulation

Red and near-infrared light work with your biology by stimulating your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally. Unlike blue light from screens that suppresses melatonin by up to 85%, red light actually signals "sunset" to your brain, initiating natural wind-down. When used in the evening (1-2 hours before bed), red light resets your circadian rhythm by aligning your body's internal clock with natural light patterns. For shift workers and those with jet lag, consistent red light exposure helps reset dysregulated rhythms, restoring normal sleep-wake cycles. Enhanced mitochondrial function in brain cells regulating sleep also means your nervous system can transition to restful states more efficiently.

Mood, Anxiety, Depression & Stress

Red and NIR light enhance mitochondrial function in neurons responsible for mood regulation, providing more ATP for neurotransmitter production including serotonin and dopamine. Improved cerebral blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients to mood centres whilst NIR wavelengths reduce neuroinflammation that disrupts emotional regulation. For anxiety, light therapy to the forehead significantly reduces hyperarousal of the nervous system, lowering cortisol responses to stress. Sleep improvement from better circadian alignment further stabilises mood and stress resilience — sleep deprivation drives anxiety and depression, so fixing sleep resolves much of the mood dysfunction.

Brain Fog, Mental Clarity & Cognitive Function

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of your weight. When sleep is poor and mitochondria are exhausted, cognitive performance suffers: memory weakens, focus deteriorates, and processing slows. Near-infrared light penetrates your skull to enhance mitochondrial function in neurons and glial cells, boosting ATP production that powers memory formation, information processing, and mental clarity. With better sleep and improved cellular energy, executive function and attention improve significantly — particularly important for ADHD and autism-related attention challenges where nervous system regulation is already demanding.

Energy, Motivation & Nervous System Regulation

Chronic sleep deprivation creates mitochondrial dysfunction — your cells' power plants produce insufficient ATP, leaving you exhausted no matter how much you rest. Red and NIR light directly activates cytochrome c oxidase in your mitochondria, dramatically increasing ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages these cellular powerhouses. For nervous system regulation, red light modulates your autonomic nervous system by supporting parasympathetic activation (the "rest and digest" branch), making it easier to shift from alert to relaxed states. With improved sleep, better energy production, and a more balanced nervous system, motivation and stamina naturally increase.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Timing is critical for your goals, Ricky. For sleep, use the SOLO 2.0 in the evening 1-2 hours before bed — this signals "sunset" to your brain and naturally boosts melatonin production. For mood, anxiety, and energy, morning sessions (within 30 minutes of waking) mirror natural sunrise and anchor your circadian rhythm powerfully. If you're managing shift work or jet lag, consistency matters most — use the panel at the same time daily to reset your rhythm. The best schedule is: morning session for mood and energy, evening session for sleep. This combination directly addresses all your goals by resetting your entire circadian system.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 NIR LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.

Going further
How else can it help you?

In addition to your primary goals, regular use of your SOLO 2.0 may deliver a range of broader wellness benefits that many users notice over time:

Muscle recovery
Enhanced ATP production supports muscle repair and reduces soreness, particularly valuable if you're training or managing physical fatigue from poor sleep recovery. Post-exercise red light sessions accelerate tissue repair and adaptation.
Nervous system regulation
Beyond sleep and mood, red light modulates your autonomic nervous system by supporting parasympathetic activation. This helps if you experience hyperarousal, restlessness, or difficulty shifting from alert to calm states — common with ADHD and autism-related nervous system dysregulation.
Inflammation reduction
Chronic sleep deprivation elevates systemic inflammation. By restoring sleep quality and enhancing mitochondrial function, red light reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines, supporting recovery from the physiological stress of sleep disruption.
Hormone regulation
Sleep and circadian rhythm restoration naturally rebalance hormones like cortisol, melatonin, and testosterone. These hormonal improvements compound over weeks, further improving mood stability, energy, and overall wellbeing.
Cognitive resilience
As sleep improves and brain mitochondrial function enhances, your capacity to handle stress, concentrate on demanding tasks, and regulate attention improves. This is especially valuable if ADHD or autism-related attention challenges are compounded by sleep deprivation.
