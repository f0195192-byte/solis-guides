The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief — Back & Spine Pain

Back pain results from spinal inflammation, compromised nerve function, and poor blood flow to damaged tissue. Red and near-infrared light penetrates deep to inflamed spinal tissues, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst enhancing blood circulation. This delivers oxygen and nutrients to support tissue repair, particularly critical after surgery. Enhanced ATP production in spinal tissues gives your cells energy to heal and regenerate, addressing the inflammatory cascade driving chronic pain.

Pain Relief — Knee & Joint Pain

Joint pain stems from cartilage breakdown, synovial fluid inflammation, and reduced blood flow to the joint capsule. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate the joint structure, stimulating chondrocytes (cartilage-producing cells) whilst reducing inflammatory mediators. Enhanced circulation delivers nutrients whilst removing inflammatory waste, supporting cartilage repair and improving joint mobility.

Pain Relief — Shoulder & Neck Pain

Shoulder and neck pain typically involves muscular tension, inflammation, and compromised nerve signalling. Red light therapy reduces localised inflammation whilst improving blood flow to tight muscles, allowing them to relax and recover. Enhanced mitochondrial function gives muscle tissue energy to repair micro-damage, whilst improved circulation alleviates tension-related pain.

Arthritis

Arthritis involves cartilage degeneration and inflammatory compound accumulation in synovial fluid. Red light reduces systemic inflammatory markers whilst stimulating chondrocyte activity for cartilage repair. The anti-inflammatory effect addresses the root dysfunction rather than masking pain, whilst improved circulation supports the joint's natural healing capacity.

Nerve Pain & Neuropathy

Nerve pain occurs when damaged nerve axons send faulty signals, compounded by poor microcirculation starving nerves of oxygen and nutrients. Near-infrared light stimulates nerve regeneration and triggers nitric oxide release to dilate capillaries feeding nerve tissue. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and removes inflammatory compounds, reducing burning and tingling sensations whilst supporting nerve repair.

Sleep — Difficulty Falling Asleep & Waking Through the Night

Poor sleep results from circadian dysregulation, pain-induced wakefulness, and reduced melatonin production. Red light therapy stimulates your pineal gland to produce melatonin naturally, signalling "sleep time" to your brain. By addressing underlying pain through enhanced tissue repair and inflammation reduction, red light removes the pain-driven wakefulness preventing restorative sleep. Evening red light exposure supports natural circadian regulation without the side effects of medication.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis STUDIO 2.0?

For your pain and sleep goals, timing is strategic. Morning or midday sessions target your pain — allow your tissue time to respond before daily activity or before bed. For sleep support, use the panel 1–2 hours before bed to naturally stimulate melatonin production. For spinal and joint pain, many users find that morning sessions help mobilise their spine and reduce stiffness before activity, whilst evening sessions support tissue repair during sleep. Consistency matters more than timing — the best time is the one you'll use every day. If pain is your priority, daily morning sessions combined with an evening wind-down session before bed will give you both pain relief and improved sleep.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your STUDIO 2.0 has 480 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deeper into tissues than the visible red light. The invisible wavelengths are particularly important for deep tissue pain relief, nerve regeneration, and systemic effects. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming the NIR lights are active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, spine, nerves), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially with the STUDIO 2.0. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
