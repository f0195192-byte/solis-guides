The science
How red light therapy helps

Muscle Recovery & Post-Workout Soreness

Intense exercise creates micro-tears in muscle fibres and causes metabolic waste like lactic acid to accumulate, triggering delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) that peaks 24–48 hours post-workout. Red light therapy applied after training reduces muscle damage markers whilst accelerating waste product removal. Enhanced ATP production gives muscle cells the energy to repair micro-tears efficiently, whilst improved circulation flushes inflammatory compounds that perpetuate soreness. The result? Shorter recovery windows, reduced DOMS intensity, and the ability to train more frequently with better results.

Athletic Performance & Endurance

Athletic performance depends on mitochondrial efficiency—your cells' ability to produce ATP for muscle contraction, energy sustenance, and recovery. Red and near-infrared light directly activate cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in the mitochondrial energy production pathway, dramatically increasing ATP output. Enhanced cellular energy means muscles have more fuel for power generation, strength gains, and sustained effort during endurance activities. Better mitochondrial function also reduces oxidative stress that damages performance capacity. The result? Improved strength, better endurance, faster fatigue recovery, and enhanced athletic output from the same training stimulus.

Injury Recovery & Rehabilitation

Tissue injuries trigger inflammatory cascades and reduced blood flow that impair healing. Red light penetrates deep into injured tissue, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst simultaneously enhancing blood flow to deliver oxygen and nutrients necessary for repair. Enhanced ATP production in damaged cells gives them energy to rebuild tissue structure efficiently. NIR wavelengths reach deep joint, muscle, and tendon damage that surface-level treatments cannot address. The result? Accelerated tissue repair, reduced pain during rehabilitation, and faster return to full function—particularly significant for injuries that typically limit training and quality of life.

Hormonal Health

Hormonal balance at 48 depends on optimal mitochondrial function in hormone-producing glands and neurotransmitter synthesis in the brain. Red light enhances mitochondrial function throughout your endocrine system, supporting efficient hormone production. Enhanced cellular energy in the hypothalamus and pituitary glands—your master hormone regulators—helps maintain steady hormone levels. Reduced systemic inflammation (a key driver of hormonal dysregulation) means your hormonal feedback loops function optimally. The result? Better energy regulation, improved recovery capacity, stable mood, and overall hormonal resilience during an important life transition.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SURGE 2.0?

Your protocol uses morning sessions as a circadian reset (replacing natural sunrise), post-workout sessions for muscle recovery, and optional pre-workout sessions for performance. For your hormonal health and energy goals, morning is ideal—the SURGE 2.0 mimics sunrise wavelengths and aligns your body's natural rhythm. Post-workout recovery sessions should occur within 1 hour of finishing training for maximum effectiveness. Consistency and timing matter more than the exact clock time, so establish a routine you can maintain daily.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SURGE 2.0 has 90 near-infrared LEDs (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) that emit light completely invisible to the human eye—this is entirely normal and expected. The NIR wavelengths are working powerfully beneath the surface, penetrating deep into muscle tissue, joints, and organs where therapeutic effects occur. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming the NIR lights are active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue—and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription—the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required—red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially at close distances. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes—for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption. For your morning protocol, this means minimal clothing on your torso and face. For post-workout recovery, your sweaty skin is ideal—no additional prep needed beyond exposing the muscle groups you trained.
