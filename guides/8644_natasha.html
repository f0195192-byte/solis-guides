The science
How red light therapy helps

Pain Relief & Inflammation Reduction

Pain is your body's inflammatory response to tissue damage. Pro-inflammatory cytokines flood affected areas, creating swelling, restricting blood flow, and sensitising nerve endings. Red light therapy interrupts this at source by downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines whilst increasing blood flow to damaged tissues. Near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deep to reach joint spaces where chronic pain originates. Enhanced blood flow delivers oxygen and nutrients for repair whilst removing inflammatory waste that perpetuates the pain cycle. Your body's natural pain-relieving mechanisms activate more efficiently without medication.

Arthritis & Joint Function

Arthritis means protective cartilage breaks down whilst inflammatory compounds accumulate in synovial fluid — your joints lose shock absorption and become inflamed, causing characteristic stiffness and pain, especially mornings. Red light penetrates to the joint capsule, reducing inflammation whilst stimulating chondrocytes, cells responsible for cartilage repair. Enhanced blood flow delivers crucial nutrients and oxygen, supporting this repair process and breaking the inflammatory cycle preventing healing. The result is reduced morning stiffness, improved mobility, and decreased pain during movement—particularly significant for osteoarthritis where conventional treatments only mask symptoms rather than supporting actual joint tissue repair.

Thyroid & Hashimoto's Support

Hashimoto's thyroiditis means your immune system attacks thyroid tissue, progressively destroying hormone production. Red light reaches your thyroid directly due to its neck location. It enhances mitochondrial function in thyroid cells, improving hormone synthesis whilst reducing autoimmune inflammatory response. Red light also modulates immune activity, reducing anti-thyroid antibodies whilst enhanced cellular energy means more efficient function. Studies show 47% of patients were able to discontinue thyroid medication, and a 600-point drop (~50%) in thyroid antibodies (anti-TPO) after consistent treatment.

Energy Levels & Mitochondrial Support

Chronic fatigue isn't laziness or poor sleep alone — it's often mitochondrial dysfunction. Your mitochondria are cellular powerplants that produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency fuelling every biological process. When mitochondrial efficiency declines due to age, autoimmune disease, or chronic inflammation, ATP production drops and you feel exhausted regardless of rest. Red light therapy directly activates cytochrome c oxidase, a key enzyme in your mitochondria's energy production pathway. This stimulation dramatically increases ATP output whilst reducing oxidative stress that damages mitochondrial function. Essentially, you're optimising your cellular powerplants to produce more energy more efficiently.

Immunity Support

Your immune system's effectiveness depends on white blood cells having sufficient energy to patrol, identify threats, and mount responses. When mitochondrial function in immune cells declines, your immune response becomes sluggish — pathogens evade detection longer and infections persist despite good hygiene. Red light enhances mitochondrial function in immune cells like macrophages and lymphocytes, giving them more ATP to perform defence roles effectively. Better-energised cells detect pathogens faster, mount stronger responses, and resolve infections more efficiently. Clinical studies show an average 82.5% reduction of "bad" inflammatory cytokines in just 3 days, and significantly enhanced T-lymphocyte proliferation.

Practical questions
Getting started

What time of day should I use my Solis SOLO 2.0?

Red light therapy can be used at any time of day, but timing enhances specific benefits for your needs. For pain relief and joint function, morning or afternoon sessions allow your joints time to benefit before movement. For energy and mitochondrial support, morning sessions mirror natural sunrise wavelengths and support your circadian rhythm, combating the fatigue associated with Hashimoto's. For thyroid support, consistent timing (morning or evening) builds routine. For immunity support, any time works, but consistency matters more than timing. Aim for the time you'll stick to — the best protocol is the one you'll follow consistently.

Why can't I see all the lights?

Your SOLO 2.0 has 30 LEDs that emit near-infrared light (810nm, 830nm, 850nm) which is completely invisible to the human eye — this is entirely normal. The NIR lights are working just as powerfully beneath the surface. Point a smartphone camera at the panel to see a faint purple glow confirming they're active.

Why are the distances and session times different for each benefit?

Red light therapy works by delivering a specific dose of light energy to your tissue — and like any dose, getting it right depends on the target.

Distance affects intensity. The further you are from the device, the more the light spreads and weakens. For deeper targets (joints, muscles, gut), you generally want to be closer to maximise penetration. For surface-level concerns (skin, wound healing), a slightly greater distance can actually be more effective as it allows even coverage without oversaturation.

Time compensates for intensity. If you're further away and receiving less power per square centimetre, you need longer to accumulate the same therapeutic dose. Closer sessions can often be shorter for the same effect.

There's also a "Goldilocks" principle at play — red light therapy follows a biphasic dose response, meaning too little has no effect, the right amount works, and too much can actually reduce results. Different tissues and biological processes have different optimal dose windows, which is why a skin protocol looks different to a joint or recovery protocol.

Think of it less like "more is more" and more like a prescription — the goal is to hit the right energy target for that specific benefit, and distance and time are just the two dials you use to get there.

Should I use goggles with red light therapy?

Goggles are not required — red and near-infrared (NIR) light are not harmful to the eyes when used appropriately. However, there are a few factors to consider:

Brightness: The light can be quite intense, especially during facial treatments. If you find it uncomfortable, wearing protective goggles or closing your eyes can help.

Prolonged exposure: While occasional exposure is safe, avoid staring directly into the light diodes for extended periods.

Personal preference: If you're sensitive to light, using goggles can enhance comfort during your sessions.

Do I need to expose bare skin during treatment?

Yes — for best results, red light therapy should be used on clean, bare skin. Clothing can block light penetration, so ensure the targeted areas are free from lotions, makeup, or sunscreen to allow maximum absorption.
